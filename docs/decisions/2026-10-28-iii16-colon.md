- **III.16 reads a colon before "which is impossible", as a second copy of the 1908 printing
  prints it (Heath's text, [#551](https://github.com/dodecaplex-co/stoicheia/issues/551)).**
  This is the coordinator's ruling of 28 October 2026, given in the orders that carry it
  out; it claims no decision of the owner's.

  The reading of `heath/` against the scans ([Heath's text against the
  scans](../reports/heath-against-scans.md), note 4) found that on leaf n50 of
  `thirteenbookseu00heibgoog` (p. 38) the stop in "equal to two right angles; which is
  impossible." could be a semicolon with a broken tail or a colon with a speck below it.
  The file read a semicolon. A second copy of the 1908 printing, the Internet Archive's
  `euclid-the-thirteen-books-of-the-elements-vol.-2`, which carries a library's stamp that
  the copy scanned by Google lacks, prints a plain colon at its leaf n45 (p. 38). The
  coordinator's ruling reads: "Heath's text is what we print, so it takes the colon."

  What this train does. `heath/III/16.md`, line 21, now reads "equal to two right angles:
  which is impossible.", and its comment names both leaves. The `--` line at
  `Elements/BookIII/Prop16.lean:90` changes its semicolon to a colon, and no other word.
  III.16's page is regenerated, and its review record is read in full and stamped again
  by a reader of the other family from its proof's writer. No statement changes, nor any
  porism, definition, axiom or lock. I.11's "and CF is common" (note 1) stays as it is.
