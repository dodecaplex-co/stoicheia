- For the sine rule in our modern way, and where the sine itself came from, Glen Van Brummelen's [*The Mathematics of the Heavens and the Earth*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691129730/the-mathematics-of-the-heavens-and-the-earth) (2009) tells the early history of trigonometry, from the chord tables of Greek astronomers onwards.
- For what Euclid can prove before he needs Postulate 5, as he proves this, Marvin Jay Greenberg's [*Euclidean and Non-Euclidean Geometries*](https://store.macmillanlearning.com/us/product/Euclidean-and-Non-Euclidean-Geometries/p/0716799480) (2008), ch. 4, "Neutral Geometry", is a warm and careful guide.
