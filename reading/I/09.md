- The modern way's bisector has a good companion in Liang-shin Hahn's [*Complex Numbers and Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1090/text/052) (2019; first published 1994), a book that assumes nothing about complex numbers and then puts them to work in quick, clean proofs of classical theorems.
- No ruler and compass can trisect every angle, and Felix Klein's [*Famous Problems of Elementary Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/famousproblemsof00kleiiala) (1897, free online), in Wooster Woodruff Beman and David Eugene Smith's translation, proves it in about eighty pages, which also show why the cube cannot be doubled or the circle squared.
- The conchoid, the quadratrix and the spiral have their chapter, "Curves", in David S. Richeson's [*Tales of Impossibility*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691194233) (2019), a tour full of history and short tangents, and Pierre Wantzel's proof of 1837 has another, "Pierre Wantzel".
