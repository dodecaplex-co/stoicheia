- Liang-shin Hahn's [*Complex Numbers and Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1090/text/052) (1994), a short, gentle book that assumes nothing about complex numbers and then gives quick, clean proofs of classical theorems with them, is a good companion to the modern way's bisector.
- Why can no ruler and compass trisect every angle? Felix Klein's [*Famous Problems of Elementary Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/famousproblemsof00kleiiala) (1897, free online), in Wooster Woodruff Beman and David Eugene Smith's translation, answers in about eighty friendly pages, which also explain why the cube cannot be doubled or the circle squared.
- The conchoid, the quadratrix and the spiral have their chapter, "Curves", in David S. Richeson's [*Tales of Impossibility*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691194233) (2019), a tour full of history and short tangents, and Pierre Wantzel's proof of 1837 has another, "Pierre Wantzel".
