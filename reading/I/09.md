- For angles bisected and turned with complex numbers, as in the modern way, Liang-shin Hahn's [*Complex Numbers and Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1090/text/052) (1994), a short, gentle book that assumes nothing about complex numbers and then gives quick, clean proofs of classical theorems with them.
- For why no ruler and compass can trisect every angle, Felix Klein's [*Famous Problems of Elementary Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/famousproblemsof00kleiiala) (1897), in Wooster Woodruff Beman and David Eugene Smith's translation, about eighty friendly pages that also explain why the cube cannot be doubled or the circle squared (free online).
- For the conchoid, the quadratrix and the spiral, and Pierre Wantzel's proof of 1837, David S. Richeson's [*Tales of Impossibility*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691194233) (2019), chapters "Curves" and "Pierre Wantzel", a tour full of history and short tangents.
