- Glen Van Brummelen's [*The Mathematics of the Heavens and the Earth*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691129730/the-mathematics-of-the-heavens-and-the-earth) (2009), a full history of early trigonometry, traces the cosine back to Ptolemy's table, which gave, for each angle at the centre of a circle, the straight line that subtends it: I.24's comparison for two radii, set out in numbers. It weighs, too, the scant evidence for an earlier table by Hipparchus.
