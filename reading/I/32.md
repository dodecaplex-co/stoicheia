- Tristan Needham's [*Visual Complex Analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192868916.001.0001) (2023), complex numbers almost entirely in pictures, makes an angle an argument and multiplying a turn, all that the modern way uses, in ch. 1, "Geometry and Complex Arithmetic" (also on [I.1](01.md)).
- On a sphere I.32 fails, and a triangle's angles add up to more than two right angles: Glen Van Brummelen's [*Heavenly Mathematics*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400844807) (2013), a hands-on introduction to spherical trigonometry, works this out in the chapter "Areas, Angles, and Polyhedra".
- Freek Wiedijk's ["Formalizing 100 Theorems"](https://www.cs.ru.nl/~freek/100/) (free online) lists I.32 as no. 27, "Sum of the Angles of a Triangle", among the famous theorems checked by machine, and names the systems that have checked it.
