- For the complex numbers of our modern way, where an angle is an argument and multiplying turns the plane, Tristan Needham's [*Visual Complex Analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192868916.001.0001) (2023), ch. 1, "Geometry and Complex Arithmetic", explains it almost entirely in pictures.
- For a world where I.32 fails, Glen Van Brummelen's [*Heavenly Mathematics*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400844807) (2013), the chapter "Areas, Angles, and Polyhedra", takes us onto the sphere, where a triangle's angles add up to more than two right angles.
- For where I.32 stands among the famous theorems checked by machine, Freek Wiedijk's [*Formalizing 100 Theorems*](https://www.cs.ru.nl/~freek/100/) (free online), no. 27, "Sum of the Angles of a Triangle", keeps a running scoreboard.
