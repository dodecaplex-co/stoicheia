- The crossbar theorem that finds D, and Pasch's axiom behind it, are in ch. 3, "Hilbert's Axioms", of Marvin Jay Greenberg's [*Euclidean and Non-Euclidean Geometries*](https://store.macmillanlearning.com/us/product/Euclidean-and-Non-Euclidean-Geometries/p/0716799480) (2008), a textbook that works through the rules of order Euclid leaves unsaid before it turns to the parallel postulate.
- Felix Klein's lectures of 1908 for future teachers, [*Elementary Mathematics from a Higher Standpoint, Volume II: Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-49445-5) (2016), in Gert Schubring's translation, say why those rules of "between" matter: their section on the *Elements* points out that Euclid has no axioms of betweenness, the gap through which the old geometric sophisms slip.
