- For the crossbar theorem that finds D, and Pasch's axiom behind it, Marvin Jay Greenberg's [*Euclidean and Non-Euclidean Geometries*](https://store.macmillanlearning.com/us/product/Euclidean-and-Non-Euclidean-Geometries/p/0716799480) (2008), ch. 3, "Hilbert's Axioms", a friendly textbook on the rules of order that Euclid leaves unsaid.
- For why those rules of "between" matter, Felix Klein's [*Elementary Mathematics from a Higher Standpoint, Volume II: Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-49445-5) (2016), Gert Schubring's translation of his lectures of 1908, whose section on the *Elements* points out that Euclid has no axioms of betweenness, the gap through which the old geometric sophisms slip.
