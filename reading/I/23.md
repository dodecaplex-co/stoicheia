- For copying an angle with one multiplication, and its mirror image with one conjugation, Liang-shin Hahn's [*Complex Numbers and Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1090/text/052) (1994), the chapter "Complex Numbers", a short, self-contained start that assumes nothing.
- For Euclid's figure as a real part of his proof, as here, where it settles which side and which way, Nathaniel Miller's [*Euclid and His Twentieth Century Rivals*](https://web.stanford.edu/group/cslipublications/cslipublications/site/9781575865072.shtml) (2007), ch. 3, "Diagrammatic Proofs", a short book that builds a formal system around Euclid's diagrams, complete with a computer proof checker.
