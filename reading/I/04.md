- George E. Martin's textbook [*Transformation Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-5680-9) (1982) builds congruence itself out of reflections, and its ch. 5, "Congruence", shows superposition as a rigid motion.
- Eli Maor's [*Trigonometric Delights*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691202198/trigonometric-delights) (2020 paperback; first published 1998), a history of trigonometry told in stories, follows the sine and cosine, which fix I.4's base and area once two sides and their angle are given, from Egyptian pyramid slopes, through Greek tables built from straight lines in a circle, to imaginary angles.
