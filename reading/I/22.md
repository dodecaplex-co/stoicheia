- For when circles and lines must meet, which Simson thought too plain to need saying, Robin Hartshorne's [*Geometry: Euclid and Beyond*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-22676-7) (2000), the chapter "Hilbert's Axioms", which says patiently and exactly when they do.
- For the square root in the modern way, and why ruler and compass can always reach it, Richard Courant and Herbert Robbins' [*What Is Mathematics?*](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL815900M) (1996, revised by Ian Stewart), ch. III, "Geometrical Constructions. The Algebra of Number Fields", a much-loved classic for curious readers.
