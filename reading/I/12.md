- The dot product that finds the foot of a perpendicular is set out in ch. 4, "Vectors and Euclidean spaces", of John Stillwell's [*The Four Pillars of Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/0-387-29052-4) (2005) (also on [I.3](03.md)).
- On a sphere every great circle through a pole is perpendicular to its equator, as in Heath's note. To walk about on one, try the chapter "Exploring the Sphere" of Glen Van Brummelen's [*Heavenly Mathematics*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400844807) (2013), a charming, hands-on introduction to spherical trigonometry.
