- For the dot product that finds the foot of a perpendicular, John Stillwell's [*The Four Pillars of Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/0-387-29052-4) (2005), ch. 4, "Vectors and Euclidean spaces", in the book met under I.3.
- For geometry on a sphere, where every great circle through a pole is perpendicular to its equator, as in Heath's note, Glen Van Brummelen's [*Heavenly Mathematics*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400844807) (2013), the chapter "Exploring the Sphere", a charming, hands-on introduction to spherical trigonometry.
