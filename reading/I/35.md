- For "equality in a new sense", equal area without equal form, Robin Hartshorne's [*Geometry: Euclid and Beyond*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-22676-7) (2000), ch. 5, "Area", patiently shows how Euclid's equal figures become a real theory of area.
- For why "where G falls" needs saying at all, Felix Klein's [*Elementary Mathematics from a Higher Standpoint, Volume II: Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-49445-5) (2016), in Gert Schubring's translation, ends with a tour of the *Elements* that points out Euclid has no axioms of betweenness.
