- For the theorem's whole life, from Babylonian clay tablets to Einstein, Eli Maor's [*The Pythagorean Theorem: A 4,000-Year History*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691196886/the-pythagorean-theorem) (2007) is a warm, wide-ranging biography, with a chapter on Euclid's own proof.
- For hundreds of other proofs, Elisha Scott Loomis' [*The Pythagorean Proposition*](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED037335.pdf) (1940, free online) collects 370 of them: dip in anywhere and compare them with Euclid's windmill.
- For the ox, and the praise of Euclid that Heath quotes, in a full modern translation, Proclus' [*A Commentary on the First Book of Euclid's Elements*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691020907/proclus) (1970), translated by Glenn R. Morrow, has his whole note on I.47.
