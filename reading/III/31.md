- William Dunham takes a proof from III.31 and finishes it his own way, adding up the triangle's angles where Euclid carries BA through to F, then squares Hippocrates' lune with it: ch. 1, "Hippocrates' Quadrature of the Lune" (pp. 1–26), of [*Journey through Genius*](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Journey+through+Genius%3A+The+Great+Theorems+of+Mathematics-p-9780471500308) (1990), a warm, story-filled chapter that begins with Thales.
- For the carpenter's square that tests whether a casting is a true semicircle, and the protractor and plumb line that find a level, David Eugene Smith's [*The Teaching of Geometry*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/37681) (1911, free online), ch. XV (pp. 209–210) (also on [III.14](14.md)).
- The inner product shows the two arms of BAC at right angles in the modern way. John Stillwell explains it in [*The Four Pillars of Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/0-387-29052-4) (2005), §4.4, "The inner product" (from p. 74) (also on [III.2](02.md)).
