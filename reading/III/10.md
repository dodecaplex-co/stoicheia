- For the radical axis of the modern way, found just as there by subtracting one circle's equation from the other's, and at right angles to the line of centres, S. L. Loney's [*The Elements of Coordinate Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/elementsofcoordi00lone) (1895, free online), ch. IX, "Systems of Circles", Art. 183 (p. 161), a clear Victorian textbook that does with equations what Book III does with figures.
- For the radical axis as the line where a point's powers with respect to two circles agree, I. M. Yaglom's [*Geometric Transformations IV: Circular Transformations*](https://doi.org/10.5948/UPO9780883859582) (2009), ch. 3, "Pencils of circles. The radical axis of two circles" (pp. 49–51), which introduces reflection in a circle by analogy with reflection in a line.
