- For why Postulate 5 lies under every fact about circumferences, the remark that had Euclid taken Lobachevsky's or Riemann's assumption in place of the parallel postulate, "he would have been unable to prove that all angles inscribed in the same circular arc are equal", Julian Lowell Coolidge's [*A Treatise on the Circle and the Sphere*](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001508955) (1916, free online), the preface (p. 3), a great old treatise wholly about the circle and the sphere, scholarly but rewarding to dip into.
- For the cross-ratio of the modern way, real when four points lie on one circle, Dan Pedoe's [*Circles: A Mathematical View*](https://doi.org/10.1090/spec/012) (1995), ch. III, §5, "The cross-ratio of four complex numbers" (pp. 47–49), a small, concise book given wholly to the circle, gentle in its opening Chapter 0 and more demanding later.
- For the second case, "wanting in the Greek", and the simpler proof Simson draws from the first, Robert Simson's [*The Elements of Euclid*](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_elements-english-sele_euclid_1756_0) (1756, free online), his note on III.21 (p. 366) (also on [III.1](01.md)).
