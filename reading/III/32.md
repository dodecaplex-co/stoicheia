- Isaac Todhunter calls the converse "true and important": a straight line making with BD the angles in the alternate segments touches the circle. His note on III.32 (p. 277) is in [*The Elements of Euclid*](https://archive.org/details/todhuntereuclid00todhrich) (1872, free online) (also on [III.3](03.md)).
- Two touching circles fill Props. 2–4 of Book III, Section I (pp. 29–30), in John Casey's [*A Sequel to the First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid*](https://archive.org/details/sequeltofirstsix00caserich) (1886, free online): Prop. 2 uses two straight lines through the point of contact, the common tangent and III.32; Prop. 3 uses equal tangents (Prop. 1) and angles in isosceles triangles, adding a limiting deduction from Prop. 2; Prop. 4 cuts the circles at four points with one straight line, then uses the common tangent and III.32 (also on [III.17](17.md)).
- Evan Chen's olympiad course, [*Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads*](https://doi.org/10.5948/9781614444114) (2016), works on the circle |z| = 1, as the modern way does, in §6.4, "The Unit Circle" (pp. 100–102) (also on [III.24](24.md)).
