- For why ruler and compass, which bisect any circumference, cannot in general trisect one, Richard Courant and Herbert Robbins' [*What Is Mathematics?*](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL815900M) (1996), ch. III, "Geometrical Constructions. The Algebra of Number Fields", in a much-loved classic for curious readers.
- For the square roots of the modern way, which halve an arc because multiplying complex numbers adds their angles, Titu Andreescu and Dorin Andrica's [*Complex Numbers from A to ... Z*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-8176-8415-0) (2014), §2.1.4, "Geometric Interpretation of Multiplication" (p. 41) (also on [III.23](23.md)).
- For the fifteen-angled figure of IV.16 and its sequel, Gauss's regular 17-gon, Felix Klein's [*Famous Problems of Elementary Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/famousproblemsof00kleiiala) (1897, free online), about eighty friendly pages on what ruler and compass can and cannot do.
