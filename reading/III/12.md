- For Heron, whose proof for circles touching without became III.12, Thomas L. Heath's [*A History of Greek Mathematics*, vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/cu31924008704227) (1921, free online), ch. XVIII, "Mensuration: Heron of Alexandria", the second of Heath's own history, which runs from Aristarchus to Diophantus.
- For the points of contact of the modern ways of III.11 and III.12, which divide FG in the ratio of the radii, outwardly and inwardly, and which geometers call the two centres of similitude, Roger A. Johnson's [*Modern Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/bwb_P9-AFM-481) (1929, free online), ch. II, §25, "Centers of Similitude of Two Circles" (pp. 18–21), where two circles are seen as similar figures.
