- For Heron's observation that the two straight lines touching from A are equal, proved there in a few lines by I.47, John Casey's [*A Sequel to the First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid*](https://archive.org/details/sequeltofirstsix00caserich) (1886, free online), Book III, Section I, Prop. 1 (p. 29), written in Dublin for readers who had finished Euclid, adding the propositions he left out, proved in Euclid's manner, by the author whose *First Six Books* our README thanks.
- For the square on the straight line touching from A, d² − r², found by putting A's coordinates into the circle's equation, S. L. Loney's [*The Elements of Coordinate Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/elementsofcoordi00lone) (1895, free online), ch. VIII, Art. 168 (p. 142) (also on [III.10](10.md)).
- For a straight line touching from an outside point put to work, finding the inverse of that point in the circle, Tom Davis's ["Inversion in a Circle"](http://www.geometer.org/mathcircles/inversion.pdf) (2011, free online), §2, "Basic Definition of Inversion" (p. 2), one of the friendly handouts he wrote for math circles for high-school students, showing inversion mostly in pictures.
