- For a careful history of the "extreme and mean ratio" that starts from Euclid's own text, Roger Herz-Fischler's [*A Mathematical History of the Golden Number*](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL703522M) (1998), patient and free of legends about pyramids and paintings.
- For the golden-ratio folklore you can now enjoy leaving behind, George Markowsky's ["Misconceptions about the Golden Ratio"](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2686193) (1992), a short, cheerful debunking.
- For how far ruler and compass can reach, from the regular pentagon that Book IV draws with this proposition's help to Gauss's 17-gon, Felix Klein's [*Famous Problems of Elementary Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/famousproblemsof00kleiiala) (1897, free online), the book met under I.9.
