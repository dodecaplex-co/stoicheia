- For a careful history of the "extreme and mean ratio" that starts from Euclid's own text, Roger Herz-Fischler's *A Mathematical History of the Golden Number* (1998), patient and free of legends about pyramids and paintings.
- For the golden-ratio folklore you can now enjoy leaving behind, George Markowsky's ["Misconceptions about the Golden Ratio"](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2686193) (1992), a short, cheerful debunking.
- For how far ruler and compass can reach, the regular pentagon Book IV builds from this section and Gauss's 17-gon among them, Felix Klein's [*Famous Problems of Elementary Geometry*](https://archive.org/details/famousproblemsof00kleiiala) (1897), about eighty friendly pages (free online).
