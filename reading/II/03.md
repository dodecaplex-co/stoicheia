- For Heron and Pappus, whom Heath takes for the first exponents of this semi-algebraical method, Thomas L. Heath's [*A History of Greek Mathematics*, vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/cu31924008704227) (1921), which gives each of them a chapter of his own (free online).
- For the teachers and editors who reworked Euclid's proofs for centuries, as Lardner and Clavius do in Heath's note here, Benjamin Wardhaugh's [*Encounters with Euclid*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691211695/encounters-with-euclid) (2021), a charming history of the Elements' many lives.
