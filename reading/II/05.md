- For a scrap of papyrus from around AD 100 that carries this very proposition and its figure, Bill Casselman's ["The oldest diagram from Euclid"](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~cass/Euclid/papyrus/), a short, lovely page on Papyrus Oxyrhynchus I 29 (free online).
- For quadratic problems solved by cutting and pasting squares and rectangles in Babylon, more than a thousand years before Euclid, Jens Høyrup's [*Algebra in Cuneiform*](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/textbooks/2/) (2017), ch. 3 "The Fundamental Techniques for the Second Degree", a readable introduction to the moves we see here (free online).
