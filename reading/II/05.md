- Bill Casselman's ["The oldest diagram from Euclid"](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~cass/Euclid/papyrus/) (free online) is a short page on Papyrus Oxyrhynchus I 29, a scrap from around AD 100 that carries this very proposition and its figure.
- More than a thousand years before Euclid, Babylonian scribes solved quadratic problems by cutting and pasting squares and rectangles; Jens Høyrup's [*Algebra in Cuneiform*](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/textbooks/2/) (2017, free online) shows how in ch. 3, "The Fundamental Techniques for the Second Degree".
