- For algebra drawn as rectangles, as Euclid draws a(b + c + d) here, Roger B. Nelsen's [*Proofs Without Words*](https://doi.org/10.1090/clrm/001) (1993), "Geometry & Algebra", page after page of pictures that prove things at a glance.
- For the whole of Book II as a mathematician sees it, Benno Artmann's [*Euclid: The Creation of Mathematics*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-1412-0) (1999), the chapter "Euclid Book II: The Geometry of Rectangles", a short, affectionate account of what the book is for.
