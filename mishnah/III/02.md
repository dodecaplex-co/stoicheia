**A straight line that bows outside.** To show that the straight line AB falls within the circle, Euclid supposes that it falls outside, as AEB, and his figure bends it to make room for the impossible. We draw the bend too. Then DE is read two ways. The angles of the triangle DEB make it less than the radius DB; but with E outside, DE reaches past the circumference and is greater than the radius DF. The same straight line would be both less and greater.

**"The destruction of two hypotheses."** Heath would rather prove it directly: "The *reductio ad absurdum* form of proof is not really necessary in this case, and it has the additional disadvantage that it requires the destruction of two hypotheses, namely that the chord is (1) outside, (2) on the circle" (vol. 2, p. 9). Euclid dismisses the second with "Similarly", and in Lean it is the shorter: on the circumference, DE would itself be a radius, and the angles have already made it less than one. So the comparison with the radius, which is all Heath's direct proof needs, carries both.

**What if AB passes through D?** Euclid's figure keeps the centre off AB, so that DAE and DEB are triangles. Our statement lets AB pass through the centre too, and Lean takes that case apart in `Prop2.through_centre`: D is then between A and B, and DE is part of DA or of DB, which are equal. When E is D itself there is no straight line DE at all, and Lean counts its length as zero.

**A rule that is III.2.** System E has this proposition among its rules, as Circle 2: between two points on or within a circle, every point is within it. We follow Euclid instead, and the Lean of III.2 never calls on the rule. III.1 does, though, to put the middle point D within the circle, and III.2 takes its centre from III.1. So III.2 rests, through III.1, on a rule that is its own conclusion, the very gap Isaac Todhunter points out under III.1.

**The modern way.** Put the centre at the origin, with |a| = |b| = r and a ≠ b; for 0 < t < 1, |(1 − t)a + tb|² = r² − t(1 − t)|a − b|² < r², so every point strictly between a and b lies inside the circle.
