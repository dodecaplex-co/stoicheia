**A straight line that bows outside.** Euclid asks us to suppose that the straight line joining A and B leaves the circle. His figure bends AEB to make room for that impossible supposition; we keep the bend. The argument turns on two readings of DE. The angles make it less than the radius DB, but, with E outside, DE reaches beyond the equal radius DF. The same straight line would be both greater and less.

**Two ways not to be within.** Heath writes: "The *reductio ad absurdum* form of proof is not really necessary in this case, and it has the additional disadvantage that it requires the destruction of two hypotheses, namely that the chord is (1) outside, (2) on the circle" (vol. 2, p. 9). Showing that E is not outside leaves the circumference to exclude. There the second contradiction is shorter: DE would itself be a radius, although the angle argument has made it less than one. Heath's direct proof stops at the comparison with the radius.

**What if AB passes through D?** Euclid's figure places the centre off AB, so that DAE and DEB are triangles. Our statement also admits a straight line through the centre. Lean separates this case in `Prop2.through_centre`: D lies between A and B, and we compare DE with whichever of the equal radii DA, DB contains it. If E is D, its distance from D is zero. The angle argument then handles every remaining case, and its conclusion serves both contradictions.

**The result is already a rule.** System E's Circle 2 says that a point between two points on or within a circle lies within it. On the circumference, this is III.2 itself. We follow Euclid's proof here, but III.1 uses Circle 2 to put the middle of its chosen straight line within the circle before finding the centre. Thus III.2 still rests through III.1 on a rule containing its own conclusion, the difficulty Isaac Todhunter raises under III.1.

**The modern way.** Put the centre at the origin, with |a| = |b| = r and a ≠ b; for 0 < t < 1, |(1 − t)a + tb|² = r² − t(1 − t)|a − b|² < r², so every interior point of the chord is strictly inside the circle.
