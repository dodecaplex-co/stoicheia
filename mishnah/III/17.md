**A second circle finds the point of contact.** Euclid knows how to draw a straight line touching a circle at a given point of its circumference. Here the point given is outside, and the point of contact must be found. We borrow a right angle from the larger circle: the equal triangles carry the right angle at D to B, where III.16's porism makes AB touch.

**Meeting is not yet touching.** Heath distinguishes the Greek verbs ἅπτεσθαι and ἐφάπτεσθαι: "the former meaning 'to *meet*' and the latter 'to *touch*.' The distinction was generally observed by Greek geometers from Euclid onwards" (vol. 2, p. 2). The construction puts the distinction to work. AE cuts the given circle, while AB reaches it at B without passing within. Finding a common point is part of the problem; keeping the straight line outside is what the right angle supplies.

**D is already there in the picture.** Our circles carry a centre only by hypothesis, so we find the two points III.1 needs on the straight line from that centre to A, one towards A and one beyond the centre; III.1's centre is the same point. The first crossing gives D between E and A. The Lean cites I.11 for drawing DF at right angles to EA. Our `Touches` says that AB meets the circle and does not cut it; `LineMeetsCircle`, despite its name, means that a straight line cuts a circle in two distinct points. These meanings keep Heath's distinction intact.

**Two straight lines, one length.** Heath credits Heron with observing that the two straight lines touching from A are "equal in length and equally inclined to the straight line joining the external point to the centre" (vol. 2, p. 44). Taking F on the other side of EA gives the second. We ask for A outside the circle. As Heath explains on the same page, Euclid leaves out a point on the circumference because III.16's porism already covers it. From a point within, no straight line touches.

**The modern way.** With E at the origin, A = (d, 0) and radius 0 < r < d, Euclid's D is (r, 0), his F is (r, √(d² − r²)), and B = (r/d)F = (r²/d, (r/d)√(d² − r²)). Thus B·(B − A) = r² − r² = 0: EB is perpendicular to AB, whose length is √(d² − r²).
