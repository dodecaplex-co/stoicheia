**Produce FG beyond G.** Euclid joins the centres and asks where the straight line leaves the inner circle. If it misses A, his inequality puts D beyond H, outside the outer circle. That contradicts internal contact. Following J. G. Camerer's analysis, Heath finds the difficulty in what the figure silently assumes (vol. 2, pp. 25–26). We keep Heath's drawing of the false case: G stands above F, but cannot be the actual centre of the inner circle drawn.

**Do they avoid cutting only here?** Definition III.3 says that touching circles meet but do not cut one another. Does that concern the point of contact, or the whole circles? Isaac Todhunter chooses the whole circles. Heath disagrees: "Todhunter thinks the latter opinion correct. I do not think this is proved; and I prefer to read the definition as meaning simply that the circles meet at a point but do not cut *at that point*" (vol. 2, p. 3). On Heath's reading, absence of a crossing elsewhere still needs a proof.

**The whole inner circle stays within.** Heath says Euclid "does practically assume in III. 11–13, without stating, the theorem that circles touching at one point do not intersect anywhere else" (vol. 2, p. 3). We take the whole-circle reading in `TouchesInternally`: two distinct circles meet at A, and no point of ADE is outside ABC. Inside and outside make Todhunter's reading precise. The definition carries the tacit claim that they do not cross elsewhere. This does not define contact as having exactly one common point. III.13 still has to rule out a second point of contact.

**The picture has to earn its order.** Lean first establishes that G lies within ABC, between two points of ADE. Then `Between F G A` says which way FG must be produced. These are the direction and containment that Heath's repair demands (vol. 2, p. 26): D cannot be farther from G than H, since D cannot be outside ABC. We also handle A on GF produced beyond F, the case Heath says Euclid leaves to the reader. The strict inequality excludes D coinciding with H. The Lean cites III.6 to distinguish the centres and I.20 for the inequality of the triangle's sides; Euclid gives no citation for either here.

**The modern way.** Circles with centres F, G and radii R > r touch internally exactly when FG = R − r, and the point of contact is then A = F + (R/(R − r))(G − F), beyond G on the ray from F.
