**What the figure assumes.** Euclid joins the centres, produces the straight line beyond G to meet the outer circle at H, and supposes that H is not A; then GD is greater than GH, "the less than the greater". Heath finds the proof "beset with difficulties in consequence of what it tacitly assumes in the figure" (vol. 2, p. 25), and sets out J. G. Camerer's careful note on them. Our figure is Heath's, drawn for the false case, so G there cannot be the true centre of the inner circle.

**Do they avoid cutting only here?** Definition III.3 says that touching circles meet but do not cut one another. Does that concern the point of contact, or the whole circles? Isaac Todhunter chooses the whole circles. Heath disagrees: "Todhunter thinks the latter opinion correct. I do not think this is proved; and I prefer to read the definition as meaning simply that the circles meet at a point but do not cut *at that point*" (vol. 2, p. 3). On Heath's reading, that they cross nowhere else still needs a proof.

**The whole inner circle stays within.** Heath says Euclid "does practically assume in III. 11–13, without stating, the theorem that circles touching at one point do not intersect anywhere else" (vol. 2, p. 3). We take the whole-circle reading in `TouchesInternally`: two distinct circles meet at A, and no point of ADE is outside ABC. Inside and outside make Todhunter's reading precise. The definition carries the tacit claim that they do not cross elsewhere. This does not define contact as having exactly one common point. III.13 still has to rule out a second point of contact.

**Produce FG beyond G.** Heath's repair asks two things: that FG be produced beyond G, the centre of the inner circle, and that the point where it meets the inner circle be no farther from G than the point where it meets the other (vol. 2, p. 26). The statement says the first: `Between F G A`. Lean does both. G lies within ABC, between two points of ADE, so FG produced beyond G meets ABC at H; and Euclid's inequality, GD greater than GH, puts D beyond H, outside ABC, which internal contact forbids. The case Heath says Euclid would have left to the reader, A on GF produced beyond F, takes one step more: there AG is AF and FG together, so it exceeds AF. The Lean cites III.6 to distinguish the centres and I.20 for the triangle AFG; Euclid brackets neither.

**The modern way.** Circles with centres F, G and radii R > r touch internally exactly when FG = R − r, and the point of contact is then A = F + (R/(R − r))(G − F), beyond G on the ray from F.
