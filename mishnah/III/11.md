**"Beset with difficulties."** Euclid joins the centres F and G, produces the straight line beyond G to meet the outer circle at H, and supposes that H is not A; then GD comes out greater than GH, "the less than the greater". Heath finds the proof "beset with difficulties in consequence of what it tacitly assumes in the figure" (vol. 2, p. 25), and sets out J. G. Camerer's careful note. Our figure is Heath's, drawn for the impossible case, so its G is not truly the inner circle's centre.

**Must touching circles never cross?** Definition III.3 has touching circles meet but not cut one another. Isaac Todhunter reads this of the whole circles, Heath of the point of contact alone: "Todhunter thinks the latter opinion correct. I do not think this is proved; and I prefer to read the definition as meaning simply that the circles meet at a point but do not cut *at that point*" (vol. 2, p. 3). On Heath's reading, that they cross nowhere else wants a proof, and Euclid "does practically assume in III. 11–13, without stating, the theorem that circles touching at one point do not intersect anywhere else" (vol. 2, p. 3).

**Todhunter's reading, made exact.** Heath's reading lives at a single point, and Lean has no neighbourhoods to say it in. So we take Todhunter's whole circles, made exact with the inside and outside that Heath says the definition must imply: in `TouchesInternally`, two different circles meet at A, and no point of ADE is outside ABC. Our definition carries what Euclid assumes, that touching circles cross nowhere. That they meet at one point only is left for III.13.

**"Absolutely conclusive."** Euclid's proof becomes "absolutely conclusive", Heath shows, once FG is produced beyond G, the centre of the inner circle, and found to meet that circle "*not further* from G" than the other (vol. 2, p. 26). Our statement asks the first, `Between F G A`, and Lean finds the second inside Euclid's argument: G lies within ABC, between two points of ADE, and GD greater than GH puts D beyond H, outside ABC, which touching internally forbids. Euclid cites nothing for "AG, GF are greater than FA", which is I.20, or for the centres being two, III.6.

**The modern way.** Circles with centres F, G and radii R > r touch internally exactly when FG = R − r, and the point of contact is then A = F + (R/(R − r))(G − F), beyond G on the ray from F.
