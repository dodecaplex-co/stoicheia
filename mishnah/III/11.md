Euclid joins the centres F and G, produces the straight line beyond G to meet the outer circle at H, and supposes that H is not A. The triangle inequality then makes AG greater than GH. Since AG and GD are radii of the inner circle, GD would be greater than GH, though D should come no later than H. Our figure is Heath's, drawn for this impossible supposition, so its G is not truly the inner circle's centre.

**Where must touching circles stop crossing?** Heath distinguishes two readings of Definition III.3 (vol. 2, p. 3). Isaac Todhunter reads it of the whole circles: they meet but cut nowhere. Heath prefers a reading at the point of contact, leaving their behaviour elsewhere to be proved. He also observes that Euclid assumes in III.11–III.13 that circles touching at one point do not intersect elsewhere. Our definition follows the whole-circle reading. That choice matters here, because D must be forbidden from falling outside ABC however far it lies from A.

In `TouchesInternally`, ABC and ADE meet at A, a point of ADE is within ABC, and no point of ADE is outside ABC. The point within expresses the passage into the outer circle that Heath says the definition must imply. It makes the circles different without a separate inequality hypothesis. III.6 uses it to show that F and G differ, so FG can be drawn. The definition still does not say that A is the only common point; III.13 proves that.

Heath's discussion of J. G. Camerer's objections identifies the direction of production as essential: FG must be produced beyond the inner centre G (vol. 2, pp. 25–26). Our conclusion says just that, `Between F G A`. The proof first puts G within ABC, between two points of ADE, neither outside ABC. It then shows that GD greater than GH puts D beyond H and outside ABC, contradicting contact within. I.20 supplies the triangle inequality; Heath gives it no bracketed reference here.

**The modern way.** Circles with centres F, G and radii R > r touch internally exactly when FG = R − r. The point of contact lies beyond G on the ray from F, as the proposition concludes.
