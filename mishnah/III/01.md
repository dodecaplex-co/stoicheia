**Find the centre, then justify it.** Euclid bisects AB to choose where to draw the straight line on which the centre must lie; his second bisection picks out the centre itself. The distinction matters: finding a straight line through the centre does not yet locate a point. The Lean cites I.10 for the two bisections and I.11 for drawing the right angle, steps Euclid leaves without citations.

**Why assume a centre?** Robert Simson observed that the proof must proceed by *reductio ad absurdum*. Heath explains: "At the beginning of Book III. we have nothing more to base the proof upon than the definition of a circle, and this cannot be made use of unless we assume some point to be the centre" (vol. 2, p. 8). We therefore give the circle a centre by hypothesis, without identifying it with the point constructed. Equal radii then let the argument rule out every other position.

**Two crossings, and then a middle.** Isaac Todhunter points out that D is assumed to lie within the circle, a fact Euclid proves in III.2. Heath adds that the construction needs the perpendicular to meet the circle twice, something the preceding results do not ensure (vol. 2, p. 7). There is a further step at the end: "It is however taken for granted rather than proved that the centre must be the middle point of *CE*" (vol. 2, p. 8).

**D may already be the centre.** We use System E's Circle 2 to put D within the circle, the very fact proved in III.2, and Intersections 5 to find C and E. If AB passes through the centre, F = D, so the angle FDB disappears. Lean compares the right angles CDB and GDB instead, with G on either side of AB. Once G is on CE, equal radii and `Prop1.middle` identify it with F. We obtain the porism from the construction, using uniqueness of the centre and of the perpendicular to identify the cutting straight line.

**The modern way.** For distinct position vectors a and b, |p − a|² = |p − b|² is equivalent to 2p·(b − a) = |b|² − |a|², the perpendicular bisector of AB. Since the centre o lies on it, the circle of radius r meets it at c = o + ru and e = o − ru (u a unit vector along the line), and their midpoint (c + e)/2 is o.
