**The diameter makes the comparison.** Euclid builds BA at right angles to EF, the construction of I.11. The angle in the semicircle then lets him compare two right angles: one at B, the other made up of the remaining angles of triangle BAD. Removing ABD gives the first equality. For the second, he compares the opposite angles of the quadrilateral with DBF and DBE, whose sum is two right angles by I.13. We use III.21 to let the angle in BAD stand for any angle in that segment.

**The argument runs backwards.** Heath gives the converse on p. 66 and proves it "with Clavius, directly" (vol. 2, p. 67). Christopher Clavius' route adds ABD back, recovers the right angle ABF, and uses the porism to III.16 to show that EF touches the circle. The equality can therefore test for contact as well as follow from it.

**One tangent joins two circles' arguments.** Heath reports Pappus' consequence: "*If two circles touch, any straight line drawn through the point of contact and terminated by both circles cuts off segments in each which are respectively similar.*" (vol. 2, p. 67). Draw their common tangent: III.32 equates each angle in a segment with the angle at contact. Both circles answer to the same straight line.

**The figure chooses a side.** Lean needs the circle, apart from B, to lie on one side of EF. Our `Prop32.sameSide` proves this: if two points lay on opposite sides, the straight line between them would meet EF inside the circle, although EF touches it. The figure also puts D on F's side of BA. We repeat the same argument with E and F exchanged when D is on the other side. If BD is itself the diameter, III.31 makes each angle in either segment right, matching the two right angles at B; Heath mentions this case under III.33 (vol. 2, p. 70).

**The modern way.** On |z| = 1, write D = Be^{iθ}, with 0 < θ < 2π: then D − B = 2 sin(θ/2)·iB·e^{iθ/2}. Thus BD turns through θ/2 from the tangent direction iB, half the counterclockwise arc BD, the angle at any interior point of the complementary arc; the opposite tangent ray gives the supplementary angle.
