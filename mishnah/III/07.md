**Turn away from the centre.** From F, the straight line through E reaches the farthest point A of the circle; the other direction reaches the nearest point D. Euclid then orders everything between them. On either side of AD, the farther a straight line turns from FA, the less it becomes. This is why the last claim belongs with the comparisons: after constructing FH equal to FG on the other side, Euclid can rule out a third equal straight line by the ordering he has just proved. We need no new comparison for that last step.

**Nearer at which point?** Heath writes, "De Morgan points out that there is an unproved assumption in this demonstration" (vol. 2, p. 15). Augustus De Morgan's objection concerns the move from F to E. The straight lines are ordered by the angles they make at F, but I.24 compares the angles between the equal radii at E. Those two orderings need a bridge. Heath's proof of it begins with a crossing, "*FB* must cut *EC* in some point *M*" (vol. 2, p. 15), and goes on by the perpendicular from E on FB. His M belongs to the figure in his note, and Euclid's figure keeps Euclid's letters.

**The crossing lies within.** Lean goes by the circle instead. `BookI.Prop24.within` puts FB within the angle EFC, and `crossbar` finds M between E and C, on the ray from F through B. System E's Circle 2 puts M within the circle. It must lie before B: if B lay between F and M, B too would be within the circle. Now EM lies within the angle BEF, so CEF is less than BEF, exactly what I.24 needs. Our statement speaks of every point of the circle, so B may be D, G may be A, and FK may lie along AD. Circle 1 puts E between any two points of the circle on AD, so equal radii make such a point A or D, and FA greatest or FD least settles the case.

**The modern way.** Put E at the origin, let the radius be r, and put F at distance d towards D, with 0 < d < r: a ray making angle θ with FA reaches the circle at distance ρ(θ) = d cos θ + √(r² − d² sin² θ). This strictly decreases from r + d to r − d as θ runs from 0 to π; the symmetry ρ(−θ) = ρ(θ) gives exactly two equal straight lines for every intermediate length, one on each side of AD.
