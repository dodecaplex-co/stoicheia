**"More than two equal straight lines."** Euclid turns equal lengths into right angles. He bisects AB and BC at E and F, and I.8 makes the two angles at E equal, and so right, and those at F too. By the porism to III.1 the centre lies on each of the straight lines GK and HL, and these have only D in common, so D is the centre. The construction that opened Book III comes back, now to recognise the centre in a given point.

**A logical equivalent, proved again.** Augustus De Morgan saw it already proved: "III. 9 is, as De Morgan remarks, a *logical* equivalent of part of III. 7, where it is proved that every *non*-central point is *not* a point from which three equal straight lines can be drawn to the circle" (Heath, vol. 2, p. 22). H. M. Taylor draws III.9 from his own III.7 as a corollary. Euclid proves it afresh, and Heath sees a habit in that: "Euclid does not allow himself these logical inferences, as we shall have occasion to observe elsewhere also" (vol. 2, p. 22).

**"The most obscure case for proof."** J. G. Camerer asks what happens if AB passes through D: then one of the middle points is D itself, and there is no straight line ED or FD to draw. Heath answers with the habit met under III.6: Euclid takes "the most obscure case for proof" (vol. 2, p. 22). Our proof leaves nothing to the reader, and one more bisection, of CA, answers Camerer.

**Within, or anywhere?** Euclid takes D within the circle, and our statement leaves "within" out. His proof never uses it: ED and FD are drawn through from E and F, which are within wherever D is, and III.25 needs it in this form, for a point not yet known to be within. One thing the figure shows still wants its reason, that GK and HL are two straight lines, and I.17 gives it.

**The modern way.** The conditions |X − A| = |X − B| = |X − C| are two linear equations, 2X·(B − A) = |B|² − |A|² and 2X·(C − B) = |C|² − |B|², with B − A and C − B independent, since A, B, C are not on one straight line. So exactly one point is as far from all three, and the centre and D are both that point.
