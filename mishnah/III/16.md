**No room for a straight line.** Euclid proves two things of the straight line AE at right angles to the diameter at A: it falls outside the circle, and no other straight line through A can be interposed between it and the circumference. The second is the striking one: turn AE about A however little, and it cuts the circle.

**Is contact an angle?** The enunciation ends with two angles contained by a straight line and a circumference, the angle of the semicircle and the horn-like angle beside it. Our angles are made by two straight lines only, so our statement leaves that clause out and keeps what Euclid proves it by: every other straight line through A has a point within the circle, and so cuts it. Why mention such angles at all? Heath's guess is that "Euclid thought that an allusion to them would be expected of him" (vol. 2, p. 39). Centuries later Jacques Peletier held that the horn-like angle is no angle: "the essence of an angle is in *cutting*, not contact" (Heath, vol. 2, p. 41).

**"At one point only."** J. L. Heiberg drops as Theon's addition the words "and that a straight line touches a circle at one point only, since in fact the straight line meeting it in two points was proved to fall within it" (Heath, vol. 2, p. 43). Here they come back as our porism's proof, for that is just how `Touches` reads Definition III.2. Robert Simson added that only one straight line touches the circle at a given point (Heath, vol. 2, p. 43), and that follows from the statement's second clause.

**Could the foot be A?** Euclid's figure puts G, the foot of DG, away from A. Were G at A, FA would be at right angles to DA as AE is, but at A only one straight line is at right angles to AD, as in III.1. If FA passes through the centre, D itself is the point within.

**The modern way.** With centre O and radius r, the points A + tu (u ≠ 0) of a straight line through A on the circle satisfy |A + tu − O|² = r² + t(2(A − O)·u + t|u|²). So if u is at right angles to OA, every point but A is outside; otherwise the straight line is within the circle between t = 0 and t = −2(A − O)·u/|u|².
