**No room for a straight line.** Euclid first shows that AE falls outside the circle, then that every other straight line through A passes within. The second claim gives the first its force: turning the straight line at all makes it cut the circle. Heath's figure draws the supposition, with G beyond H. We draw what the proof establishes: G within the circle, H beyond G on DG produced.

**Is contact an angle?** The angle of the semicircle and the horn-like angle beside it are mixed angles, as in Definition III.7, discussed under III.31. They are not angles of ours. Our statement carries the rectilineal facts Euclid proves them by. Heath suggests that Euclid mentions them because his contemporaries expected the subject to be acknowledged (vol. 2, p. 39). Centuries later Jacques Peletier argued that "the essence of an angle is in *cutting*, not contact" (Heath, vol. 2, p. 41). What counts as an angle was itself at issue.

**One point of contact.** J. L. Heiberg drops as Theon's addition the words "and that a straight line touches a circle at one point only, since in fact the straight line meeting it in two points was proved to fall within it" (Heath, vol. 2, p. 43). Our `Touches`, the reading of Definition III.2 discussed under III.17, says that the straight line meets the circle but never in two points. Those omitted words are exactly what the porism proves. Robert Simson's further observation, that just one straight line touches at a given point, follows from our second clause.

**The foot cannot be A.** Lean asks why G differs from A: otherwise FA would be another perpendicular to DA there. We prove that perpendicular unique in `Prop16.one_perp`. If FA passes through D, the centre itself supplies a point within. The Lean cites I.12 for drawing DG, where Euclid gives no citation. The second clause supplies a point within every other straight line through A; System E, Intersection 3 then says it cuts the circle. That is what Euclid's last paragraph rests on.

**The modern way.** With centre O, radius r and nonzero direction u, the points A + tu of a straight line through a point A on the circle satisfy |A + tu − O|² = r² + t(2(A − O)·u + t|u|²). If u is at right angles to OA every point but A is outside; otherwise the straight line meets the circle again at t = −2(A − O)·u/|u|², every point between being inside.
