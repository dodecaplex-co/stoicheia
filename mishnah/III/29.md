**Back from the circumference to the straight line.** III.28 begins with equal straight lines and compares the circumferences they cut off. Euclid now travels the other way. III.27 supplies equal angles at the centres, and the radii supply the two sides needed for I.4. Here we see why the circles must be equal: the angles alone would give no equality of length. This is the reading by equal radii in Heath's note on Definition III.1, discussed at III.26.

**A diameter leaves no triangle.** Heath writes: "The particular case of this converse of III. 28 in which the given arcs are arcs of semicircles is even easier than the corresponding case of III. 28 itself." (vol. 2, p. 61). When BC passes through K, BGC is a semicircle, so the equal circumference EHF is one too. Both straight lines are diameters. Each is made of two radii, and their equality needs no comparison of triangles.

**Which circumference does the angle see?** Lean makes us distinguish three positions of the centre: beyond the given circumference, on its straight line, or on its side. When BGC and EHF are greater than semicircles, we take them from the equal whole circles. The remaining circumferences BAC and EDF are equal, and Euclid's triangle argument applies to those. Heath's discussion under III.26 of angles at the centre equal to or greater than two right angles reaches this case too. Our statement lets G and H name a side without lying on the circles; the proof finds points of the circles on the required sides.

**One circle will do.** Heath adds that III.26–III.29 "are of course equally true if the *same* circle is taken instead of *two equal* circles" (vol. 2, p. 61). We can let the two circles be one, since its radii are equal to one another. Nothing in the argument needs the circles to lie apart as they do in the picture, or even to be different circles.

**The modern way.** An arc of angular measure θ in a circle of radius r is subtended by a chord of length 2r sin(θ/2), so equal arcs in equal circles have equal chords, arcs greater than a semicircle included.
