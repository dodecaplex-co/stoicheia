**The figure drawn double.** Euclid puts BD equal to CB beyond B and describes the square AEFD. The square described on AB, BC as on one straight line is `sq A D`, for any D so placed. The diameter DE and two pairs of parallels divide the square into nine figures. I.36 and I.43 make four equal to CK, then four equal to AG. These eight form the gnomon STU, four times AK; adding OH completes the square. Heath explains the gnomon in his note on p. 371, and remarks under II.5 that Euclid's figure marks it with three letters and a dotted curve (vol. 1, p. 383). So STU labels the figure, not three points, and Lean writes the sum of its three parallelograms.

**What the drawing assumes.** The unbracketed construction uses `BookI.Prop46`. A point off AD supplies the side on which to build: `exists_off_line` uses Postulates 1 and 3, Definition 15 and `circles_meet` (System E, Intersection 5). Postulate 1 also names the straight lines. Two calls to `Parallelogram.figure` draw the figure double, bringing in I.30 and I.31. `BookI.Prop30.meet` supplies G and R, since a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, as Proclus argues under I.31. `Parallel.between` puts the points in order; `Prop8.cell` recognizes the parallelograms. `Parallelogram.about_diameter` assembles the pieces and also brings in I.30.

**The squares within the square.** `Square.about_diameter` identifies KD, CP, GR and OH as squares, using I.5, I.6, I.29 and I.34, the step discussed under II.4's porism (Heath, vol. 1, p. 381). I.34 supplies the opposite-side equalities CB = GK, BD = KN, BK = CG and OQ = AC. `Rectangle.of_right` makes AK a rectangle by I.29. Naming it by its sides with `Rectangle.eq_rect`, and naming OH with `Square.eq_sq`, rests on I.4 and I.34. `Square.described` also brings in I.28 to identify the constructed square as a parallelogram.

**Heath's other proofs.** Heath records Pappus' use of the proposition and Euclid's use in the *Data*, Prop. 86, adding that "it is of decided use in proving the fundamental property of a parabola" (vol. 1, p. 391). He gives Clavius's proof from II.4 and II.7, following the observation under II.7 (p. 389), then another dissection with four rectangles around a square (pp. 391–392). Our proof follows Euclid's successive equalities of the eight figures.

**The modern way.** With AB = a and BC = b, the identity is 4ab + (a − b)² = (a + b)². This is the geometrical algebra discussed by Heath before the propositions (vol. 1, pp. 372–374), expressed here by equal figures and their sums.
