**The figure proves the equality.** Heath writes: "It is important however to bear in mind that the whole procedure of Book II. is *geometrical*; rectangles and squares are shown in the figures, and the equality of certain combinations to other combinations is proved by those figures" (vol. 1, p. 373). Here the diameter BD and the parallels CF, HK divide the square into two squares and two equal complements. The proof identifies each piece before putting them together again.

**Where the parallels meet.** Euclid names G before asking whether CF meets BD. `Parallelogram.figure` supplies that meeting and places every point between the ends of the appropriate straight line. It cites I.31 for the parallels and I.30 for their meetings, by the argument Heath reports Proclus drawing under I.31. `Parallelogram.complements` and `Parallelogram.about_diameter` also use I.30 to establish the complements and the division of the whole. Before the square is drawn, `exists_off_line` supplies an unlettered point to choose its side. This uses Postulates 1 and 3 and Definition 15; Postulate 1 also joins BD. Postulate 5 enters through the cited propositions, including I.29 and I.30.

**All four angles.** I.29 and I.5 give the equal angles of the triangle BCG; I.6 gives BC equal to CG, and I.34 supplies the remaining sides. Euclid then proves all four angles right. Common Notion 3, left unnamed in his text, takes the right angle KBC from two right angles and leaves BCG right. For HF, his phrase "For the same reason" becomes `Square.about_diameter`, proved from I.5, I.6, I.29 and I.34. Heath calls the Greek text's porism about these squares "undoubtedly interpolated" (vol. 1, p. 381); the shared lemma carries out the reasoning needed here.

**Naming the pieces.** The unbracketed equality of AG and GE is I.43, the equality of complements. `Rectangle.of_right` makes AG a rectangle using I.29. That the rectangle drawn is the rectangle contained by its sides rests on rectangles with equal sides being equal, proved once from I.4 and I.34 in `Rectangle.contained`. `Square.eq_sq` uses the same ground for the smaller squares; `Square.described` also brings in I.28 to make ADEB a parallelogram. These account for the certificate's citations beyond Euclid's brackets. The two complements are each the rectangle contained by AC, CB, and the four pieces exhaust the square on AB.

**The modern way.** With AC = a and CB = b, the identity is (a + b)² = a² + b² + 2ab.
