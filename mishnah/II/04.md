**Shown in the figures.** Heath writes: "It is important however to bear in mind that the whole procedure of Book II. is *geometrical*; rectangles and squares are shown in the figures, and the equality of certain combinations to other combinations is proved by those figures" (vol. 1, p. 373). Here the diameter BD and the parallels CF, HK cut the square on AB into two squares and two equal complements, and Euclid names each piece before he puts the four together again.

**Where G falls.** Euclid draws CF, then HK "through G", without saying that G is where CF crosses BD. `Parallelogram.figure` draws the parallels by I.31, finds where they meet BD and the sides by I.30, with the argument Heath reports Proclus giving under I.31, and puts each point between the ends of its straight line. I.30 comes in again through `Parallelogram.complements`, which makes AG and GE parallelograms, and `Parallelogram.about_diameter`, which makes the four pieces the whole. A point off AB, with no letter in the figure, chooses the square's side (`exists_off_line`, by Postulates 1 and 3 and Definition 15). Postulate 5 comes in through the propositions cited, I.29 and I.30 among them.

**All four angles.** Euclid proves CGKB a square in full: I.29 and I.5 make the angles CGB, GBC equal, I.6 makes CG equal to CB, and I.34 gives the other sides; I.29 makes the angles KBC, GCB two right angles, and Common Notion 3, unnamed in his text, takes away the right angle KBC and leaves BCG right. The proof attributed to Theon stops at the right angle at B, "a legitimate abbreviation of the genuine proof", Heath says (vol. 1, p. 381), since one right angle makes a parallelogram right-angled. "For the same reason" is the porism Heath calls "undoubtedly interpolated" (vol. 1, p. 381), that the parallelograms about the diameter of a square are squares; Lean proves it once, as `Square.about_diameter`, by I.5, I.6, I.29 and I.34.

**Naming the pieces.** "AG is equal to GE" has no bracket: it is I.43, on the complements. AG has a right angle at C, beside BCG, so `Rectangle.of_right` makes it a rectangle, by I.29. It is the rectangle AC, CB, since GC is equal to CB; that a rectangle drawn is the rectangle contained by its sides rests on I.4 and I.34 (`Rectangle.contained`). `Square.eq_sq` names CK and HF the squares on CB and AC in the same way, and `Square.described` brings in I.28 as well, to make ADEB a parallelogram.

**The modern way.** With AC = a and CB = b, II.4 is (a + b)² = a² + b² + 2ab. For vectors, |u + v|² = |u|² + |v|² + 2u·v, and AC and CB point the same way, so for them u·v is ab.
