**Contained by two straight lines.** Heath explains the Greek expression: "In both cases the substantive and participle can be omitted because the feminine or neuter of the article enables us to distinguish whether an angle or a rectangle is meant" (vol. 1, p. 370). Our `rect` names the rectangle contained by two straight lines from their lengths. `Rectangle.eq_rect` identifies a drawn rectangle with that magnitude: rectangles contained by equal straight lines are equal, proved once from I.4 and I.34. Euclid takes this equality for granted whenever he names a rectangle by its sides.

**Making room for the cut.** I.11 supplies the perpendicular direction, but I.3 cuts from a greater straight line. `BookI.Prop2` first places a straight line equal to A at B, and `produce_longer` supplies enough length along BF before I.3 cuts off BG. This brings in I.2 beyond Euclid's brackets. Heath places F beyond G; Lean fixes their common ray without deciding their order. To start I.11, `exists_off_line` supplies a point off BC, using Postulates 1 and 3, Definition 15 and the rules for circles.

**Where the parallels meet.** `BookI.Prop31.parallelogram` draws each parallel through D, E, C and finds its meeting with GH. It brings in I.30: a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, as Heath reports Proclus proving under I.31. `BookI.Prop30.parallel` also uses I.30 to make the uprights parallel to one another. `Parallel.between` then puts G, K, L, H in order. Lean needs this order and the inner parallelograms before `quadArea_add_of_parallelograms` can cut the whole into its three pieces.

**The three rectangles.** `Rectangle.of_right` brings in I.29: one right angle makes all four right. The adjacent right angles at D and E then make DL and EH rectangles too. I.34 gives DK and EL equal to BG, so every piece is the rectangle contained by A and its part of BC. Our statement takes the two points of section in Euclid's setting-out. As Heath observes under II.2, Euclid never uses II.1 later; Heron's account through an-Nairīzī, discussed under II.4, nevertheless singles out II.1 as needing the straight lines drawn.

**The modern way.** With A = a, BD = b, DE = c and EC = d, the identity is a(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ad. To divide both straight lines, Heath says that "a series of parallels to BC would have to be drawn as well as the series of parallels to BF" (vol. 1, p. 376).
