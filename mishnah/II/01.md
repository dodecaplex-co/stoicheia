**A rectangle named like an angle.** Book II does its multiplying with rectangles, and II.1 cuts one into strips. Euclid names a rectangle as he names an angle, by the two straight lines that contain it, and Heath explains how the Greek shortens both: "In both cases the substantive and participle can be omitted because the feminine or neuter of the article enables us to distinguish whether an angle or a rectangle is meant" (vol. 1, p. 370). So wherever Euclid leaves out "contained", Heath writes simply "the rectangle A, BC". Augustus De Morgan advised "the rectangle *under* two lines" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 376). Heath declines it, for it might suggest that the Greek word means "under", which it does not.

**Two points for "any number".** Euclid's enunciation cuts BC into "any number of segments whatever"; his setting-out cuts it at two points, D and E, and so does our statement, which names the ends of his straight line A as A₁ and A₂. Euclid never cites II.1 again, as Heath remarks under II.2, but Heron made it the root of the rest. He explains, Heath reports from an-Nairīzī, "that it is not possible to prove II. 1 without drawing a number of lines (i.e. without actually drawing the rectangles), but that the following propositions up to II. 10 inclusive can be proved by merely drawing one line" (vol. 1, p. 373).

**The rectangle drawn is the rectangle named.** Our `rect` is the rectangle contained by two straight lines, taken from their lengths. Euclid draws BH and says it is the rectangle A, BC, "for it is contained by GB, BC, and BG is equal to A". He takes for granted that a rectangle drawn is the rectangle contained by its sides, or by straight lines equal to them. We prove it once, from I.4 and I.34, as `Rectangle.eq_rect`, and Book II leans on it again and again.

**The modern way.** Write a for A and b, c, d for BD, DE, EC: II.1 is the distributive law, a(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ad. For a sum times a sum the figure grows both ways, Heath notes: "a series of parallels to BC would have to be drawn as well as the series of parallels to BF" (vol. 1, p. 376).
