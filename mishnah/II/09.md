**Squares without their outlines.** Heath observes that "the method of demonstration by showing in the figures the various rectangles and squares to which the theorems relate is here abandoned" (vol. 1, p. 394). On the same page he remarks that every proposition from II.9 on is proved by means of I.47. Four right triangles do the work: ACE and EGF give doubles, AEF puts them together, and ADF gives the squares sought. Our proof calls `BookI.Prop47.sq` four times, including the unbracketed comparison of the square on EF with the squares on EG, GF.

**The unbracketed steps.** I.11 draws CE at right angles to AB; `OnRay.exists_len_eq` makes it equal to AC. I.5 gives the equal angles at the base of each isosceles triangle. I.31 draws DF and FG through `BookI.Prop31.line` and `BookI.Prop31.parallelogram`. `BookI.Prop30.meet`, also inside the latter step, supplies the meetings with EB and CE: a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, as Heath reports Proclus deducing from I.30 under I.31. These steps account for I.5, I.11, I.30 and I.31 beyond Euclid's brackets.

**Where the points fall.** Lean proves that F lies between E and B and G between E and C with `Between.of_sameSide`, which carries order across a straight line. Half a right angle is expressed by an angle added to itself being equal to a right angle. `Magnitude.eq_of_add_self_eq` makes two such halves equal. At D, `adjacent_eq_of_right` transfers the right angle FDB to ADF. The statement permits D on either side of C; exchanging A and B reduces the other case to Euclid's figure.

**What the named squares rest on.** `BookI.Prop47.sq` describes squares by I.46, proves them parallelograms by I.28, and identifies each with the square on its side through I.4 and I.34. This explains the remaining extra citations, I.4, I.28 and I.46. Postulate 5 enters through I.29, I.30, I.32, I.34, I.46 and I.47. Heath also gives a proof using II.4 and II.7, following his note under II.7, and a proof by figures (pp. 394–395). These continue the geometrical algebra discussed under II.4, where Heath reports from an-Nairīzī that Heron proved II.2–II.10 by drawing one straight line.

**The modern way.** Let AC = CB = a and CD = b, with 0 < b < a. Then AD and DB are a + b and a − b, in one order or the other, and the identity is (a + b)² + (a − b)² = 2a² + 2b².
