**A figure made rectangular.** We begin with the four-sided figure A, represented in Lean by the two triangles of I.45. That proposition constructs a parallelogram with a given angle; `BookI.Prop11` supplies a right one. Then `Rectangle.of_right`, using I.29, makes the parallelogram a rectangle. Naming it as the rectangle contained by BE, ED uses `Rectangle.eq_rect`: rectangles with equal sides are equal, proved once from I.4 and I.34. If BE equals ED, the required square is already there.

**The greater straight line.** Simson says the choice of the greater side is unnecessary. Heath answers that "perhaps Euclid may have regarded it as conducive to clearness to have the points B, G, E, F in the same relative positions as the corresponding points … in the figure of II. 5 which he quotes in the proof" (vol. 1, p. 410). Our inner proof treats BE as greater; reversing the rectangle supplies the other case. Bisecting BF uses `BookI.Prop10`, without a bracket in Heath. Lean proves that G lies between B and E, using `OnRay.between_of_len_lt`, which brings in Common Notion 5. Thus E lies between G and F, as II.5 requires in Euclid's figure.

**Where the semicircle meets.** Producing BE uses Postulate 2; `OnRay.exists_len_eq` places EF equal to ED. Postulate 3 draws the circle with centre G. Lean uses the whole circle: E lies inside it by `inside_of_between`, and `line_circle_beyond` gives H beyond E from D, on the semicircle away from the rectangle. Postulate 1 names the straight line DE. The angle BEH beside the rectangle's right angle is right by `adjacent_eq_of_right`; G on the ray EB makes HEG that same right angle.

**The same square taken away.** II.5 gives the rectangle contained by BE, EF together with the square on GE. Definition 15 identifies GF with GH. Then `BookI.Prop47.sq` compares the squares on the right triangle's sides; this shared step brings in I.28 and I.46, as well as I.4 and I.34 when naming the squares. Common Notion 3 removes the square on GE. Since EF equals ED, the square on EH equals BD, and therefore A.

**The modern way.** With BE = a and ED = EF = b, GH = (a + b)/2 and GE = (a − b)/2, so EH² = ((a + b)/2)² − ((a − b)/2)² = ab. Thus EH is √(ab), the mean proportional, and Heath calls the construction "the equivalent of the extraction of the square root, or of the solution of the pure quadratic equation" (vol. 1, p. 410).
