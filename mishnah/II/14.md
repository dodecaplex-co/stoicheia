**The given rectilineal figure.** Euclid's A may have any number of sides; ours is the figure I.45 is proved for, two triangles on one diameter, and Lean names its corners A₁, A₂, A₃, A₄. I.45 makes a parallelogram in a given angle, so I.11 first gives it a right one, though Euclid brackets only I.45. One right angle makes it a rectangle (`Rectangle.of_right`, which brings in I.29), and `Rectangle.eq_rect` names it the rectangle BE, ED, since rectangles with equal sides are equal, as Lean proves once from I.4 and I.34.

**"Let BE be greater."** Simson pointed out that the words are not needed. Heath grants it, but "perhaps Euclid may have regarded it as conducive to clearness to have the points B, G, E, F in the same relative positions as the corresponding points … in the figure of II. 5 which he quotes in the proof" (vol. 1, p. 410). So Lean writes Euclid's case once, and reads the rectangle the other way round when ED is the greater. G, where BF is bisected (I.10, which Euclid uses without a bracket), lies between B and E because BE is more than half of BF (`OnRay.between_of_len_lt`, which brings in Common Notion 5); so E lies between G and F, as in II.5.

**The semicircle BHF.** Postulate 2 produces BE, and `OnRay.exists_len_eq` makes EF equal to ED. Postulate 3 gives Lean the whole circle with centre G, with E inside it (`inside_of_between`), so the straight line DE meets it at H beyond E (`line_circle_beyond`), on the far side of BF from the rectangle. The angle BEH is right, beside the rectangle's right angle DEB (`adjacent_eq_of_right`), and since G lies on EB it is the angle HEG.

**The same square taken away.** II.5 makes the rectangle BE, EF with the square on GE equal to the square on GF, that is on GH (Definition 15). By I.47 the squares on HE, EG make the square on GH; `BookI.Prop47.sq` describes the three squares (I.46) and names each by its side (I.4, I.28, I.34). Common Notion 3 takes away the square on GE, and the rectangle BE, EF that remains, which is BD since EF is equal to ED, is the square on EH.

**The modern way.** With BE = a and ED = EF = b, GH = (a + b)/2 and GE = (a − b)/2, so EH² = ((a + b)/2)² − ((a − b)/2)² = ab. Thus EH is √(ab), the mean proportional between BE and ED, and Heath calls Euclid's solution "the equivalent of the extraction of the square root, or of the solution of the pure quadratic equation" x² = ab (vol. 1, p. 410).
