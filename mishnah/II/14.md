**A figure made rectangular.** We begin with the four-sided figure A, represented in Lean by the two triangles of I.45. That proposition constructs a parallelogram with a given angle; `BookI.Prop11` supplies a right one. Then `Rectangle.of_right`, using I.29, makes the parallelogram a rectangle. Naming it as the rectangle contained by BE, ED uses `Rectangle.eq_rect`: rectangles with equal sides are equal, proved once from I.4 and I.34. If BE equals ED, the required square is already there.

**The greater straight line.** Simson says the choice of the greater side is unnecessary. Heath answers that "perhaps Euclid may have regarded it as conducive to clearness to have the points B, G, E, F in the same relative positions as the corresponding points … in the figure of II. 5 which he quotes in the proof" (vol. 1, p. 410). Lean writes Euclid's case once, with BE the greater, and reads the rectangle the other way round when ED is the greater. Bisecting BF uses `BookI.Prop10`, without a bracket in Heath. Lean proves that G lies between B and E, using `OnRay.between_of_len_lt`, which brings in Common Notion 5. Thus E lies between G and F, as in II.5's own figure.

**Where the semicircle meets.** Producing BE uses Postulate 2; `OnRay.exists_len_eq` places EF equal to ED. Postulate 3 draws the circle with centre G, and Lean uses the whole circle: E lies inside it by `inside_of_between`. Postulate 1 draws the straight line DE, and `line_circle_beyond` gives H on it beyond E from D, on the semicircle away from the rectangle. The angle BEH beside the rectangle's right angle is right by `adjacent_eq_of_right`; G on the ray EB makes HEG that same right angle.

**The same square taken away.** II.5 makes the rectangle contained by BE, EF together with the square on GE equal to the square on GF, and Definition 15 makes GF equal to GH. Then `BookI.Prop47.sq` makes the squares on HE, EG equal to the square on GH; this shared step describes the three squares (I.46) and names each as the square on its side (I.4, I.28, I.34). Common Notion 3 removes the square on GE. Since EF equals ED, the square on EH equals BD, and therefore A.

**The modern way.** With BE = a and ED = EF = b, GH = (a + b)/2 and GE = (a − b)/2, so EH² = ((a + b)/2)² − ((a − b)/2)² = ab. Thus EH is √(ab), the mean proportional, and Heath calls the construction "the equivalent of the extraction of the square root, or of the solution of the pure quadratic equation" (vol. 1, p. 410).
