**A rectangle that exceeds.** In II.5 the rectangle AD, DB, applied to AB, falls short by a square; here D lies beyond B, and it exceeds by one. Heath reads II.6 as a problem: to apply to a given straight line a rectangle equal to a given square and exceeding by a square figure (vol. 1, p. 386). He finds a problem of this kind already presupposed in Hippocrates' *Quadrature of lunes*, in the fifth century B.C. (vol. 1, pp. 386–387). Its equation has one positive root, and Heath writes, "From Euclid's point of view there would only be one solution in this case" (vol. 1, p. 388).

**Where the parallels meet.** Lean first puts B between C and D, from the setting-out. A point off CD, with no letter in Heath, chooses the side for the square: `exists_off_line` finds one with Postulates 1 and 3 and Definition I.15. `Parallelogram.figure` draws BG and KM, and `BookI.Prop31.parallelogram` draws AK and finds where it meets KM, at K. Both rest on I.30, that a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, as Heath reports Proclus arguing under I.31; so do `Parallelogram.complements`, `Parallelogram.about_diameter` and `BookI.Prop30.parallel`, which makes AK parallel to DF. `Parallel.between` puts L between K and M, so that AM is AL and CM together.

**Naming the pieces.** As in II.5, the gnomon NOP is three parallelograms, here CH, BM and HF, and its letters name an area, not points. That AM is the rectangle AD, DB, and CEFD and LG the squares on CD and BC, rests on rectangles with equal sides being equal, which Lean proves once from I.4 and I.34 (`Rectangle.eq_rect`, `Square.described`, `Square.eq_sq`). `Square.described` makes CEFD a parallelogram by I.28, and `Rectangle.of_right` makes AM a rectangle from its right angle at D, by I.29. BM and LG are squares by the argument Euclid gives inside II.4, `Square.about_diameter` (I.5, I.6, I.29, I.34), so DM is equal to DB; and I.34 makes HL equal to BC.

**The modern way.** Put C at 0, A at −h, B at h and D at d, with d > h > 0: then (d + h)(d − h) + h² = d². It is II.5's identity with CB and CD exchanged, as Heath shows under II.5.
