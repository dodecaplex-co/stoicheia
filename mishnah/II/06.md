**The rectangle beyond the end.** The square CEFD stands on CD, with D beyond B. Since AC equals CB, I.36 makes AL equal to CH; I.43 makes CH equal to HF. Adding CM replaces AL by HF and turns AM into the gnomon NOP. Adding LG completes the square. NOP labels three figures together, CH, BM and HF, as in the note on the gnomon quoted under II.5. Heath's remark there that II.6 expresses the same theorem is visible in this exchange of pieces.

**Where the parallels meet.** Lean first places B between C and D. An unlettered point from `exists_off_line` chooses a side for the square, using Postulates 1 and 3, Definition 15 and System E's rules. `Parallelogram.figure` draws the parallels, and `BookI.Prop31.parallelogram` finds K. Both bring in I.30: the meeting follows the argument of Proclus reported by Heath under I.31. `Parallel.between` puts L between K and M, allowing AM to be cut at CL. I.30 also enters through `BookI.Prop30.parallel`, which makes AK parallel to DF, and through `Parallelogram.complements` and `Parallelogram.about_diameter`, which identify the four pieces of the square.

**Naming the pieces by their sides.** `Rectangle.eq_rect`, `Square.eq_sq` and `Square.described` identify AM, LG and CEFD with the rectangle and squares in the conclusion. They bring in I.4 and I.34, which prove that rectangles contained by equal straight lines are equal. `Square.described` also uses I.28 to make the square a parallelogram. `Rectangle.of_right` uses I.29 to make AM a rectangle from its right angle at D. The smaller figures BM and LG are squares by `Square.about_diameter`, using I.5, I.6, I.29 and I.34, the argument Euclid gives in II.4. Finally I.34 gives HL equal to BC. These are the certificate's citations beyond Euclid's brackets.

**A rectangle exceeding by a square.** Heath reads the construction as applying to AB a rectangle equal to a given square and exceeding AB by a square figure (vol. 1, p. 386). The added straight line BD is the unknown to be constructed. He writes, "From Euclid's point of view there would only be one solution in this case" (vol. 1, p. 388). The condition that D lies beyond B selects the positive length.

**The modern way.** Following the geometrical algebra discussed under II.4, put C at 0, A at −h, B at h and D at d > h > 0: then (d + h)(d − h) + h² = d². This is II.5's identity with the roles of CB and CD exchanged.
