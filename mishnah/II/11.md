**Two subtractions.** II.6 and I.47 each give something equal to the square on EB. Take away the square on AE, and the rectangle CF, FA is left equal to the square on AB; take away AK, common to FK and AD, and FH is left equal to HD. Both are Common Notion 3. Heath observes: "The construction shows that CF is also divided at A in the manner described in the enunciation, since the rectangle CF, FA is equal to the square on CA" (vol. 1, p. 403). The proof has that equality before its second subtraction, for CA is equal to AB.

**Where F and H fall.** Euclid's bisection has no bracket: it is I.10. His figure shows F beyond A and H within AB, and Lean needs reasons. The angle ABE is less than the right angle BAE (I.17), so EB is greater than EA (I.19), and F falls beyond A. The angle FAB, beside the right angle CAB, is right too, and `onRay_of_angle_eq` puts H on the ray AB: on a given side, a given angle fixes its ray. EB is less than EA and AB together (I.20); EF is equal to EB, so AF is less than AB, and H lies within AB.

**The crossing and the names.** K comes from `BookI.Prop30.meet`: GH meets AB at H, so it meets CD, the parallel to AB, as Heath reports Proclus drawing from I.30 under I.31. `BookI.Prop30.parallel` gives the other parallels, and `Parallel.between` carries the order of the points across them. Book Zero's `quadArea_add_of_parallelograms` shows FK made up of FH and AK, and AD of AK and HD. Naming the squares and rectangles drawn brings in I.4, I.28 and I.34, through `Square.described`, `Square.eq_sq` and `Rectangle.eq_rect`, as inside `BookI.Prop47.sq`; and `Rectangle.of_right` brings in I.29, since a parallelogram with one right angle has four.

**The circle behind the construction.** "Let EF be made equal to BE" is `OnRay.exists_len_eq`, which describes a circle about E (Postulate 3) and reads off its radius (Definition 15). Euclid's figure has no circle, but Heath finds one in Simson's solution of the more general equation, based on II.6, which applied here "describes a circle with EB as radius cutting EA produced in F" (vol. 1, p. 403). The first square stands on a side of AB found by `exists_off_line`, the second on B's side of AF. The certificate records Postulates 1–3 and Definition 15 directly; Postulate 5 comes in through I.29, I.30, I.34, I.46, I.47 and II.6.

**The modern way.** With AB = a and AH = x, the statement is a(a − x) = x², or x² + ax = a². Since EB = a√5/2, AH = AF = EF − EA = a(√5 − 1)/2, the positive root; and AB is to AH as AH is to HB, the golden section.
