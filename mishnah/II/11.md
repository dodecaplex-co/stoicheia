**Two subtractions.** Euclid describes the square on AB, bisects AC, and makes EF equal to EB. II.6 and I.47 then give two expressions for the square on EB. Subtracting the square on AE leaves the rectangle contained by CF, FA equal to the square on AB; subtracting their common rectangle AK leaves FH equal to HD. Both cancellations use Common Notion 3. Heath observes: "The construction shows that CF is also divided at A in the manner described in the enunciation, since the rectangle CF, FA is equal to the square on CA" (vol. 1, p. 403). The proof reaches this equality before its second subtraction, since CA equals AB.

**Where F and H fall.** The unbracketed bisection uses I.10. Lean must also establish the order of the points. I.17 makes the angle ABE less than the right angle BAE; I.19 makes EB greater than EA, placing F beyond A. The adjacent angle FAB is right, and `onRay_of_angle_eq`, uniqueness of a ray making a given angle on a given side, puts H on the ray AB. This step uses Common Notion 5. I.20 makes EB less than EA and AB together; since EF equals EB, AF is less than AB, so H lies within AB.

**The crossing and the names.** `BookI.Prop30.meet` finds K where GH meets CD: GH meets AB at H, so it meets CD, the parallel to AB, a consequence of I.30 that Heath reports Proclus drawing under I.31. `BookI.Prop30.parallel` supplies the remaining parallels, and `Parallel.between` carries the order of the points across them. Book Zero's `quadArea_add_of_parallelograms` justifies removing AK. Naming the drawn squares and rectangles uses `Square.described`, `Square.eq_sq` and `Rectangle.eq_rect`, bringing in I.4, I.28 and I.34; `BookI.Prop47.sq` uses the same naming steps. `Rectangle.of_right` brings in I.29 to make every angle right from one right angle in a parallelogram.

**The circle behind the construction.** Making EF equal to EB uses `OnRay.exists_len_eq`, which draws a circle about E by Postulate 3 and reads its radius by Definition 15. The circle is not drawn in Euclid's figure, but Heath finds it in Simson's solution of the more general equation, based on II.6: applied here, it "describes a circle with EB as radius cutting EA produced in F" (vol. 1, p. 403). An unlettered point off AB supplies a side for the first square through `exists_off_line`; the second square stands on B's side of AF. The certificate records Postulates 1–3 and Definition 15 directly; Postulate 5 enters through I.29, I.30, I.34, I.46, I.47 and II.6.

**The modern way.** With AB = a and AH = x, the statement is a(a − x) = x², or x² + ax = a². Since EB = a√5/2, we obtain AH = AF = EF − EA = a(√5 − 1)/2, the positive root.
