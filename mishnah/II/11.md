**A cut for the pentagon.** Euclid makes this cut for Book IV, and Heath reads history in it: "As the solution of this problem is necessary to that of inscribing a regular pentagon in a circle (Eucl. IV. 10, 11), we must necessarily conclude that it was solved by the Pythagoreans, or, in other words, that they discovered the geometrical solution of the quadratic equation" a(a − x) = x² (vol. 1, p. 403). Even Heron, who in an-Nairīzī's account did without figures from II.2 to II.10, needed one here: a problem, he says, requires an operation (Heath, vol. 1, p. 373).

**"Extreme and mean ratio."** Our title is Book VI's name for this cut: a straight line is cut in extreme and mean ratio when the whole is to the greater segment as the greater is to the less (Def. VI.3). Book II, with no ratios yet, says the same with areas. Heath sees the construction cut two lines so, CF at A as well as AB at H, since the rectangle CF, FA is equal to the square on CA. And he ends his note: "The problem in II. 11 reappears in VI. 30 in the form of *cutting a given straight line in extreme and mean ratio*" (vol. 1, p. 403).

**A circle in hiding.** "Let EF be made equal to BE" is a circle about E, and our proof draws it: Postulate 3 describes the circle, and Definition I.15 makes EF equal to its radius EB (`OnRay.exists_len_eq`). Euclid's figure has no circle, but Heath finds one in Robert Simson's construction for x² + ax = b², built on II.6, which applied here "describes a circle with EB as radius cutting EA produced in F" (vol. 1, p. 403).

**Beyond A, within AB.** So Euclid's figure puts F and H, and our proof gives each its reason. The angle ABE is less than the right angle BAE (I.17), so EB is greater than EA (I.19), and F falls beyond A. The square FH stands on B's side of AF, and the angle FAB, beside the right angle CAB, is right too, so `onRay_of_angle_eq` puts H on the ray AB. EB is less than EA and AB together (I.20); EF is equal to EB, so AF is less than AB, and H lies within AB.

**The modern way.** With AB = a and AH = x, the statement is Heath's equation a(a − x) = x², or x² + ax = a². Since EB = a√5/2, AH = AF = EF − EA = a(√5 − 1)/2, the positive root; and AB is to AH as AH is to HB, the golden section.
