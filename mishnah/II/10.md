**Where the straight lines meet.** Euclid draws CE at right angles to AB and equal to AC, then completes the parallelogram ECDF. The angles CEF, EFD make two right angles by I.29. EB lies within CEF, so FEB and EFD together are less than two right angles by Common Notion 5. Postulate 5 now gives the meeting G; I.29, I.30, I.32, I.34, I.46 and I.47 rest on it too. This is the only postulate Book II brackets, printed here as "[I. Post. 5]". Lean then proves that B lies between E and G, and D between F and G, using Book Zero's `Between.of_inside` and `Between.of_sameSide`.

**What the figure asks us to say.** I.11 needs a side on which to draw CE; `exists_off_line` supplies it. I.3 needs a greater straight line from which to cut off one equal to AC, so `produce_longer` first extends the perpendicular far enough. A half right angle is written as an angle added to itself equalling a right angle. Common Notion 3 cancels a right angle from I.32's sum. I.5 and I.32 give these halves at A, E and B; I.15 carries the angle at B across the crossing. I.29 and I.34, with I.32 and I.6, then give BD equal to GD and GF equal to EF. An unlettered point beyond G supplies the extension required by I.32 in triangle EFG.

**The shared steps.** `BookI.Prop31.parallelogram` finds F where the parallel through D meets EF, bringing in I.30 through the argument of Proclus that Heath reports under I.31. The four uses of `BookI.Prop47.sq` compare the squares on EA, EG and AG, with AG compared in two triangles. This step describes the squares by I.46 and makes them parallelograms by I.28; I.4 and I.34 identify each drawn square with the square on its side. These explain the certificate's citations beyond Euclid's brackets. The squares remain undrawn here, as Heath discusses under II.9.

**The Pythagorean connection.** On this proposition's page Heath reports Proclus using II.10 to explain the rule for side- and diagonal-numbers. He takes this as confirmation "that the whole of the substance of that Book was Pythagorean" (vol. 1, p. 400). That inference rests on the part the gnomon and I.47 play in Book II, whose geometrical algebra is discussed under II.4.

**The modern way.** Let AC = CB = a and BD = b; then (2a + b)² + b² = 2a² + 2(a + b)². With c = CD = a + b, this is II.9's identity (c + a)² + (c − a)² = 2a² + 2c².
