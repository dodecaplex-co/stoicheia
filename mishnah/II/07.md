**The square counted twice.** I.43 makes the complements AG and GE equal. Adding CF to each makes AF equal to CE. Together these are the gnomon KLM and another copy of CF: the square at B is counted twice. Since BF equals BC, AF is the rectangle contained by AB, BC. Adding DG then completes ADEB while leaving the extra CF. The gnomon is a sum of three figures in Lean, following Definition II.2 and Heath's note there (vol. 1, p. 371), discussed under II.5. K, L, M label this sum, not points.

**The figure drawn.** Euclid's short instruction hides the parallels and their meeting points. `Parallelogram.figure` supplies them from I.31 and I.30, using the meeting principle that Heath reports Proclus deriving under I.31. It puts G between B and D and each other point between the ends of its side. H and N appear in Heath's figure although his proof never names them. Before I.46, `exists_off_line` supplies an unlettered point to choose the square's side, using Postulates 1 and 3. `Parallelogram.complements` and `Parallelogram.about_diameter` also use I.30 to establish the pieces and their sum.

**Recognising the pieces.** `Square.about_diameter` makes CF and DG squares, using I.5, I.6, I.29 and I.34. This is the result proved inside II.4, whose appended porism Heath calls interpolated in his note there (vol. 1, p. 381). I.34 also makes GH equal to CA, identifying DG as the square on AC. `Square.described` uses I.28 to make ADEB a parallelogram; `Rectangle.of_right` uses I.29 to make AF a rectangle. Naming a drawn rectangle by its sides rests on rectangles with equal sides being equal: `Rectangle.eq_rect` and `Square.eq_sq` use I.4 and I.34. These are the citations beyond Euclid's two brackets.

**The difference of two straight lines.** Heath reads AC as the difference between AB and BC: "*the square on the difference of two straight lines is equal to the sum of the squares on the straight lines diminished by twice the rectangle contained by them*" (vol. 1, p. 389). This connects II.7 with his account of geometrical algebra before the propositions, discussed under II.4 (pp. 372–374). II.13 uses II.7 with the pieces made by a perpendicular in a triangle.

**The modern way.** With AB = a and BC = b, so that AC = a − b, the identity is a² + b² = 2ab + (a − b)², or, in Heath's rearrangement, (a − b)² = a² + b² − 2ab.
