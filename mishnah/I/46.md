**From one side.** Heath distinguishes constructing a triangle from describing a square on a straight line: "The *triangle* (or *angle*) is, so to say, pieced together, while the describing of a square on a given straight line is the making of a figure 'from' *one* side, and corresponds to the multiplication of the number representing the side by itself" (vol. 1, p. 348). Euclid builds ADEB from AB, then proves it equilateral and right-angled, the two requirements of Definition 22.

**Choosing the side.** Our statement names a point P off AB to choose the side on which the square is described, so that I.48 can describe outward the squares I.47 asks for. I.11 draws AC on that side. Book Zero's `OnRay.exists_len_eq` makes AD equal to AB by a circle about A with distance AB, using Postulate 3. It puts D on the ray from A through C; Heath draws C beyond D, but the proof leaves their order open. `SameSideOf.of_onRay` keeps D on the chosen side, and `Parallelogram.sameSideOf` puts E there too. P chooses a side without belonging to Heath's figure.

**Where the parallels meet.** I.31 draws DE parallel to AB and BE parallel to AD. Euclid names E without proving that the straight lines meet. A straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`), as Heath reports Proclus drawing it under I.31. This accounts for the certificate's citation beyond Euclid's brackets. I.34 gives the equal sides; I.29 gives two right angles together, and `CommonNotion3` takes away BAD to leave ADE right. I.34 then gives the opposite angles. The proof rests on Postulate 5 through I.29 and I.30, and through I.34's use of I.29.

**Equal squares and equal sides.** Heath reports Proclus proving that squares described on equal straight lines are equal, and conversely (vol. 1, pp. 348–349). These are our `Prop46.square_eq` and `Prop46.side_eq`. The former cuts each square into two triangles and uses I.4, after I.28 makes each square a parallelogram. The converse takes another road because a figure cannot be moved: I.3 cuts off from the greater side a straight line equal to the less, I.46 describes a square inside the larger, and I.34 compares the halves. Equality would make a whole equal to its part.

**The modern way.** Regard the plane as the complex numbers: D = A + i(B − A) and E = B + i(B − A) give the square ADEB. Replacing i by −i gives the square on the other side; P chooses between them.
