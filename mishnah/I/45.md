**Two triangles make the given figure.** Euclid joins DB, constructs FH equal to ABD by I.42, and applies GM equal to DBC to GH by I.44. We therefore state the given area as the sum of those two triangles. Book Zero's `quadArea` measures a four-sided figure along the diameter joining its first and third corners; applied to ABCD it would cut along AC. That cut need not divide a figure re-entrant at B or D into its two parts. Naming the triangles preserves Euclid's cut.

**H falls on D.** Our I.42 builds on the triangle's own base: BD is bisected at K in its construction, H falls on D, and F lies on A's side of BD. The Lean writes D wherever Euclid writes H, and the finished KFLM overlaps ABCD in our figure. Lean also chooses which side GM goes on. The construction `exists_oppositeSidesOf` supplies an unlettered point across GH from K, and I.44 places M on that side. Thus KH and HM are on opposite sides of GH when I.14 puts them on one straight line. The second use of I.14 puts F, G, L in order too.

**Joining the two parallelograms.** I.34 equates the opposite sides of each piece. Common Notion 1 equates KF and ML, and `Prop30.parallel` makes them parallel through GH. Lean proves their straight lines different before applying I.30, and proves F and L on one side of KM, so that KM and FL join KF and ML in the same directions, as I.33 asks. Book Zero's `quadArea_add_of_parallelograms` then adds the pieces; `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` lets their areas be named from another corner. The certificate cites exactly Euclid's bracketed propositions: I.14, I.29, I.30, I.33, I.34, I.42 and I.44. Postulate 5 comes in through I.29 and I.30, here and inside I.42 and I.44.

**Transformation of areas.** Heath describes what these constructions together achieve: "We have now learnt how to represent any rectilineal area, which can of course be resolved into triangles, by a single parallelogram having one side equal to any given straight line and one angle equal to any given rectilineal angle" (vol. 1, p. 347). Further triangles can be attached in the same way, and I.44 supplies the prescribed side.

**The modern way.** In coordinates the given area is S = ½|(B − A) × (D − A)| + ½|(B − D) × (C − D)|, where × is the planar cross product, the determinant of the two vectors. A parallelogram with sides a and b containing the angle E has area ab sin E, so b = S/(a sin E): applying an area is division.
