**Two triangles make the figure.** Euclid joins DB and deals with the figure as two triangles, ABD by I.42 and DBC by I.44. So our statement gives the figure as the sum of those two triangles, cut where Euclid cuts it. Our `quadArea` would read ABCD along the other diameter, AC, which falls outside the figure when it is re-entrant at B or D.

**H falls on D.** Our I.42, like Euclid's, builds its parallelogram on half of the triangle's own base, so KH lies along BD and H falls on D, with F on A's side of BD. The Lean writes D wherever Euclid writes H, and in our figure KFLM overlaps ABCD. Lean must also say which side of GH the parallelogram GM goes on: I.44 is given a point across GH from K, with no letter in Heath. Then KH and HM are "not lying on the same side", as I.14 asks, and the same choice later puts FG in a straight line with GL.

**Joining the two parallelograms.** Before I.30 makes KF and ML parallel (`Prop30.parallel`), Lean must know that they are not one straight line, and before I.33, that F and L lie on one side of KM, so that KM and FL join them in the same directions. Book Zero's `quadArea_add_of_parallelograms` then puts the two pieces together. The certificate cites exactly the propositions Euclid brackets, and Postulate 5 comes in through each of them from I.29 on.

**Transformation of areas.** Heath takes stock: "We have now learnt how to represent any rectilineal area, which can of course be resolved into triangles, by a single parallelogram having one side equal to any given straight line and one angle equal to any given rectilineal angle" (vol. 1, p. 347). A figure of more sides is cut into more triangles, and each is applied by I.44 to the side the last one left, as DBC is applied to GH.

**The modern way.** In coordinates the figure's area is ½|(B − A) × (D − A)| + ½|(B − D) × (C − D)|, where u × v is the determinant of u and v. A parallelogram with sides a and b containing the angle E has area ab sin E, so for a given a the side b is that area divided by a sin E: applying an area, as Heath says, is division.
