**The halves of equal things.** Euclid completes two parallelograms on BC, makes them equal by I.35, and halves each by I.34. The sentence that takes their halves stands in brackets, and Heath gives the reason: "Here and in the next proposition Heiberg brackets the words 'But the halves of equal things are equal to one another' on the ground that, since the *Common Notion* which asserted this fact was interpolated at a very early date (before the time of Theon), it is probable that the words here were interpolated at the same time" (vol. 1, p. 332).

**The little argument.** Tannery, as Heath reports in his notes on the Common Notions, observes that the halves are no inference from Common Notion 3, as Heiberg had it: they would need a *reductio ad absurdum* through Common Notion 2. Heath adds that even then "a little argument would be necessary in addition to the reference to *Common Notion* 2" (vol. 1, p. 224). Book Zero gives it in `Magnitude.eq_of_add_self_eq`: were one half less than the other, its double would be less too (`Magnitude.add_self_lt_add_self`), so equal doubles have equal halves. Bracketed or not, the step is needed.

**Where the parallels meet.** Euclid's E is where BE, drawn through B parallel to CA, meets AD produced, and F is found likewise on CF. That they meet at all comes from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`, within `Prop31.parallelogram`): AD meets CA at A, so it meets BE, the parallel to CA, as Heath reports Proclus drawing it under I.31. So the certificate cites I.30 besides Euclid's I.31, I.35 and I.34; Postulate 5 comes in through I.29, by way of I.30, I.34 and I.35.

**AD produced, and the diameter AB.** Our statement reads "in the same parallels" as two whole straight lines, so A and D may be one point. The "AD" that Euclid produces is then the parallel through the vertices, and E and F fall on it wherever they may, for I.35 covers every arrangement. Our `quadArea`, the area of a four-sided figure, adds the two triangles on the diameter from its first corner to its third: EC in EBCA. For Euclid's AB, `Parallelogram.rotate` names the figure from B, `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` keeps its area, and `Prop34.bisect` makes it twice ABC. DBCF already has DC.

**The modern way.** Put D = A + t(C − B) for a real number t. Replacing A by D changes the cross product (B − A) × (C − A), whose absolute value is twice the triangle's area, by t times (C − B) × (C − B), which is zero. So sliding the vertex along the parallel leaves the area alone.
