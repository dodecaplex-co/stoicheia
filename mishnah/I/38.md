**Two bases in place of one.** Euclid draws BG parallel to CA and FH parallel to DE, and completes the parallelograms GBCA and DEFH. They are on the equal bases BC, EF and in the same parallels, so I.36 makes them equal, and the diameters AB and DF halve them (I.34, `Prop34.bisect`). Heiberg brackets the sentence on halves here as well, as Heath notes under I.37, and Book Zero's `Magnitude.eq_of_add_self_eq` again takes the step.

**Where the parallels meet.** As in I.37, Euclid's G and H are where the parallels he draws meet AD produced. That they meet at all comes from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`, within `Prop31.parallelogram`): AD meets CA at A, so it meets BG, the parallel to CA; and it meets DE at D, so it meets FH. So the certificate cites I.30 besides Euclid's I.31, I.36 and I.34, and Postulate 5 comes in through I.29, by way of I.30, I.34 and I.36.

**What Lean made us say.** "In the same parallels" is two parallel straight lines, one through A and D, one through both bases. The two vertices may be one point, as when I.42 cites this proposition for two triangles with one vertex; so the "AD" that Euclid produces to G and H is the parallel through the vertices itself. The bases may overlap or share an end, and I.36 covers these arrangements too. Our `quadArea` measures GBCA by its diameter GC, so `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` names it from B to reach Euclid's AB, and `area_cba` turns his FED back into DEF.

**"Assumed in order to prove VI. 1."** Proclus thought that Euclid had given one proof of all four of I.35–I.38 in VI.1, where triangles and parallelograms of the same altitude are to one another as their bases. Heath answers: "It is true that VI. 1 generalises these propositions, but it must be observed that it does not prove the propositions themselves, as Proclus seems to imply; they are in fact assumed in order to prove VI. 1" (vol. 1, p. 334). The equalities come first, and Book VI's ratios are built on them.

**The modern way.** Put BF on the x-axis and AD on the straight line y = h, with h positive: the triangles have areas ½·BC·h and ½·EF·h, equal because BC = EF. With the height fixed the area goes with the base, as VI.1 says.
