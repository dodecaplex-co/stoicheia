**I.13 turned round.** Euclid proves the converse of I.13 by *reductio ad absurdum*, as in I.6. He supposes that BD is not in a straight line with CB, produces CB to E, and finds the angle ABE equal to the angle ABD, "the less to the greater: which is impossible." In Lean the argument is a sub-proof under the supposition that D is off the ray from B through E; `Classical.byContradiction` turns its failure round, and `Prop14.straight` finishes. Producing CB is `produce` in Book Zero, so the proof uses Postulates 1 and 2 where Euclid gives no bracket (I.13 brings both in as well).

**"Not lying on the same side."** Heath reports Proclus' reason for the condition: "two straight lines on the *same* side of another straight line and meeting it in one and the same point may make with one and the same portion of the straight line terminated at the point two angles which are together equal to two right angles, in which case however the two straight lines would not be in a straight line with one another" (vol. 1, p. 277). In Lean it is the hypothesis `OppositeSidesOf C D A B`, used once: E and D, each on the other side of AB from C, are on the same side as each other (`OppositeSidesOf.of_between`, `OppositeSidesOf.sameSideOf`). On that side just one ray from B makes a given angle with BA (`onRay_of_angle_eq`), so D would lie along BE, whether BE falls within the angle ABD, as Euclid draws it, or BD within ABE.

**Postulate 4 and Common Notion 1.** Heath: "Todhunter points out that for the inference in this line Post. 4, that all right angles are equal, is necessary as well as the Common Notion that things which are equal to the same thing (or rather, here, to *equal things*) are equal. A similar remark applies to steps in the proofs of I. 15 and I. 28" (vol. 1, p. 277). In Lean, Postulate 4 has done its work before I.14 begins: every right angle is the one magnitude `∟`, and I.13 hands its two back as `∟ + ∟`. So the step itself is `CommonNotion1`, and the subtraction that follows is `CommonNotion3`.

**The modern way.** Put B at 0 and A at 1 in the complex plane, and C = r e^{iθ} above the real axis. Then D, below it and making the angle π − θ with BA, is s e^{−i(π − θ)} = −s e^{iθ}, so B lies between C and D. Were D above the axis, it would be −s e^{−iθ}, in general not in a straight line with CB at all: that is Proclus' warning.
