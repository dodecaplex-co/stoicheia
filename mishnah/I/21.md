**Drawn through to E.** "For let BD be drawn through to E," says Euclid, and from then on E is a point of AC. Two things are read off the figure in that one sentence: that BD, produced, reaches AC at all, and that it gets there beyond D, so that D lies between B and E. The first is the crossbar theorem, proved once in Book Zero for sentences like this one. D lies within the angle ABC, on C's side of BA and on A's side of BC, so [`crossbar`](../../Elements/Figure/Angles.lean) makes the ray from B through D cross AC at a point E between A and C. The second is `Prop21.through`. B and D are on one side of AC, so E, which is on AC, cannot lie between them (System E, Pasch 3); B is not between D and E either, since E is on the ray from B through D; and of three points on a straight line one is between the other two, so it is D.

**"Meeting within the triangle."** Heath notes that "the word 'meeting' is not in the Greek … but it is necessary in English to indicate that they meet" (vol. 1, p. 289). In Lean the meeting is the point D itself, and "within the triangle" is three facts of side (`InsideTriangle`): D is on A's side of BC, on B's side of CA, and on C's side of AB. The proof needs all three. Two of them put D within the angle ABC, so that BD can be drawn through to AC; the third keeps E beyond D; and the first is used once more, to show that C is off the straight line BE.

**What else Lean made us say.** Before I.20 and I.16 can be cited, ABE and CED have to be triangles. B is off AC because ABC is a triangle. C is off BE because otherwise BE would be BC, and D, which is on A's side of BC, would lie on it (`Triangle.of_line`, `Triangle.off_line`). "Let EC be added to each" is System E's law that adding the same thing keeps "less than" (`add_lt_add_right`). "BA, AC are greater than BE, EC" needs AE and EC to make up AC, since E lies between A and C (Diagram-segment 1), and in the same way ED and DB make up EB. "Much greater" is "less than" used twice (`lt_trans`). For the angles, I.16 names them the other way round, which `angle_symm` corrects. The angle CED is the angle CEB because D lies on the ray from E through B, and BAE is BAC because E lies on the ray from A through C (`angle_onRay_right`). These are the rules of Jeremy Avigad, Edward Dean and John Mumma's System E, and LeanEuclid's careful Lean version of it, by Logan Murphy, Kaiyu Yang, Jialiang Sun, Zhaoyu Li, Anima Anandkumar and Xujie Si, was a steady guide. The proof cites only the statements of I.20 and I.16, so it did not have to wait for I.20 to be proved.

**From the ends of the side.** Heath: "The editors generally call attention to the fact that the lines drawn within the triangle in this proposition must be drawn, as the enunciation says, from the ends of the side; otherwise it is not necessary that their sum should be less than that of the remaining sides of the triangle" (vol. 1, p. 290). Proclus gives an example. In a right-angled triangle ABC, take D on BC, join DA, cut off DE from it equal to AB, bisect AE at F and join FC. "Then shall CF, FD be together greater than CA, AB." For CF, FE are equal to CF, FA, which are greater than CA by I.20, and ED is equal to AB. Pappus went much further, following "the so-called paradoxes" of one Erycinus (vol. 1, p. 290), "of whom nothing more is known" (vol. 1, p. 27). Among them: "In any triangle, except an equilateral triangle or an isosceles triangle with base less than one of the other sides, it is possible to construct on the base and within the triangle two straight lines the sum of which is equal to the sum of the other two sides of the triangle", and "the lines may be so constructed that their sum will bear to the sum of the two sides of the triangle any ratio less than 2 : 1" (vol. 1, p. 290). In Lean the ends of the side are in the statement: the two straight lines start from B and C themselves. The proof depends on this, because it is BD, from the end B, that is drawn through to AC.

**A use for it.** Heath records that "it is after I. 21 that (as remarked by De Morgan) the important proposition about the perpendicular and obliques drawn from a point to a straight line of unlimited length is best introduced" (vol. 1, p. 291). To show that of two obliques AE, AF the one whose foot is further from the perpendicular AD is the longer, Heath produces AD to A′, making A′D equal to AD. Then E is "a point within the triangle AFA′", and by I.21 "AF, FA′ are together greater than AE, EA′", that is, twice AF is greater than twice AE (vol. 1, p. 292).

**The modern way.** In coordinates, the points P with |PB| + |PC| = |AB| + |AC| make an ellipse with foci B and C passing through A. The triangle lies inside it, since the inside of an ellipse is convex, and inside the ellipse the sum is less. Likewise the points on A's side of BC that see BC under the angle BAC make an arc of the circle through A, B and C. The triangle lies inside that circle, and from inside the circle BC is seen under a wider angle.
