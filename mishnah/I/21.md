**Drawn through to E.** Euclid writes "let BD be drawn through to E", and from then on E is a point of AC. Two things are read off the figure here: that BD, produced, reaches AC at all, and that it gets there beyond D. The first is Book Zero's `crossbar`, since D lies within the angle ABC. The second is `Prop21.through`. B and D are on one side of AC, so E, which is on AC, is not between them (System E, Pasch 3); nor is B between D and E, since E is on the ray from B through D; so D lies between B and E.

**"Meeting within the triangle."** Heath notes that "the word 'meeting' is not in the Greek … but it is necessary in English to indicate that they meet" (vol. 1, p. 289). In Lean the meeting is the point D, and "within the triangle" is three facts of side (`InsideTriangle`): D is on A's side of BC, on B's side of CA, and on C's side of AB. The proof needs all three. A's side of BC and C's side of AB put D within the angle ABC, so that BD can be drawn through; B's side of CA keeps E beyond D; and A's side of BC keeps C off the straight line BE, so that CED is a triangle for I.20 and I.16 (`Triangle.of_line`, `Triangle.off_line`). The rest is adding, with AE and EC making up AC (System E, Diagram-segment 1), and reading angles off rays (`angle_onRay_right`).

**From the ends of the side.** Heath: "The editors generally call attention to the fact that the lines drawn within the triangle in this proposition must be drawn, as the enunciation says, from the ends of the side; otherwise it is not necessary that their sum should be less than that of the remaining sides of the triangle" (vol. 1, p. 290). Pappus, from "the so-called paradoxes" of one Erycinus, shows that two straight lines on the base, within the triangle, may together bear to the other two sides any ratio less than 2 : 1 (vol. 1, p. 290). In Lean the ends of the side are in the statement, and the proof depends on it: it is BD, from the end B, that is drawn through to AC.

**The modern way.** The points P with PB + PC equal to AB + AC make an ellipse with foci B and C through A, and the triangle lies within it, where the sum is less. Likewise the points on A's side of BC that see BC under the angle BAC make an arc of the circle through A, B and C, and from within that circle, on A's side of BC, as D is, BC is seen under a wider angle.
