**Applying an area.** I.42 supplies an area and an angle; I.44 fixes a side as well. Heath judges that this proposition "will always remain one of the most impressive in all geometry" (vol. 1, p. 342). BF becomes a complement in the larger parallelogram HLKF, whose diameter passes through B. I.43 makes the other complement LB equal to BF, and I.15 transfers the given angle to ABM. Proclus gives an example in numbers: an area of twelve feet, applied in a right angle to a straight line of four feet, has a breadth of three (Heath, vol. 1, p. 343).

**"Let it be placed."** I.42 constructs its parallelogram on the triangle's own base. `Prop44.place` rebuilds an equal one at B: I.2 copies the sides, I.23 copies the angle, I.31 draws parallels, `Prop30.meet` finds where they meet, and I.4 with I.34 makes the areas equal. The point P of our statement chooses the side of AB on which the answer stands. The placement takes G on the other side, so M ends on P's side. P and the unnamed construction points receive no letters in the figure.

**Where the straight lines meet.** `Prop30.meet` supplies H, L and M: a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, by I.30, as Heath reports Proclus drawing it under I.31. `Prop30.parallel` justifies "parallel to either": AH is parallel to EF, and KL to AB. Lean also needs the order of the points. `Between.of_sameSide` carries A, B, E across BG to H, G, F, and then supplies the five further orders needed for the diameter and its complements.

**The fifth postulate.** I.29 makes AHF and HFE together two right angles. `angle_add_of_within` splits AHF at HB; Common Notion 5 makes BHG and GFE together less than two right angles. `Postulate5` then gives K on HB and FE, on B's side of FG. The proof itself uses Postulates 1, 2, 3 and 5 and Definition 15: Postulate 1 joins HB, and `Prop44.place` uses Postulate 2 to produce AB and Postulate 3 with Definition 15 to set copied lengths on rays. Through the cited propositions come Postulate 4 (I.15 brings it in) and superposition (I.4, and I.8 through I.23); Postulate 5 comes in again through I.29 and I.30.

**The modern way.** A parallelogram with sides a and b in the angle θ has area ab sin θ, so an area S applied to a has b = S/(a sin θ). In oblique axes along BE and BM, with B at the origin, H = (−AB, −BG) and K = (BE, BM) lie on one straight line through B, so BM = BE·BG/AB, and AB·BM = BE·BG.
