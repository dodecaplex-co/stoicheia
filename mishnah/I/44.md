**Applying an area.** I.42 supplies an area and an angle; I.44 fixes a side as well. Heath calls the result "one of the most impressive in all geometry" (vol. 1, p. 342). BF becomes a complement in the larger parallelogram HLKF, whose diameter passes through B. I.43 makes the other complement LB equal to BF, and I.15 transfers the given angle to ABM.

**"Let it be placed."** I.42 constructs its parallelogram on the triangle's own base. `Prop44.place` rebuilds an equal one at B: I.2 copies the sides, I.23 copies the angle, I.31 draws parallels, `Prop30.meet` finds their meeting point, and I.4 with I.34 proves equality of area. The input P chooses the side of AB on which the answer stands. The placement takes G on the other side, so M ends on P's side. P and the unnamed construction points receive no letters in the figure.

**Where the straight lines meet.** `Prop30.meet` supplies H, L and M, as Heath reports Proclus deriving this intersection property from I.30 under I.31. `Prop30.parallel` justifies "parallel to either": AH is parallel to EF, and KL to AB. Lean also needs the order of the points. `Between.of_sameSide` carries A, B, E across BG to H, G, F, and then supplies the five further orders needed for the diameter and its complements.

**The fifth postulate.** I.29 makes AHF and HFE together two right angles. `angle_add_of_within` splits AHF at HB; Common Notion 5 makes BHG and GFE together less than two right angles. `Postulate5` then gives K on HB and FE, on B's side of FG. The direct certificate includes Postulates 1, 2, 3 and 5, Definition 15, and tacit rules for incidence, order, circles, sides, lengths, angles and areas: Postulate 1 joins HB, while `Prop44.place` uses Postulate 2 to produce AB and Postulate 3 with Definition 15 to set copied lengths on rays. Postulate 5 also comes through I.29 and I.30; I.4 supplies superposition, and I.15 supplies Postulate 4.

**The modern way.** For area S, side a and angle θ, the required breadth is S/(a sin θ). In unit oblique axes along BE and BM with origin B, H = (−AB, −BG) and K = (BE, BM) lie on one straight line through B, giving BM = BE·BG/AB. Proclus' rectangular example applies twelve square feet to four feet and obtains a breadth of three feet (Heath, vol. 1, p. 343).
