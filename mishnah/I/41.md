**"By way of practice."** Heath reports Proclus extending the proposition "by way of practice" to a *trapezium*, "a quadrilateral with only one pair of opposite sides parallel" (vol. 1, p. 338). Proclus proves: "*If a triangle be formed by joining the middle point of either of the non-parallel sides to the extremities of the opposite side, the area of the trapezium is always double of that of the triangle.*" (vol. 1, p. 338). Thus the double can survive when the two sides between the parallels cease to be parallel themselves.

**The middle point.** In Proclus' figure, E is the middle point of DC; BE produced meets AD produced. I.26 equates the triangles on either side of E; adding ABED makes the trapezium equal to the triangle on AB whose third vertex is that meeting point. I.38 halves this triangle at E (Heath, vol. 1, p. 339). Heath gathers three cases: the trapezium is greater than, equal to or less than double the triangle formed from a side's middle point and the opposite side, as that middle point lies on the greater parallel side, a non-parallel side or the lesser parallel side. The account ends incompletely, as Heath notes under I.43 that the end of Proclus' note on I.41 is lost.

**Where E may lie.** Our figure follows Euclid's, with E beyond D on AD produced. The statement also allows E between A and D, at either end, or beyond A. I.37 compares ABC and EBC throughout: the parallel through their vertices is a whole straight line, even when the vertices are one point. I.34 then makes the parallelogram double of ABC, and hence of EBC.

**What Lean made us say.** Joining AC needs no construction in the proof: `quadArea` already measures ABCD by the triangles ABC and ACD. We name BC using Postulate 1. `Parallel.not_onLine` puts E off BC, so `Triangle.of_line` supplies the triangle EBC for I.37. The shared step `Prop34.bisect` expresses I.34's bisection as a double; `area_bac` changes EBC to BEC at the end. The certificate cites exactly I.34 and I.37. Its direct axioms are Postulate 1, `line_unique` for the straight line through two points, and `area_rotate` and `area_swap` for naming a triangle in another order. Postulate 5 enters through I.34's use of I.29 and through I.37's parallel arguments.

**The modern way.** Let b = C − B; then D − A = b and E = A + t·b for some real t. The parallelogram has area |b × (A − B)|, while EBC has area ½|b × (E − B)| = ½|b × (A − B)|, since b × b = 0.
