**Slide the vertex, halve the parallelogram.** Euclid joins AC and needs just two steps. I.37 slides the triangle's vertex from E to A along the parallel, and the diameter AC halves the parallelogram, by I.34. He uses the result at once: I.42 builds a parallelogram equal to a triangle with it, and in I.47 it makes each square on the sides, and each piece of the square on the base, double of a triangle.

**"By way of practice."** On this proposition Proclus considers "the area of a *trapezium* (a quadrilateral with only one pair of opposite sides parallel)", Heath reports (vol. 1, p. 338). He finds it less than double the triangle on its greater parallel side and more than double that on its lesser. Then he proves this: "*If a triangle be formed by joining the middle point of either of the non-parallel sides to the extremities of the opposite side, the area of the trapezium is always double of that of the triangle*" (vol. 1, p. 338).

**Why the middle point?** Proclus produces the straight line from one end of the opposite side through the middle point until it meets the other parallel side, produced. The two small triangles at the middle point are equal (I.26), so the trapezium is equal to one large triangle, and the triangle of the theorem is half of it, by I.38 (Heath, vol. 1, p. 339). Heath gathers Proclus' three results into one: the trapezium is greater than, equal to or less than double the triangle, as the middle point is on the greater parallel side, a side that is not parallel, or the lesser. The end of Proclus' note is lost, as Heath remarks under I.43.

**Where may E lie?** Our figure is Euclid's, with E on AD produced beyond D, but our statement lets E fall anywhere on the straight line AD, at A or at D among them. One argument serves for all, since our I.37 was stated with these cases in mind, and joining AC costs nothing: our area of a four-sided figure is already measured by a diameter.

**The modern way.** Let b = C − B; then D − A = b, and E = A + t·b for some real number t. With the cross product of I.35, the parallelogram has area |b × (A − B)|, while EBC has area ½|b × (E − B)| = ½|b × (A − B)|, since b × b = 0.
