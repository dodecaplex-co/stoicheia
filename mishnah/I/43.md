**What the complements fill.** Heath glosses complements as "the figures put in to fill up (interstices)" (vol. 1, p. 341). The parallelograms EH and FG lie about the diameter AC, leaving BK and KD to complete ABCD. Our statement takes the case where the two parallelograms share one point K on AC. Heath, discussing Proclus, observes that "the complements need not be parallelograms" (vol. 1, p. 341): when the two parallelograms have no common point, the remaining figures have five sides; when they overlap, the areas left over require a different reckoning. Those arrangements lie beyond our statement.

**Equal wholes, equal parts.** Euclid uses I.34 three times: AC divides ABCD into equal triangles, AK divides EH, and KC divides FG. Common Notion 2 adds the smaller equal triangles, and Common Notion 3 takes their sums from the equal wholes. The certificate cites I.34 alone, exactly Euclid's bracket. The proof uses Postulate 1 to name the straight lines in the cutting argument, together with Book Zero's rules for sides, betweenness and area. Postulate 5 enters through I.34 and its use of I.29.

**What Lean made us say.** `Prop43.cut` proves that ABC is made up of AEK, the complement BK and KGC, using the three betweennesses. The definition `quadArea` measures EBGK by EG, whereas cutting ABC uses BK. Since BK separates E from G, `OppositeSides.exists_between` gives their crossing point; `Between.of_inside` puts it between B and K. The point has a name in Lean and no letter in the figure. Then `quadArea_rotate_of_cross` allows either diameter to measure the complement, and `area_cut` supplies the three pieces. The same argument cuts ADC. Lean never needs the complements to be parallelograms, only that their diameters cross.

**The modern way.** Put A at the origin, AB = u, AD = v and K = su + tv, with 0 < s, t < 1. The complements have areas (1−s)t|u×v| and s(1−t)|u×v|, equal exactly when s = t, which says that K lies on AC. This converse is not Euclid's assertion.
