**"The so-called complements."** About the diameter AC of the parallelogram ABCD stand two smaller parallelograms, EH and FG, meeting at a point K of AC; BK and KD fill up the rest. Their Greek name means, in Heath's rendering, "the figures put in to fill up (interstices)", and Euclid's "so-called" tells Heath that the term "was not new, though it might not be universally known" (vol. 1, p. 341). The proof is I.34 three times: AC halves ABCD, AK halves EH, and KC halves FG. Common Notion 2 adds the two small halves on each side of AC, and Common Notion 3 takes them from the halves of the whole, leaving the complements equal. I.44 is built on this.

**Must the complements be parallelograms?** In Euclid's figure the parallelograms about the diameter meet at K, and our statement asks for that. Proclus lets them stand apart or overlap, and then, Heath notes, "the complements need not be parallelograms" (vol. 1, p. 341). Apart, they have five sides; overlapping, each is, as Heath counts it, a parallelogram less a triangle. Proclus says Euclid needed no definition of a complement, the name being "simply suggested by the facts", and Heath sums up: "In all the cases it is easy to show, as Proclus does, that the complements are still equal" (vol. 1, p. 341).

**"The complement BK which remains."** Euclid takes for granted that the triangle ABC is made up of the triangle AEK, the complement BK and the triangle KGC, so that BK is what remains. We prove it from the order of the points alone (`Prop43.cut`). Our area of a four-sided figure is measured along a diameter, but the cut runs along the other one, BK. The two diameters of the complement cross, at a point Euclid never needed to name, so either will do, and Lean never needs the complements to be parallelograms.

**The modern way.** Put A at the origin, AB = u, AD = v and K = su + tv, with 0 < s, t < 1. The complements have areas (1−s)t|u×v| and s(1−t)|u×v|, equal exactly when s = t, that is, when K lies on AC. Euclid proves that K on AC makes them equal; the converse is not his.
