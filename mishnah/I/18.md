**Which comes first?** I.18 and I.19 say almost the same words in a different order. Heath's rule tells them apart: "the *datum* comes first and the *quaesitum* second, the *datum* being in this proposition the greater *side* and in the next the greater *angle*" (vol. 1, p. 283), the given before the sought. Aristotle already quotes the result of I.19 "using the exact wording" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 283). And to help the student remember which of the two is proved directly, Isaac Todhunter observes that "the order is similar to that in I. 5 and I. 6" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 283): I.18, like I.5, is direct; I.19, like I.6, is a *reductio*.

**From the other end.** Heath gives a proof of Porphyry's, from Proclus, which "starts by supposing a length equal to AB cut off from the other end of AC; that is, CD and not AD is made equal to AB" (vol. 1, p. 283). Then AB is produced to E, with BE equal to AD, so that AE is equal to AC and the triangle AEC has equal angles at E and C. By I.16 the angle ABC is greater than the one at E, so greater than ACE, and all the more than its part ACB. Both proofs rest on I.5, which Euclid uses in "since the side AB is equal to AD" without a citation; the Lean cites it.

**"Much greater."** Here Euclid reads off his figure that BD falls within the angle ABC, and Lean makes us say so. D lies between A and C, so the angle ABC is made up of ABD and DBC (`angle_add_of_between`), and since DBC is not nothing, the whole is greater than the part (Common Notion 5). "Much greater" is then the tacit rule that what is greater than the greater is greater still (`lt_trans`). The figure also spares us a step: I.3 has put D between A and C, so the side CD of the triangle BCD already runs on to A, and the angle DCB that I.16 gives is the triangle's own angle ACB.

**The modern way.** Name the sides a = BC, b = CA, c = AB. The law of cosines gives cos C − cos B = (b − c)(b + c − a)(a + b + c)/(2abc), and b + c is greater than a (that is I.20), so when b is greater than c, cos B is less than cos C and the angle B is the greater.
