**Room to cut off CE.** "Let CE be made equal to CD," says Euclid, citing I.3. But I.3 cuts a less straight line off a greater, and nothing says CB is longer than CD; C might even be B. Postulate 2 produces a straight line "continuously", but never says how far. So Book Zero's [`produce_longer`](../../Elements/Figure/Lines.lean) makes room: DC, produced beyond the centre of a circle about C through D (Post. 3), cuts it again (System E, Intersections 5), and a little further on (Post. 2) it is greater than CD (Def. 15). I.3 cuts CE off it, and then C lies between D and E (`between_cut`), which is what makes the angles at C adjacent.

**Which point, and which side.** Euclid's figure has C between A and B. In Lean C may be any point of AB, even A itself, and then there is no AC to take D on: B itself will do. Our I.11 also puts the perpendicular on the side of AB where a given point lies, as I.46 and I.48 will need, and I.1 builds its triangle on whichever side we name. Being on that side, F is off AB, so CDF and CEF really are the triangles that I.8 compares (`Triangle.of_line`).

**Right, and the right angle.** Definition 10 calls an angle right when it is equal to the angle adjacent to it, and in Lean that is `IsRightAngle`. The statement, though, speaks of `∟`, the one size that all right angles share, and it is Postulate 4 that makes each right angle equal to it. So the proof uses Postulate 4, though Euclid cites only Definition 10. Our statement also asks for a right angle with every point of AB other than C, not only D and E: each lies on the ray from C through D or on that through E ([`OnRay.cases_of_between`](../../Elements/Figure/Rays.lean), from System E, Between 7), and an angle depends only on its rays (`angle_onRay_left`).

**I.9 in disguise.** De Morgan remarks that this proposition, which is "to bisect the angle made by a straight line and its continuation", should be a particular case of I.9. Heath: "This is certainly worth noting, though I doubt the advantage of rearranging the propositions in consequence" (vol. 1, pp. 269–270). In Lean the rearrangement isn't on offer: our I.9 asks for a triangle BAC, and a straight line with its continuation makes none.

**The modern way.** Put C at the origin and let u point along AB, so that D and E are −u and u. Then F is as far from one as from the other, |f + u|² = |f − u|², and that simplifies to f·u = 0: CF is perpendicular to AB. In the complex plane it is shorter still: multiplying by i turns AB through a right angle.
