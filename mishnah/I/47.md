**Doubles of equal triangles.** Euclid adds ABC to two right angles and uses I.4 to make the triangles ABD and FBC equal. By I.41, BL is double of ABD and GB double of FBC, so BL equals GB. The same argument gives CL equal to HC, and the rectangles together make BDEC. Lean writes the argument once and applies it twice. Heiberg brackets the sentence about doubles as an interpolation; Heath explains that it quotes a Common Notion itself judged interpolated (vol. 1, p. 350). Here Common Notion 2 proves the step by adding the triangle equality to itself.

**What the figure supplies.** I.17 makes the angles at B and C less than right angles. With the squares drawn outward, `Prop47.within` uses I.13 to put C within DBA and A within FBC; `angle_add_of_within` then justifies adding the angles. I.14 puts CA in a straight line with AG, and BA with AH. The three squares are hypotheses, so the bracketed I.46 is not cited. `Prop28.square` brings in I.28 to show that each given square is a parallelogram.

**Where AL meets the square.** I.31 draws AL, without a bracket in Euclid's text. `Prop30.parallel` makes it parallel to both BD and CE. That a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other comes from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`), as Heath reports Proclus drawing it under I.31. This supplies L and the foot on BC, which has a name in Lean and no letter in the figure. `Between.of_inside` puts the foot between B and C and L between D and E. Book Zero's `quadArea_add_of_parallelograms` then makes the two rectangles the whole square.

**What the proof rests on.** The certificate records Postulates 1 and 2 directly: straight lines are drawn, and produced inside `Prop28.square`. Book Zero supplies the rules for sides, order, angles and cutting areas. I.4 supplies the equality of triangles, and I.41 their doubles; Postulate 5 enters through I.30 and I.41. Heath quotes Proclus: "while I admire those who first observed the truth of this theorem, I marvel more at the writer of the Elements" (vol. 1, p. 350).

**The modern way.** Put A = (0, 0), B = (b, 0), C = (0, c), with b and c positive, and write a = BC. The foot of AL is b²/a from B and c²/a from C, so the rectangles BL and CL have areas b² and c². Their sum is the square BDEC, of area a².
