**Doubles of equal triangles.** Euclid proves the parallelogram BL equal to the square GB, as doubles of the equal triangles ABD, FBC (I.4, I.41), and says "similarly" for CL and HC. We write that half once, over any letters, and use it at B and at C. Heiberg brackets the sentence on doubles because it quotes a Common Notion itself interpolated, as Heath explains (vol. 1, p. 350); in Lean, Common Notion 2 adds the equal triangles to themselves.

**What the figure shows.** "Let the angle ABC be added to each" needs C within the angle DBA, and A within the angle FBC. Euclid reads both off his figure; Lean needs a reason. The angles at B and C are less than right angles (I.17), and the squares are described outward, so `Prop47.within` puts each point where the figure does, by I.13, and `angle_add_of_within` adds the angles. Our statement gives the three squares, so the bracketed I.46 is not cited, and `Prop28.square` brings in I.28 to make each one a parallelogram.

**Where AL falls.** "Through A let AL be drawn parallel" is I.31, though no bracket names it, and `Prop30.parallel` (I.30) makes AL parallel to CE as well. A straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`). So AL meets DE at L, and BC at a point Heath leaves without a letter: it has a name in the Lean and none in the figure. Since A lies between BD and CE, `Between.of_inside` puts that point between B and C and L between D and E, and Book Zero's `quadArea_add_of_parallelograms` makes BL and CL the whole square.

**"I marvel more at the writer of the Elements."** Heath reports Proclus retelling the story that Pythagoras sacrificed an ox for this theorem, and adding: "But for my part, while I admire those who first observed the truth of this theorem, I marvel more at the writer of the Elements" (vol. 1, p. 350). Of the postulates, the certificate records 1 and 2 directly, the second for a straight line produced inside `Prop28.square`, and Postulate 5 comes in through I.30 and I.41.

**The modern way.** Put A = (0, 0), B = (b, 0) and C = (0, c), and write a for BC. The foot of AL divides BC into b²/a and c²/a, so the rectangles BL and CL, of height a, have areas b² and c², and together they make the square on BC: b² + c² = a².
