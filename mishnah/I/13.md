**Two right angles, or equal to two.** Euclid's enunciation offers a choice: a straight line set up on a straight line makes "either two right angles or angles equal to two right angles". The first is the case of Definition 10, where the two angles are equal and so each is right; the second is every other case. In Lean a right angle has one size, `∟`, because Postulate 4 says that all right angles are equal, so the statement keeps only the second form, ∠ CBA + ∠ ABD = ∟ + ∟. The first case then needs one citation more than Euclid gives: Definition 10 says each angle is right, and Postulate 4 says each is `∟`. Heath, a page later, passes on Todhunter's remark about the same postulate in I.14: "for the inference in this line Post. 4, that all right angles are equal, is necessary as well as the Common Notion that things which are equal to the same thing (or rather, here, to equal things) are equal" (vol. 1, p. 277).

**Which way AB leans.** Euclid draws AB leaning towards C, so that BA falls within the right angle CBE and BE falls within the angle DBA. Both of his "since the angle … is equal to the two angles" read this off the figure, and Lean wants each said. Book Zero's `angle_add_of_within` is System E's Diagram-angle rule 2 (an angle is made up of two when a straight line falls within it), phrased with "on the same side of the straight line VP" so that no line has to be named. `Prop13.within` shows that when BA is within CBE, BE is within DBA. The three straight lines through B cut the plane into six regions, and Triple incidence 1, Pasch 3 and the rule that a straight line has only two sides put E on D's side of BA. If AB leans towards D instead, the figure is Euclid's with C and D exchanged, so the Lean gives his argument once, for either naming of the ends of CD, as I.6 does for its two cases. `Prop13.cases` says which way it leans. A cannot lie on BE itself, for then the angles CBA, ABD would be the right angles CBE, EBD over again (`angle_onRay_left` and `angle_onRay_right` in Book Zero) and so equal, which is the case already done. Every rule here is one of Jeremy Avigad, Edward Dean and John Mumma's, read, as always in this book, beside the Lean rendering of them by Logan Murphy, Kaiyu Yang and their LeanEuclid colleagues.

**Which side of CD.** "Let BE be drawn from the point B at right angles to CD" does not say on which side of CD, but the argument needs BE on A's side. Drawn the other way, BA would fall within neither of the angles CBE, EBD, and neither of Euclid's sums would be true. The statement of I.11 lets us choose the side of the perpendicular, and we choose A's. Lean also asks why E is not B, and why A is on E's side of BC and of BD as well as of CD: `SameSideOf.of_line` and `Triangle.off_line` from Book Zero answer, since B, C and D lie on one straight line.

**The same three angles.** The three angles CBA, ABE, EBD and the three angles DBE, EBA, ABC are the same angles, named from the other end and added in the other order. Euclid sees no difference, and Lean wants two tacit rules to agree: an angle is the same read either way (`angle_symm`, System E's Metric rule 4), and magnitudes may be added in any order (`add_comm` and `add_assoc`, System E §3.5). After that, Common Notions 2 and 1 do the rest, exactly where Euclid cites them.

**"Added to each."** Heath's one note on I.13 is about a phrase. "Let the angle EBD be added to each" is "literally 'let the angle EBD be added (so as to be) common,' κοινὴ προσκείσθω ἡ ὑπὸ ΕΒΔ". He explains: "'Let the common angle EBD be added' is clearly an inaccurate translation, for the angle is not common before it is added, i.e. the κοινή is proleptic". Since the matching phrase is used for subtracting, "it seems hopeless to attempt to keep the word 'common,' and I have therefore said 'to each' and 'from each' simply" (vol. 1, p. 276). The Lean says it the Greek way: `CommonNotion2 hCBE_sum rfl` adds the angle EBD to both sides, and the `rfl` is there because it is one and the same angle, common to both.

**The modern way.** Put B at 0 in the complex plane, D at 1, C at −1 and A at e^{iθ} with 0 < θ < π. The angle ABD is arg e^{iθ} = θ, the angle CBA is arg(−e^{−iθ}) = π − θ, and together they make π: two right angles.
