**Two right angles, or equal to two.** Euclid's enunciation offers a choice: "either two right angles or angles equal to two right angles". The first is the case of Definition 10, where the two angles are equal and so each is right. In Lean a right angle has one size, `∟`, because Postulate 4 says that all right angles are equal, so the statement keeps only the second form, ∠ CBA + ∠ ABD = ∟ + ∟. The first case then needs one citation more than Euclid gives: Definition 10, which he cites, makes each angle right, and Postulate 4, which he doesn't, makes each `∟` (I.11 brings it in as well); Isaac Todhunter points out its quiet work under I.14.

**Which way AB leans.** Euclid draws AB leaning towards C, so that BA falls within the right angle CBE and BE within the angle DBA, and both of his "since the angle … is equal to the two angles" read this off the figure. Lean wants each said: `angle_add_of_within` (System E, Diagram-angle 2) makes an angle the sum of two when a straight line falls within it, and `Prop13.within` shows that BE falls within DBA. If AB leans towards D, the figure is Euclid's with C and D exchanged, so the Lean gives his argument once and uses it either way (`Prop13.cases`). A cannot lie on BE itself, for then the angles CBA, ABD would be the right angles CBE, EBD over again, and equal. And BE must be drawn on A's side of CD, which the statement of I.11 lets us choose.

**"Added to each."** Heath's one note on I.13 is about a phrase. "Let the angle EBD be added to each" is "literally 'let the angle EBD be added (so as to be) common,' κοινὴ προσκείσθω ἡ ὑπὸ ΕΒΔ", and "'Let the common angle EBD be added' is clearly an inaccurate translation, for the angle is not common before it is added, i.e. the κοινή is proleptic" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 276). The Lean says it the Greek way: `CommonNotion2 hCBE_sum rfl` adds the angle EBD to both sides, and the `rfl` is there because it is one and the same angle, common to both.

**The modern way.** Put B at 0 in the complex plane, D at 1, C at −1 and A at e^{iθ} with 0 < θ < π. The angle ABD is arg e^{iθ} = θ, the angle CBA is arg(−e^{−iθ}) = π − θ, and together they make π: two right angles.
