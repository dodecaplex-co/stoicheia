**Two right angles, or equal to two?** Two right angles enter the propositions here, and stay: I.14, I.15 and I.32 all turn on this one. Euclid offers a choice, "either two right angles or angles equal to two right angles"; the first is Definition 10's case, where the two angles are equal and so each is right. In Lean a right angle has one size, `∟`, because Postulate 4 makes all right angles equal. So our statement keeps only the second form, ∠ CBA + ∠ ABD = ∟ + ∟, and Euclid's first case needs a step he leaves unsaid: Postulate 4, to make each right angle `∟`. Isaac Todhunter saw it doing the same quiet work in I.14.

**Which way does AB lean?** Euclid draws AB leaning towards C, with BA within the right angle CBE and BE within the angle DBA, and both his sums of angles read this off the figure. Lean wants each said. AB cannot lie along BE, or the angles CBA, ABD would be the right angles CBE, EBD over again, and equal; so it leans one way or the other (`Prop13.cases`). Leaning towards D, it makes his figure with C and D exchanged, so we give his argument once and use it either way. BE falls within DBA (`Prop13.within`) because BA separates C from both E and D, and a straight line has two sides and no more; and our I.11 draws BE on A's side of CD.

**"Not common before it is added."** Euclid's "let the angle EBD be added to each" is, Heath explains, "literally 'let the angle EBD be added (so as to be) common,' κοινὴ προσκείσθω ἡ ὑπὸ ΕΒΔ", and "'Let the common angle EBD be added' is clearly an inaccurate translation, for the angle is not common before it is added, i.e. the κοινή is proleptic" (vol. 1, p. 276): it calls the angle common before it has become so. So Heath says simply "to each", and "from each" for taking away. Our Lean says it the Greek way: `CommonNotion2` adds the angle EBD to both sides, and `rfl`, which proves a thing equal to itself, says that it is one angle, common to both.

**The modern way.** Put B at 0 in the complex plane, D at 1, C at −1 and A at cos θ + i sin θ, with 0 < θ < π: the angle ABD is the argument of A, which is θ, and the angle CBA is the argument of −1/A = −cos θ + i sin θ, which is π − θ. Together they make π, two right angles.
