**"Let the straight line GK fall upon them."** Heath's figure puts EF between AB and CD, so that GK, falling on all three, crosses EF at H. I.29 makes the angle AGK equal to GHF, and GHF equal to GKD; Common Notion 1 joins the two, and AGK, GKD are alternate. Euclid's "Therefore AB is parallel to CD" is I.27, though he gives no bracket for it, so the Lean cites it. Heath notes that the usual "Therefore etc." is, "curiously enough, wanting at the end of this proposition" (vol. 1, p. 314), and our Lean goes without it too.

**When EF lies outside.** EF may lie outside AB and CD, and then a straight line from one to the other need not cross it. So the Lean proves `Prop30.unique`: two straight lines through one point, each parallel to a third, are one straight line. Join the point to a point of the third: by I.29, the interior angles this makes with each of the two and the third make two right angles, Common Notion 3 takes away the angle at the third, which they share, and `onRay_of_angle_eq` makes the two rays one. So AB and CD do not meet, or they would be one straight line; the statement keeps C off AB, a straight line not being parallel to itself. `Prop30.unique` is "Playfair's" axiom, proved here from I.29: "The proposition is, as De Morgan points out, the *logical* equivalent of 'Playfair's' axiom" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 314).

**What Lean made us say.** In Heath's arrangement, `OppositeSides.exists_between` finds H, where GK crosses EF. The given points need not stand where Heath's do, so `Prop30.point_on_side` takes an end of each straight line on whichever side of GK his figure wants, producing it if it must; parallels are whole straight lines, so the names can change. Each citation of I.29 is one of the corollaries that follow it, `Prop29.alternate`, `Prop29.interior` and `Prop29.exterior`, for which Book Zero sets out I.29's figure; `Prop29.exterior` lets the exterior angle at H be GHF itself. These steps use Postulates 1 and 2 (I.27 and I.29 bring both in as well); Common Notion 5 comes in through `onRay_of_angle_eq`, and Postulate 5 through I.29, either way.

**The modern way.** Give each straight line a point and a direction. Straight lines that never meet have directions that are multiples of one another, so AB and CD, each parallel to EF, have directions that are multiples of each other's; with C off AB, they are two straight lines with one direction, and never meet.
