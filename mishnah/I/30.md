**Three straight lines, two equalities.** We draw EF between AB and CD, as Heath does. The straight line GK crosses EF at H. I.29 makes the angle AGK equal to GHF, and then GHF equal to GKD. Common Notion 1 joins the equalities. The concluding alternate angles give I.27, which Euclid uses without a bracket. In Lean, `AreParallel.figure_alternate` supplies the figure for the first citation of I.29; `AreParallel.figure_interior` supplies the exterior angle for the second. The points used to produce that figure need no extra letters in our drawing. `OnRay.of_between_between` identifies the produced arm with the arm through G.

**The arrangement left open.** The enunciation also covers EF outside the other two straight lines. We cannot then assume that the segment GK crosses EF. `Prop30.unique` proves that two straight lines through one point, each parallel to a third, coincide: I.29 gives equal sums of interior angles, Common Notion 3 removes the common angle, and `onRay_of_angle_eq` identifies the rays on the same side. If AB and CD met, this would identify them, contrary to C being off AB. Heath writes: "The proposition is, as De Morgan points out, the *logical* equivalent of 'Playfair's' axiom" (vol. 1, p. 314). We derive the uniqueness here from I.29; it is no added assumption.

**What the figure asks us to say.** In the pictured case, `OppositeSides.exists_between` supplies H between G and K. `Prop30.point_on_side` chooses ends on the required sides of GK, producing a straight line when needed. We may rename endpoints because parallelism concerns whole straight lines. Postulates 1 and 2 enter these constructions. The current certificate records I.27 and I.29, with Common Notions 1, 3 and 5; the last enters through the argument identifying equal-angle rays. It marks I.30 proved pending those two unfinished dependencies. Postulate 5 is not yet recorded in this checkout, because I.29 remains a sketch; the completed argument depends on it through I.29.

**The modern way.** Represent a straight line by a point and a nonzero direction vector. Two straight lines parallel to a third have direction vectors proportional to that third direction, hence proportional to one another. The condition that C lies off AB excludes coincidence, leaving two disjoint straight lines.
