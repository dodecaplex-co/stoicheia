**By means of division.** I.19 turns I.18 round, as I.6 turns I.5 round; Heath's rule for telling the pair apart, and Todhunter's aid to memory, are given under I.18. "Here, says Proclus (p. 318, 16–23), Euclid proves the impossibility 'by means of division' (ἐκ διαιρέσεως). This means simply the separation of different hypotheses, each of which is inconsistent with the truth of the theorem to be proved, and which therefore must be successively shown to be impossible" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 285). If AC is not greater than AB, it is equal to it or less. Euclid takes the division for granted; Lean names it, the tacit law of magnitudes `lt_trichotomy`, and closes it with `Classical.byContradiction`, as at the end of I.6.

**A purely logical deduction.** Heath: "This proposition, like I. 6, can be proved by merely logical deduction from I. 5 and I. 18 taken together, as pointed out by De Morgan." De Morgan takes three assertions X, Y, Z "of which it is the property that one or the other must be true, and one only", and three more, P, Q, R, of the same kind; if X brings P, Y brings Q and Z brings R, then "it follows that, when P is true, X is true, when Q is true, Y is true, when R is true, Z is true" (vol. 1, p. 284). Here X, Y, Z are AC equal to, greater than and less than AB, P, Q, R the same for the angles ABC and ACB, and I.5 and I.18 make the connexions. The Lean proof is his argument written out.

**What Lean made us say.** Euclid's hypothesis calls the less angle BCA and his argument calls it ACB, and Lean turns an angle round only when told to (`angle_symm`, System E, Metric 4). Euclid's "for then the angle ABC would also have been less than the angle ACB" is I.18 for the triangle ACB (`Triangle.acb`), which hands back the angle CBA, to be turned round once more. Each "but it is not" is a law of magnitudes: `Magnitude.ne_of_lt`, then `lt_trans` and `lt_irrefl`. As in I.18, our I.5 wants its equal sides produced (`Prop5.base`), so the proof uses Postulate 2 there; I.18 brings it in as well.

**The modern way.** Put B at (0, 0), C at (a, 0) and A at (x, y) with y > 0. Then cot B = x/y and cot C = (a − x)/y, so the angle at B is the greater exactly when x < a/2; and AC² − AB² = a(a − 2x), so AC is the greater exactly then too: I.18 and I.19 in one line.
