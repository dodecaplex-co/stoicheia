**Two ways out, both shut.** I.19 turns I.18 round, as I.6 turns I.5 round. "Here, says Proclus (p. 318, 16–23), Euclid proves the impossibility 'by means of *division*' (ἐκ διαιρέσεως). This means simply the separation of different hypotheses, each of which is inconsistent with the truth of the theorem to be proved, and which therefore must be successively shown to be impossible" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 285). If AC is not greater than AB, it is equal to it or less; I.5 shuts the first way out and I.18 the second.

**Could logic alone do it?** Yes, said Augustus De Morgan: I.19, "like I. 6, can be proved by merely *logical* deduction from I. 5 and I. 18 taken together" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 284). Of three assertions X, Y, Z, "one or the other must be true, *and one only*", and so too of three more, P, Q, R. If X brings P, Y brings Q and Z brings R, then "it follows that, when P is true, X is true, when Q is true, Y is true, when R is true, Z is true" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 284). Here X, Y, Z compare AC with AB, P, Q, R the angles at B and C, and I.5 and I.18 are the links.

**A direct proof.** Proclus gives one, which an-Nairīzī also has and attributes to Heron. It rests on a lemma: if the straight line bisecting an angle of a triangle cuts the base unequally, the side that meets the greater part is the greater. Heath finds it "open to the slight objection of separating a theorem from its converse. But the lemma and proof are worth giving" (vol. 1, p. 285).

**One angle, read from either arm.** Euclid's hypothesis calls the less angle BCA and his argument calls it ACB, and our proof says that they are one angle (`angle_symm`). His division is a tacit rule of magnitudes, which we cite too (`lt_trichotomy`): of two lengths, one is less, or they are equal, or the other is less.

**The modern way.** Put B at (0, 0), C at (a, 0) and A at (x, y) with y > 0. Then cot B = x/y and cot C = (a − x)/y, so the angle at B is the greater exactly when x < a/2; and AC² − AB² = a(a − 2x), so AC is the greater exactly then too: I.18 and I.19 in one line.
