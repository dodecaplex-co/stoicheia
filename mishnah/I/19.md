**Two ways out, both shut.** I.19 turns I.18 round, as I.6 turns I.5 round. "Here, says Proclus (p. 318, 16–23), Euclid proves the impossibility 'by means of *division*' (ἐκ διαιρέσεως). This means simply the separation of different hypotheses, each of which is inconsistent with the truth of the theorem to be proved, and which therefore must be successively shown to be impossible" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 285). If AC is not greater than AB, it is equal to it or less; I.5 shuts the first way out and I.18 the second.

**Could logic alone do it?** Augustus De Morgan saw that it could: I.19, "like I. 6, can be proved by merely *logical* deduction from I. 5 and I. 18 taken together" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 284). Take three assertions X, Y, Z "of which it is the property that one or the other must be true, *and one only*", and three more, P, Q, R, alike. If X brings P, Y brings Q and Z brings R, then "it follows that, when P is true, X is true, when Q is true, Y is true, when R is true, Z is true" (vol. 1, p. 284). In I.19, X, Y, Z are AC equal to, greater than and less than AB, P, Q, R the same for the angles ABC, ACB, and I.5 and I.18 the links. Euclid's division, which we follow, is one case of the rule.

**Turning angles round.** Euclid's hypothesis calls the less angle BCA and his argument calls it ACB, and Lean turns an angle round only when told to (`angle_symm`, System E, Metric 4). His "for then the angle ABC would also have been less than the angle ACB" is I.18 for the triangle ACB, which hands back the angle CBA, to be turned round once more. As in I.6, Lean wants the division itself said, by a tacit rule of magnitudes (`lt_trichotomy`), and each "but it is not" is such a rule too: nothing is equal to what it is greater than, nor less than it. At the end we close the argument as I.6 does, with `Classical.byContradiction`.

**The modern way.** Put B at (0, 0), C at (a, 0) and A at (x, y) with y > 0. Then cot B = x/y and cot C = (a − x)/y, so the angle at B is the greater exactly when x < a/2; and AC² − AB² = a(a − 2x), so AC is the greater exactly then too: I.18 and I.19 in one line.
