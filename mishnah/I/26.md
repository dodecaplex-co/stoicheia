**Two cuts, two contradictions.** We know two angles and a corresponding side. When the side adjoins the equal angles, Euclid supposes AB greater than DE and cuts off BG equal to DE. I.4 then makes the angle BCG equal to the whole angle BCA, although it is a part. When the given side subtends an equal angle, he supposes BC greater than EF and cuts off BH equal to EF. I.4 now makes the exterior angle BHA equal to the opposite interior angle BCA, against I.16. Each contradiction supplies the missing side equality, and I.4 finishes the case. As in Heath's figure, G and H appear together; their cuts belong to separate suppositions.

**What the figure supplies.** Lean needs G strictly between B and A, and H strictly between B and C. We cite `Prop3` for both cuts, although Euclid gives no bracket there. `Triangle.of_line` proves that the smaller triangles are triangles at all. `OnRay.of_between`, `angle_onRay_left` and `angle_onRay_right` identify the unchanged angles, while `angle_lt_of_between` supplies the whole greater than the part. For I.16 we name the triangle ACH, so that CH is produced to B. Euclid treats the side in ABC as the greater; each local argument is stated for arbitrary triangles and used again with their names exchanged. The certificate cites I.3, I.4 and I.16, and includes Postulates 1–4 and Definition 15 through their proofs, with no Postulate 5.

**An early alternative.** Heath describes a proof by applying one triangle to the other, recorded by an-Nairīzī for the case of the adjoining side: "the proof is one which he had found, but of which he did not know the author" (vol. 1, p. 303). We follow Euclid's two contradictions. His applications of I.4 bring in the superposition on which I.4 rests; this proof makes no further appeal to it.

**The modern way.** In Euclidean trigonometry, the third angle is π minus the two given angles, and the sine rule fixes every side from the given side and the angles. That calculation uses the angle sum theorem; Euclid's argument here does not require the parallel postulate.
