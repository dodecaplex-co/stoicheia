**Two cuts, two contradictions.** Two angles are given equal, and one side, and Euclid takes in turn the two places the side can stand. When it adjoins the equal angles, he supposes AB greater than DE and cuts off BG equal to DE: I.4 then makes the angle BCG equal to the angle BCA, "the less to the greater". When it subtends one of them, he supposes BC greater than EF and cuts off BH equal to EF: I.4 now makes the exterior angle BHA equal to the interior and opposite angle BCA, which I.16 forbids. Either way the sides are equal after all, and I.4 finishes. Heath's figure, like ours, shows G and H together, though each belongs to its own supposition.

**What Lean made us say.** Euclid's "let BG be made equal to DE" has no bracket, but it is I.3's work, so the Lean cites `Prop3` for both cuts, and I.3 puts G between B and A, and H between B and C. `Triangle.of_line` shows that GBC, ABH and AHC are triangles at all. The angles GBC and ABH are the angle ABC, their arms being the same rays (`OnRay.of_between`, `angle_onRay_left`, `angle_onRay_right`), and the angle BCG, a part of BCA, is less than it (`angle_lt_of_between`). Euclid lets the side in ABC be the greater; the Lean gives each argument once, for any two triangles, and uses it twice, the second time with the triangles exchanged.

**An early alternative.** The other way to prove it, by applying one triangle to the other, "was very early discovered", Heath says, at least for the side adjoining the equal angles; an-Nairīzī gives it, "observing that the proof is one which he had found, but of which he did not know the author" (vol. 1, p. 303). Euclid's own proof moves nothing: the one superposition it rests on is inside I.4. It uses Postulate 1 to join GC and AH (I.3, I.4 and I.16 bring it in as well), and through those three come Postulates 2 to 4 and Definition 15, but not Postulate 5.

**The modern way.** The sine rule, a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, settles it at once: the two angles give the third, two right angles less their sum, and the one side then gives the other two. But the third angle is I.32, which needs Postulate 5, and Euclid's proof here does without it.
