**"Less than two right angles."** Here Euclid calls on Postulate 5 for the first time, and Heath honours the choice: "When we consider the countless successive attempts made through more than twenty centuries to prove the Postulate, many of them by geometers of ability, we cannot but admire the genius of the man who concluded that such a hypothesis, which he found necessary to the validity of his whole system of geometry, was really indemonstrable" (vol. 1, p. 202). Our Lean calls on it at the same step.

**"A good deal is left to be understood."** Euclid cites his postulate here in shorter words than its own, and Heath spells out what they leave unsaid, "namely that the straight lines begin from points at which they meet a transversal" (vol. 1, p. 312). Our `Postulate5` says every word: straight lines meeting GH at G and H, with B and D on one side of it and the angles BGH, GHD less than two right angles. What the postulate gives, a point on both AB and CD, is just what Definition I.23 rules out.

**"Let the angle AGH be greater."** Euclid passes over the case where GHD is the greater. It is his argument read from H's end, on the side of A and C, with I.13 at H, and we use it so, as in I.16. After the proof, `Prop29.alternate`, `Prop29.interior` and `Prop29.exterior` say I.29 of any straight line falling on two parallels, for later propositions to cite.

**"A direct criterion."** Heath also proves I.29 from John Playfair's axiom, that through a given point only one parallel can be drawn to a given straight line, as modern textbooks usually do. Charles Dodgson, better known as Lewis Carroll, argued for keeping Euclid's postulate: it puts before the beginner "clear and *positive* conceptions", not straight lines that never meet, "a *negative* conception"; and it "gives a direct criterion for judging that two straight lines meet, a criterion which is constantly required, e.g. in I. 44" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 314).

**The modern way.** In coordinates, straight lines that never meet have one direction: if u points from A to B, then D − C is a multiple of u, and a positive one, since B and D lie on one side of EF. With w pointing from G to H, the angle AGH lies between −u and w, the angle GHD between −w and u, and both have cosine −u·w/(|u||w|).
