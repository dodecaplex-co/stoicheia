**Where G falls.** "Euclid speaks of the triangle DGE without any explanation that, in the case which he takes (where AD, EF have no point in common), BE, CD must meet at a point G between the two parallels. He allows this to appear from the figure simply" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 327). Our `Prop35.figure` proves it. ED is parallel to BC, so Book Zero's `AreParallel.cross_or` says that either EC and BD cross or EB and DC do; E and B lie on opposite sides of DC, so it is EB and DC. The same helper puts E between D and F, as "the whole DF" needs.

**"Equality in a new sense."** So Heath heads his note, for here "figures are inferred to be *equal* which are equal in *area* or in *content* but need not be of the same *form*" (vol. 1, p. 327). I.34, I.29 and I.4 make the triangles EAB, FDC equal; Euclid takes DGE from each and adds GBC to the trapezia that remain. Lean writes each taking away as an adding: `area_cut` makes EAB the trapezium ABGD and DGE together, and FDC the trapezium EGCF and DGE, and Common Notion 3 takes DGE away.

**The other cases.** Proclus, "observing that Euclid has given only the most difficult of the three possible cases, adds the other two with separate proofs" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 328), and the Lean gives them as helpers. In `Prop35.between`, E falls between A and D, and the equal triangles are added to the trapezium EBCD, as Proclus adds them. In `Prop35.same`, E is D, and the common triangle DBC is added to the equal triangles ADB, DFC. Exchanging the two parallelograms covers each case the other way round. Where the diameters cross in these cases, the Lean names points that have no letters in Heath.

**What it rests on.** The Lean cites just Euclid's three brackets. The proof uses Postulate 1 for its straight lines, and Postulate 2 where `Prop29.exterior` produces one for I.29's figure; I.29 and I.34 bring in both as well, I.4 Postulate 1, and I.29 and I.34 Postulate 5. Common Notion 5, that the whole is greater than the part, comes in only with the cases Euclid leaves out: `len_lt_of_between` puts D between E and F when E lies between A and D, and `OnRay.eq_of_len_eq` makes F the point D when E is A.

**The modern way.** As E lies on AF, parallel to BC, E − B = (A − B) + t(C − B) for some number t. Crossing with C − B removes that multiple, so |(C − B) × (E − B)| = |(C − B) × (A − B)|: the two parallelograms have one base, one height and one area.
