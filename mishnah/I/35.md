**The crossing Euclid leaves to the figure.** Heath writes (vol. 1, p. 327): "Euclid speaks of the triangle DGE without any explanation that, in the case which he takes (where AD, EF have no point in common), BE, CD must meet at a point G between the two parallels. He allows this to appear from the figure simply." Our `Prop35.figure` proves this crossing using `AreParallel.cross_or`, Book Zero's rule that a four-sided figure between parallels has crossing diameters or crossing sides. It also puts E between D and F, so that adding DE to the equal straight lines AD and EF gives AE equal to DF.

**Equal areas from equal triangles.** I.34 gives the equal sides, I.29 the equal included angles, and I.4 the equal triangles EAB and FDC. Subtract DGE from each; add GBC to each remainder. Lean expresses each subtraction as an addition, using `area_cut` to divide a triangle from a corner. The trapezium ABGD is written as `quadArea` with its corners ordered BGDA, so its diameter is BD. Common Notion 3 cancels the common triangle; Common Notion 2 restores GBC. The parallelograms can have different shapes: equality here measures their areas, as Heath explains on pp. 327–328.

**The other positions of E.** Heath reports that Proclus supplies the other cases (vol. 1, p. 328). In `Prop35.between`, E lies between A and D: the equal triangles are added to the common trapezium EBCD. `Prop35.same` handles E at D by adding the common triangle DBC. When E is A, `OnRay.eq_of_len_eq` makes F equal to D. Exchanging the parallelograms covers the reverse arrangements. The auxiliary crossings used to justify these cases have names in Lean and no letters in Heath's figure.

**What the proof rests on.** The certificate cites I.4, I.29 and I.34, exactly Euclid's brackets. Postulate 1 supplies the named straight lines; Postulate 2 enters through `Prop29.exterior`, which produces straight lines for I.29's figure. Both also enter through I.29 and I.34, and Postulate 1 through I.4. Common Notion 5 enters through `len_lt_of_between`, which compares ED with EF in the overlapping case. The direct tacit rules govern incidence, order, sides, lengths, rays and area addition. Postulate 5 comes through I.29 and I.34.

**The modern way.** Since E lies on AF, parallel to BC, the vectors satisfy BE = BA + t·BC for some real t. Thus |BC × BE| = |BC × BA|: shearing the parallelogram preserves its area, the base multiplied by the height.
