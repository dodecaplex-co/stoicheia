**Oenopides' angle.** Heath: "This problem was, according to Eudemus (see Proclus, p. 333, 5), 'rather the discovery of Oenopides,' from which we must apparently infer, not that Oenopides was the first to find any solution of it, but that it was he who discovered the particular solution given by Euclid" (vol. 1, p. 295). An angle is hard to carry about, but a triangle is only three lengths, which I.22 can set down anywhere: carry DCE across, and I.8 brings the angle with it.

**"Not exactly the construction given in I. 22."** So Heath warns, adding that the editors had missed it: "We have here to construct a triangle on a certain finite straight line AG as base; in I. 22 we have only to construct a triangle with sides of given length without any restriction as to how it is to be placed" (vol. 1, p. 295). Our I.22 is stated to close the gap: its triangle has a corner at a given point, a side along a given ray from it, and its third corner on the side of a given point. I.23 then goes through as Euclid writes it.

**What Lean made us say.** I.22's condition is not repeated here, but the three straight lines are the sides of the triangle DCE, so I.20 supplies it (`add_comm` and `len_symm` put each sum in I.22's order), as the bracket in I.22's enunciation suggests. I.8 wants AFG to be a triangle: F lies on AB and is not A, and G, being on one side of AB, is off it (`SameSideOf.triangle`). D and E, "taken at random", are the points the statement names, and any others on CD and CE would do, since an angle depends only on its rays (`angle_eq_of_onRay`).

**Which side, and which way.** Euclid's "on the given straight line AB, and at the point A on it" leaves two choices open: along the ray from A through B or away from it, and above AB or below. His figure settles both, as I.24, I.31 and I.42 need, so our statement puts F on the ray from A through B, and G on the side of AB where a given point P lies. Our figure follows the proof, so its F and G trade places with Heath's, whose G stands on AB and F above it; the angle FAG is the same either way.

**The modern way.** In the complex plane, multiplying by w turns every direction through the angle arg w. So the point A + (B − A)(E − C)/(D − C) makes with AB, at A, an angle equal to DCE, and its mirror image in the straight line AB makes the same angle on the other side; which of the two lies on P's side is a matter of one sign.
