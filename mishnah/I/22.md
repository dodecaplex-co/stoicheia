**The first condition on a problem.** Heath: "This is the first case in the *Elements* of a διορισμός to a problem in the sense of a statement of the conditions or limits of the possibility of a solution. The criterion is of course supplied by the preceding proposition" (vol. 1, p. 293): hence the bracket [I. 20]. In Lean the condition is three hypotheses, every one of them used; I.20, which shows only that the condition cannot be dropped, is not cited.

**Must the circles meet?** "It was early observed," writes Heath, "that Euclid assumes, without giving any reason, that the circles drawn as described will meet" when the condition holds (vol. 1, p. 293). Robert Simson was impatient: "For who is so dull, though only beginning to learn the Elements, as not to perceive that the circle described from the centre F, at the distance FD, must meet FH betwixt F and H, because FD is less than FH" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 293). Lean is exactly that dull.

**Heath's answer.** [`Prop22.meet`](../../Elements/BookI/Prop22.lean) follows it: the circle about G has a point outside the circle about F and a point inside it (Heath, vol. 1, p. 294). H is outside, FH being B and C together, which is greater than A. The point M where GF, produced if need be, meets the circle about G is inside: whether M falls between G and F or beyond F, FM is less than A, once the same thing is taken from two unequals (`Magnitude.lt_of_add_lt_add_right`). Then "we have only to invoke the Principle of Continuity, as we have to do in I. 1" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 294): here System E, Intersection 4, in LeanEuclid's corrected form. Lean also wanted the case Heath passes over, where M is the centre F itself.

**Room on the straight line.** Euclid's DE is "terminated at D but of infinite length in the direction of E", so that I.3 can cut DF, FG and GH off it. A straight line in Lean has no length to cut from, so `Prop22.room` makes room: it sets out a straight line equal to the given one (I.2), and produces the straight line to be cut from further than that (`produce_longer`). That is why I.2 is among the propositions I.22 cites. And K is taken on a chosen side of DE (System E, Intersections 8), as I.23 will need.

**The modern way.** In the complex plane put F at 0 and G at b > 0, and let K = x + iy with x = (a² + b² − c²)/2b and y = √(a² − x²). Then |K| = a and |K − b| = c, and y is real and positive exactly when |a² + b² − c²| < 2ab, that is, when |a − b| < c < a + b: the three conditions of I.22, in one line.
