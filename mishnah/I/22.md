**A problem with a condition.** Not every three straight lines make a triangle, as the enunciation says. Heath notes: "This is the first case in the *Elements* of a διορισμός to a problem in the sense of a statement of the conditions or limits of the possibility of a solution. The criterion is of course supplied by the preceding proposition" (vol. 1, p. 293): Euclid cites I.20. Our statement makes the condition three hypotheses, each used; the Lean needs no I.20, which shows only that the condition cannot be dropped.

**"Who is so dull?"** "It was early observed," writes Heath, "that Euclid assumes, without giving any reason, that the circles drawn as described will meet" when the condition holds (vol. 1, p. 293). Robert Simson was impatient: "For who is so dull, though only beginning to learn the Elements, as not to perceive that the circle described from the centre F, at the distance FD, must meet FH betwixt F and H, because FD is less than FH" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 293). Lean is exactly that dull.

**"Only to invoke the Principle of Continuity."** Heath's answer, which our `Prop22.meet` follows: the circle about G has a point outside the circle about F and a point inside it. H is outside, FH being B and C together, greater than A. The point M where GF, produced if need be, meets the circle about G is inside, since taking the same thing from two unequals leaves FM less than A; Lean also wanted the case he passes over, where M is F itself. Then "we have only to invoke the Principle of Continuity, as we have to do in I. 1" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 294), here System E, Intersection 4, in LeanEuclid's corrected form.

**"Of infinite length in the direction of E."** Euclid's DE leaves I.3 room to cut DF, FG and GH off it. A straight line in Lean has no length to cut from, so we make room (`Prop22.room`): I.2 sets out a straight line equal to the given one, and the straight line to be cut from is produced further than that, as in I.11; hence the Lean cites I.2. And we take K on a chosen side of DE, as I.23 will need.

**The modern way.** In the complex plane put F at 0 and G at b > 0, and let K = x + iy with x = (a² + b² − c²)/(2b) and y = √(a² − x²). Then |K| = a and |K − b| = c, and y is real and positive exactly when |a − b| < c < a + b: the three conditions of I.22, in one line.
