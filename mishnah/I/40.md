**An added converse.** The whole proposition stands in brackets, and Heath explains why: "Heiberg has proved by means of the papyrus-fragment mentioned in the last note that this proposition is an interpolation by some one who thought that there should be a proposition following I. 39 and related to it in the same way as I. 38 is related to I. 37, and I. 36 to I. 35" (vol. 1, p. 338). It is I.39's argument again, with the equal bases BC, CE for the one base, and I.38 for I.37. Our statement takes Euclid's figure as it stands, with the bases BC and CE end to end at C; Heath's enunciation, like I.38, would allow any two equal bases on one straight line.

**Where F falls.** Through A, I.31 draws the parallel to BE, and Euclid's F is where it meets CD. That it meets CD at all comes from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`): CD meets BE at C, so it meets the parallel to BE. I.38 makes ABC equal to FCE; our I.38 lets the bases share C and lets F be A, so neither needs a case of its own. Euclid names the given triangle CDE, then DCE, and `area_bac` says the two names give one area; Common Notion 1 makes DCE equal to FCE. So the certificate cites I.30 besides Euclid's I.31 and I.38, with Common Notions 1 and 5, and Postulate 5 comes in through I.29, by way of I.30 and I.38.

**"The greater to the less."** The same-side hypothesis puts F on the ray from C through D (`OnRay.of_sameSideOf`); I.39 says why it is needed. If CF is less than CD, FCE is part of DCE, and `area_lt_of_cut` makes it the less, by Common Notion 5; if CF is greater, DCE is the less, the case Proclus finds equally easy under I.39. So CF equals CD, F is D (`OnRay.eq_of_len_eq`), and the parallel through A is AD. Book Zero's `OnRay.eq_of_area_eq` makes this argument once, for I.39 too. Our figure draws the supposed case, with F between C and D and AF level; Heath draws AD level and AF sloping.

**The modern way.** Put BE on the x-axis. The bases have one length b, so a vertex at height y makes a triangle of area ½b|y|; equal areas give equal |y|, and the same side gives y the same sign. So A and D are at one height, and AD is parallel to BE.
