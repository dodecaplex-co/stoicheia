**An added converse.** The whole proposition stands in brackets. Heath explains: "Heiberg has proved by means of the papyrus-fragment mentioned in the last note that this proposition is an interpolation by some one who thought that there should be a proposition following I. 39 and related to it in the same way as I. 38 is related to I. 37, and I. 36 to I. 35" (vol. 1, p. 338). It repeats I.39's argument with equal bases BC, CE in place of one shared base, and I.38 in place of I.37.

**A parallel that must meet CD.** I.31 draws the parallel through A to BE. That a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other comes from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`), as Heath reports Proclus drawing it under I.31. This supplies F on CD. I.38 makes ABC equal to FCE, and Common Notion 1 makes DCE equal to FCE. Lean's `area_bac` changes the given name CDE to DCE without changing its area. The bases share C, and I.38 also allows F to be A, so neither requires a separate argument.

**The greater to the less.** The same-side hypothesis puts F on the ray from C through D, by `OnRay.of_sameSideOf`; the need for that hypothesis is discussed under I.39. If CF is less than CD, FCE is a part of DCE, and `area_lt_of_cut` applies Common Notion 5. If CF is greater, the roles reverse, covering the case Proclus adds under I.39. Equal lengths put F at D by `OnRay.eq_of_len_eq`. Thus the parallel through A passes through D. Our figure shows the supposed case with F between C and D: AF is level and AD slopes, where Heath draws AD level and AF sloping.

**What the certificate records.** The citations are I.30, I.31 and I.38, with Common Notions 1 and 5. Postulate 1 joins the named points; Book Zero supplies incidence, sides, order and the cutting of areas. I.31 brings in Postulates 2–4 through its cited constructions. Postulate 5 enters through I.30 and through I.38's dependence on I.36, I.35, I.34 and I.29. No further assumption is needed for the intersection or either order of F and D.

**The modern way.** Put the equal bases of length b on the horizontal axis. The areas are ½·b·|y|, so equal areas give equal absolute heights; the same side gives the same sign. The distinct vertices A and D therefore have equal heights, and AD is parallel to BE.
