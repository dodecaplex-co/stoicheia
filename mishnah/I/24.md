**One case, and the hardest.** Euclid argues from one figure, with F below EG. Robert Simson added a sentence to make it the only case, "Of the two sides DE, DF let DE be the side which is not greater than the other", and Heath leaves it out: "His habit and that of the great Greek geometers was, not to set out all possible cases, but to give as a rule one case, generally the most difficult, as here, and to leave the others to the reader to work out for himself" (vol. 1, p. 297).

**Where F falls.** Lean can't leave anything to the reader. F lies within the angle EDG, because the angle EDF is the less (`Prop24.within`); so DF, drawn on, crosses EG at a point X (`crossbar`), and `Prop24.cases` reads off the three places for F: on EG, within the triangle DEG, or beyond EG, as in Euclid's figure. The first two are Proclus' cases, which Heath finds "decidedly simpler than the case taken by Euclid as typical" (vol. 1, p. 297). On EG, EF is a part of EG (`len_lt_of_between`). Within the triangle, "by I. 21, DF, FE are together less than DG, GE. But DF is equal to DG; therefore EF is less than EG", taking the equal DF and DG away by a law of magnitudes Euclid never states (`Magnitude.lt_of_add_lt_add_left`). So the Lean cites I.21, which Euclid never mentions.

**Euclid's case.** His "therefore the angle DFG is greater than the angle EGF", and then "much greater", are each the whole greater than the part, and X makes each a part: the angle EGF is XGF, a part of DGF, since X lies between D and F; DFG is XFG, a part of EFG, since X lies between E and G (`angle_onRay_left`, `angle_lt_of_between`). Only when F is beyond EG does X lie between D and F, which is why the other cases need arguments of their own.

**What else Lean made us say.** Euclid's "let DG be made equal to either of the two straight lines AC, DF" has no bracket. Book Zero's `OnRay.exists_len_eq` lays it off with a circle about D (Post. 3), and the angle EDG is still the one I.23 built, its arms being the same rays (`angle_eq_of_onRay`). Euclid's figure puts G on F's side of DE; our I.23 lets us ask for that side, and `SameSideOf.of_onRay` keeps the point laid off there.

**The modern way.** With vectors b and c along the equal sides, the base is |b − c|, and |b − c|² = |b|² + |c|² − 2|b||c| cos θ, where θ is the angle between them. As θ grows from nothing to two right angles, cos θ falls and the base grows, so the greater angle has the greater base.
