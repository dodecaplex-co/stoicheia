**On the same side.** Heath writes: "This theorem is of course the *partial* converse of I. 37." He explains: "The additional qualification in this proposition that the triangles must be *on the same side* of the base is necessary because it is not, as in I. 37, involved in the other hypotheses" (vol. 1, p. 337). Our `SameSideOf` hypothesis says this explicitly. Equal triangles on opposite sides of BC need not have AD parallel to BC. The vertices A and D must also be distinct to name AD. Heath's note on the bracketed sentence reports Heiberg's proof, from a papyrus fragment, that it is an interpolation, and that the interpolator altered "And" into "For" in the next sentence.

**Where E is found.** I.31 draws a parallel through A; Euclid takes E where it meets BD. A straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other: `Prop30.meet` brings in I.30 for this step, as Heath reports Proclus drawing it under I.31. Postulate 1 names BC and BD and joins EC. The two unnamed points supplied by I.31 serve to name its straight line in Lean and need no letters in the figure. I.37 then makes ABC equal to EBC.

**The greater to the less.** Common Notion 1 makes DBC equal to EBC. Lean must place E before comparing these triangles: the parallel puts E on A's side of BC, hence on D's side, so `OnRay.of_sameSideOf` puts E on the ray from B through D. When E lies between B and D, `area_lt_of_cut` makes EBC less than DBC by Common Notion 5. When E lies beyond D, it makes DBC less than EBC, the further case Heath attributes to Proclus (vol. 1, p. 337). Equal distances put E at D, so the parallel through A passes through D: it is AD. Our figure draws the assumed parallel AE level, with E between B and D; Heath draws AD level.

**What the proof rests on.** The certificate records I.30, I.31 and I.37, with Common Notions 1 and 5. Postulate 5 enters through I.30 and its use of I.29; I.37's route through I.35 and I.34 also reaches I.29. The construction of a parallel in I.31 itself does not require Postulate 5.

**The modern way.** Put B at (0, 0) and C at (c, 0), with c positive: a triangle on BC with vertex (x, y) has area ½c|y|. Equal areas give equal absolute heights, and being on the same side gives the same sign, so A and D have equal heights and AD is parallel to BC.
