**Why "on the same side"?** Heath: "This theorem is of course the *partial* converse of I. 37." I.37's base and its conclusion, equal areas, are the hypotheses here, and its parallels the conclusion. One hypothesis is new: "The additional qualification in this proposition that the triangles must be *on the same side* of the base is necessary because it is not, as in I. 37, involved in the other hypotheses" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 337). Equal triangles on opposite sides of BC need not make AD parallel to BC, so our statement asks for the same side; it asks too that A and D be two points, for Euclid joins AD.

**Why no converses for parallelograms?** Proclus remarks, Heath reports, "that Euclid only converts I. 37 and I. 38 relative to triangles, and omits the converses of I. 35, 36 about parallelograms as unnecessary because it is easy to see that the method would be the same, and therefore the reader may properly be left to prove them for himself" (vol. 1, p. 337). Proclus counts I.40 among Euclid's; its page tells why Heath prints it in brackets.

**"I say that they are also in the same parallels."** J. L. Heiberg proved from a papyrus fragment, Heath reports, that this sentence was added by someone "who did not observe that the words 'And let AD be joined' are part of the *setting-out*" (vol. 1, p. 337). Taking them for the construction, he thought the thing to be proved should be stated first, and he altered "And" into "For". Our `--` lines keep both brackets as Heath prints them.

**"The greater to the less."** Lean must know where E is before calling either triangle the greater. The parallel through A puts E on A's side of BC, and so on D's: on the ray from B through D. If E is between B and D, as in Heath's figure, EBC is a part of DBC and the less (Common Notion 5); if beyond D, a case Proclus finds equally easy (Heath, vol. 1, p. 337), DBC is the less. So E falls neither short of D nor beyond it: E is D, and the parallel through A is AD. `OnRay.eq_of_area_eq` holds this argument, and serves I.40 too. Our figure draws AE level, as the proof supposes; Heath draws AD level.

**The modern way.** Put B at (0, 0) and C at (c, 0), with c positive: a triangle on BC with vertex (x, y) has area ½c|y|. Equal areas give equal |y|, and the same side of BC gives y the same sign, so A and D are at one height and AD is parallel to BC.
