**A partial converse.** Heath: "This theorem is of course the *partial* converse of I. 37." I.37's base and its conclusion, equal areas, are the hypotheses here, and its parallels the conclusion. One hypothesis is new: "The additional qualification in this proposition that the triangles must be *on the same side* of the base is necessary because it is not, as in I. 37, involved in the other hypotheses" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 337). Equal triangles on opposite sides of BC need not make AD parallel to BC, so our statement has a `SameSideOf` hypothesis; it asks too that A and D be two points, for Euclid joins AD.

**"I say that they are also in the same parallels."** Heath's note on this bracketed sentence reports Heiberg's proof, from a papyrus fragment, that it was added by someone who took "And let AD be joined" for part of the construction, and so thought the thing to be proved should be stated first; the same hand altered "And" into "For". Our `--` lines keep both brackets as Heath prints them.

**Where E falls.** Through A, I.31 draws the parallel to BC, and Euclid's E is where it meets BD. That it meets BD at all comes from I.30 (`Prop30.meet`): BD meets BC at B, so it meets the parallel to BC, as Heath reports Proclus drawing it under I.31. I.37 then makes ABC equal to EBC. So the certificate cites I.30 besides Euclid's I.31 and I.37, with Common Notions 1 and 5; Postulate 5 comes in through I.29, by way of I.30 and I.37.

**"The greater to the less."** Common Notion 1 makes DBC equal to EBC, and to call one the greater Lean must know where E is. The parallel puts E on A's side of BC, and so on D's: on the ray from B through D (`OnRay.of_sameSideOf`). If E is between B and D, as in Heath's figure, `area_lt_of_cut` makes EBC less than DBC, by Common Notion 5; if E is beyond D, a case Proclus finds equally easy (Heath, vol. 1, p. 337), it makes DBC the less. So BE equals BD, E is D, and the parallel through A is AD. Our figure draws AE level, as the proof supposes; Heath draws AD level.

**The modern way.** Put B at (0, 0) and C at (c, 0), with c positive: a triangle on BC with vertex (x, y) has area ½c|y|. Equal areas give equal |y|, and the same side of BC gives y the same sign, so A and D are at one height and AD is parallel to BC.
