**"Depends only upon I. 27."** Heath: "The construction, be it observed, depends only upon I. 27, and might therefore have come directly after that proposition. Why then did Euclid postpone it until after I. 29 and I. 30? Presumably because he considered it necessary, before giving the construction, to place beyond all doubt the fact that only one such parallel can be drawn" (vol. 1, p. 316). The certificate bears out its first sentence: our proof cites I.23 and I.27, just as Euclid's brackets do, and Postulate 5 is nowhere among the things it rests on.

**A parallel, or the parallel.** Proclus, Heath reports on the same page, takes the uniqueness of the parallel from I.30: two straight lines through A, both parallel to BC, would be parallel to each other, though they meet at A. Our statement asks for a parallel, not the only one, and so it needs no more than Euclid's two brackets. That there is no other is Playfair's axiom, which Heath discusses under I.29.

**Where D is taken.** "Let a point D be taken at random on BC": Lean takes it between B and C, as Euclid's figure does (`exists_point_between`, System E, Points 3). Then B and C lie on either side of AD (`OppositeSidesOf.of_between`), and our I.23, which builds its angle on the side of a point we name, is asked for B's side. So E lies on the other side of AD from C (`SameSideOf.trans_opposite`), and the angles EAD, ADC are alternate, as I.27 asks.

**What Lean made us say.** The statement asks that A be off BC: a straight line through a point of BC meets BC, and parallels do not meet. Then A and D are two points, AD can be joined, and ADC is a triangle, as I.23 wants. I.23 marks one arm of its angle with some point of the ray from A through D, not necessarily D; an angle depends only on its rays (`angle_onRay_left`), so the angle is DAE all the same. Producing EA to F uses Postulate 2 (`produce`), and drawing BC uses Postulate 1; Euclid brackets neither here, and I.23 and I.27 bring both in as well.

**The modern way.** The parallel through A is the straight line of points A + t(C − B), for every real number t. Euclid's E is one of these with t negative, on B's side of AD, and F one with t positive.
