**Two angles brought to C.** We draw CE parallel to AB by I.31. I.29 then brings the angle at A to ACE and the angle at B to ECD. Together they fill the exterior angle ACD. Adding the angle ACB and applying I.13 gives the triangle's three angles equal to two right angles. These are exactly Euclid's cited propositions. His use of Postulate 5 lies in I.29, which supplies the two angle equalities.

**Choosing the half of the parallel.** Euclid takes E above BC, within the exterior angle. I.31 gives a straight line through C with a point on either side of C; `Prop32.choose_end` selects the end on A's side of BC. The two ends lie on opposite sides of BC, and a straight line has just two sides. Then `AreParallel.oppositeSidesOf` puts B and E on opposite sides of AC: otherwise the parallel would enter the triangle and cross AB. Since D is also opposite B across AC, E and D lie on the same side. This is what `angle_add_of_within` needs before it can add ACE and ECD. Book Zero's `AreParallel.figure_alternate` and `AreParallel.figure_interior` supply the auxiliary points needed to apply I.29; they extend the straight lines without changing the angles we use.

**Heath on turning a straight line.** Thibaut tried to obtain the angle sum by turning a straight line successively about the triangle's corners. Heath observes that it is "necessary to assume as an axiom that the two motions are independent" (vol. 1, p. 321): translation enters because the centre of rotation changes. He also reports Gauss's objection to Schumacher's related argument, which assumed an angle relation needing proof. The warning concerns the assumptions behind moving angles from one place to another. Here those equalities have their stated warrant in I.29.

**The modern way.** Traverse a triangle counterclockwise in the Euclidean plane, recording its three side directions as unit vectors. Their successive positive turns total 2π, and each turn is π minus the interior angle at that corner, so the three interior angles total π. This uses the Euclidean plane's common system of directions, the structure behind Heath's caution about translating a turning straight line.
