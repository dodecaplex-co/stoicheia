**Two angles carried to C.** Euclid draws CE through C parallel to AB (I.31), and I.29 carries the angle at A across to ACE, its alternate angle, and the angle at B up to ECD, its exterior angle. Together they fill the exterior angle ACD; add the angle ACB to each, and I.13 makes the three angles of the triangle two right angles. The Lean cites I.31, I.29 and I.13, as Euclid's brackets do, and Postulate 5 comes in through I.29 alone: without it, I.17 could say only that any two of the angles fall short of two right angles.

**Which end of the parallel.** Euclid's figure puts E above BC, within the angle ACD. Our I.31 hands back a straight line through C with a point on either side of C, and `Prop32.choose_end` takes the one on A's side of BC: the two lie on opposite sides of BC, and a straight line has just two sides. Then `AreParallel.oppositeSidesOf` puts E across AC from B, for otherwise the parallel would run into the triangle and cross AB. D is across AC from B as well, so E and D are on one side of AC, and that, with E on A's side of CD, is what `angle_add_of_within` needs to make ACD the sum of ACE and ECD. Each citation of I.29 is one of the corollaries that follow it, `Prop29.alternate` and `Prop29.exterior`, for which Book Zero sets out I.29's figure.

**Turning a straight line.** Thibaut turned a straight line about each corner of the triangle in turn: it ends on the straight line it began on, so it has turned through four right angles, the three exterior angles together. Heath's objection is that it turns about different points, so that "it is necessary to assume as an axiom that the two motions are independent" (vol. 1, p. 321). Schumacher tried to make it a turning about one point, and Gauss replied that he had assumed a proposition which is, "in essence, the very proposition to be proved" (Heath, vol. 1, p. 321). Euclid moves his angles with I.29, and pays for it with Postulate 5.

**The modern way.** Put A, B and C anticlockwise in the complex plane. The angles at A, B and C are the arguments of (C − A)/(B − A), (A − B)/(C − B) and (B − C)/(A − C), each between 0 and π, and the product of these three numbers is −1. So the three angles add up to an odd multiple of π, and as they lie between 0 and 3π, to π: two right angles.
