**Where F falls.** "But the angle ECD is greater than the angle ECF," says Euclid, citing Common Notion 5, the whole greater than the part. That is so only if CF falls within the angle ECD, and nothing but the figure says it does. Heath: "As is well known, this proposition is not universally true under the Riemann hypothesis of a space endless in extent but not infinite in size. On this hypothesis a straight line is a 'closed series' and returns on itself" (vol. 1, p. 280). Heath's "Riemann hypothesis" is the sphere-like geometry now called elliptic, not the famous conjecture about prime numbers. There, if BE is long enough, F comes right round onto CD, or past it.

**What Lean made us say.** So `Prop16.within` proves where F falls. F is on D's side of AC, because AC separates B from F (E lies between them) and B from D (C lies between them), and a straight line has two sides and no more. F is on E's side of BC, because BF sets out from B, on BC, and passes through E, off it, on its way to F. These are rules of order and sides (System E, Pasch 2, Pasch 3 and Same side 5) that a space whose straight lines return on themselves does without. Then the angle ECD is made up of ECF and FCD (`angle_add_of_within`), FCD is something (`Triangle.angle_pos`), and Common Notion 5 applies.

**Far enough to cut off.** I.3 cuts off from the greater of two straight lines, and Postulate 2 produces BE without saying how far. Book Zero's `produce_longer` makes sure it is far enough: it describes the circle with centre E and distance EB (Post. 3), finds where BE produced cuts it, and goes a little further (Post. 2). What it reaches is greater than the radius, which is equal to EB (Def. 15), so I.3 can cut EF off, and `between_cut` puts E between B and F. So Postulate 3 and Definition 15 are used, though Euclid cites neither here (I.3, I.10 and I.15 bring both in as well).

**The modern way.** Put C at 0, B at −1 and A at a, above them, so that D lies along the positive real axis. The exterior angle ACD is arg a, the angles at B and A are arg(a + 1) and arg(a/(a + 1)), all between 0 and π, and since a = (a + 1) · a/(a + 1), the first is the sum of the other two. That is I.32, which needs the parallel postulate; I.16 is the part of it that holds without.
