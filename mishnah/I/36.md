**One figure between two others.** Euclid joins BE and CH to make EBCH, a parallelogram sharing the base BC with ABCD and the base EH with EFGH. I.35 makes each given parallelogram equal to this third one, and Common Notion 1 finishes the proof. The bracket [I. 34] stands after the assertion that EBCH is a parallelogram. In Lean that assertion follows from its two pairs of parallel sides, the definition of `Parallelogram`; I.34 supplies the earlier equality FG = EH, which lets I.33 apply.

**Which way the letters run.** I.33 joins equal and parallel straight lines at the ends which are in the same directions: here C and H must lie on one side of BE, which Lean writes with `SameSideOf`. Book Zero's `AreParallel.sameSideOf_or` gives this arrangement or the one obtained by reading EFGH as HGFE. We write Euclid's argument once and use it with either lettering. Either use of I.35 may fall in any of its cases: on the base EH, for instance, BC and FG may overlap or share an end. So I.36 needs Euclid's case and the two Heath reports Proclus adding under I.35, and `Prop35` proves all three. `quadArea_reverse` and `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` return the areas to their original names.

**The cases behind the picture.** Heath lists among Heron's contributions: "Distinctions of a number of particular cases of Euclid's propositions according as the figure is drawn in one way or in another. Of this class are the different cases of I. 35, 36" (vol. 1, p. 22). Heath gives I.36 no note of its own. As he reports under I.37, the end of Proclus' note on I.36 is lost in a lacuna (p. 332); under I.39 he reports Proclus' remark that Euclid leaves the converses of I.35 and I.36 to the reader (p. 337).

**What the proof rests on.** The certificate cites I.33, I.34 and I.35, exactly the propositions in Heath's brackets, and Common Notion 1. Postulate 1 enters through Book Zero, which draws the straight lines needed to settle which way EH runs and to compare the two diameters, and through each cited proposition. The direct tacit rules concern incidence, betweenness, sides, length symmetry, triangle areas and addition of magnitudes. Postulate 5 enters through I.29 in each of the three cited propositions.

**The modern way.** In vector notation the area of ABCD is |BC × BA| = |BC|h, where h is the perpendicular distance between AH and BG. The same parallels give EFGH the same height, and BC = FG makes the products equal.
