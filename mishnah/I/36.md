**One figure between two others.** Euclid joins BE and CH to make EBCH, a parallelogram sharing the base BC with ABCD and the base EH with EFGH. I.35 makes each given figure equal to this common comparison, and Common Notion 1 finishes the proof. The bracket [I. 34] stands after the assertion that EBCH is a parallelogram. In Lean that assertion follows from its two pairs of parallel sides, the definition of `Parallelogram`; I.34 supplies the earlier equality FG = EH, which lets I.33 apply.

**Which way the letters run.** I.33 needs the joining straight lines to run in the same directions: C and H must lie on one side of BE, expressed by `SameSideOf`. Book Zero's `AreParallel.sameSideOf_or` gives this arrangement or the one obtained by reading EFGH as HGFE. We write Euclid's argument once and use it with either lettering. The second application of I.35 is on EH, so its other sides may overlap or share an endpoint, among the arrangements discussed in Heath's notes under I.35. It needs that proposition in all its cases. `quadArea_reverse` and `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` return the areas to their original names.

**The cases behind the picture.** Heath lists among Heron's contributions: "Distinctions of a number of particular cases of Euclid's propositions according as the figure is drawn in one way or in another. Of this class are the different cases of I. 35, 36" (vol. 1, p. 22). I.36 has no separate note in Heath; the discussion above it on p. 331 belongs to I.35. As Heath reports under I.37, a lacuna joins the surviving notes of Proclus on I.36 and I.37 (p. 332), while under I.39 Proclus observes that Euclid leaves readers the corresponding converses for parallelograms (p. 337).

**What the proof rests on.** The certificate cites I.33, I.34 and I.35, exactly the propositions in Heath's brackets, and Common Notion 1. Book Zero uses Postulate 1 when drawing the straight lines needed to settle orientation and to compare the two diameters. Its remaining rules concern incidence, betweenness, sides, length symmetry, triangle areas and addition of magnitudes. Postulate 5 enters through I.29 in each of the three cited propositions.

**The modern way.** In vector notation the area of ABCD is |BC × BA| = |BC|h, where h is the perpendicular distance between AH and BG. The same parallels give EFGH the same height, and BC = FG makes the products equal.
