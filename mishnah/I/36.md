**A third parallelogram.** Euclid joins BE and CH, and the figure EBCH that they close is a parallelogram with a base in common with each of the given ones: BC with ABCD, and EH with EFGH. I.35 makes it equal to each, and Common Notion 1 makes the two equal to one another. So equal bases do as well as one base, once a third parallelogram bridges them.

**Where does I.34 come in?** Euclid cites I.34 for "Therefore EBCH is a parallelogram", but that asks for no proposition: both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, and that is our `Parallelogram`. The step that does need I.34 stands a few lines up, with no citation: "while FG is equal to EH", the opposite sides of EFGH. The Lean cites it there, and that equality is what lets I.33 make EB and HC equal and parallel.

**Which way do the letters run?** I.33 joins equal parallels at the ends that are in the same directions, so C and H must lie on one side of BE (`SameSideOf`). In Euclid's figure they do. In general `AreParallel.sameSideOf_or` says that they do, or else that E and C lie on one side of HB, which is the same thing with EFGH read as HGFE. So we run Euclid's argument with either lettering, and turn the areas back to their given names at the end. Lean never draws BE or CH: their ends are all it needs.

**"The different cases of I. 35, 36."** Each use of I.35 may fall in any of its cases: on the base BC, the sides AD and EH may overlap or meet at an end, and on the base EH, so may BC and FG. So I.36 needs the cases that Proclus adds under I.35 as well as Euclid's, and our I.35 proves them all. Heath gives I.36 no note of its own, but among Heron's comments he finds "Distinctions of a number of particular *cases* of Euclid's propositions according as the figure is drawn in one way or in another. Of this class are the different cases of I. 35, 36" (vol. 1, p. 22).

**The modern way.** With the cross product of I.35, the area of ABCD is |(C − B) × (A − B)|, the length of BC times the distance between the parallels AH and BG. EFGH stands between the same parallels on a base FG equal to BC, and so has the same area.
