**A third parallelogram.** Euclid joins BE and CH, and EBCH is a parallelogram on the base BC with ABCD and on the base EH with EFGH. I.35 makes each given parallelogram equal to it, and Common Notion 1 makes them equal to each other. Euclid's bracket [I. 34] stands after "Therefore EBCH is a parallelogram", which in Lean is just the definition of `Parallelogram`, both pairs of opposite sides parallel. The Lean cites I.34 earlier, for FG equal to EH: that is what lets I.33 make EB and HC equal and parallel.

**Which way the letters run.** I.33 joins equal and parallel straight lines at the ends that are in the same directions, so C and H must lie on one side of BE (`SameSideOf`). Book Zero's `AreParallel.sameSideOf_or` says that they do, or else that E and C lie on one side of HB, which is the same thing with EFGH read as HGFE. So the Lean gives Euclid's argument once and uses it with either lettering. Each use of I.35 may fall in any of its cases: on the base EH, BC and FG may overlap or meet at an end. I.36 needs, then, the cases Heath reports Proclus adding under I.35 as well as Euclid's, and our `Prop35` proves them all. `quadArea_reverse` and `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` turn the areas back to their given names.

**The cases behind the picture.** Heath gives I.36 no note of its own. Among Heron's comments, though, he finds "Distinctions of a number of particular cases of Euclid's propositions according as the figure is drawn in one way or in another. Of this class are the different cases of I. 35, 36" (vol. 1, p. 22). As Heath reports under I.37, the end of Proclus' note on I.36 is lost in a lacuna (p. 332), and under I.39 he reports Proclus' remark that Euclid leaves the converses of I.35 and I.36 to the reader (p. 337).

**What it rests on.** The Lean cites just Euclid's three brackets, with Common Notion 1. It draws neither BE nor CH, needing only their ends: Postulate 1 comes in through Book Zero, where `AreParallel.sameSideOf_or` settles which way EH runs and `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` measures a parallelogram by either diameter, and through all three citations. Each of them cites I.29, and Postulate 5 comes in through each.

**The modern way.** The area of ABCD is |(C − B) × (A − B)|, the length of BC times the distance between the parallels AH and BG. EFGH stands between the same parallels on a base FG equal to BC, and so has the same area.
