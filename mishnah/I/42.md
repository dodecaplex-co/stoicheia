**Doubles of the same thing.** I.38 makes ABE equal in area to AEC, and I.41 makes FECG double of AEC. Thus ABC and FECG are both AEC added to itself; `CommonNotion1` identifies them. Heath reports Proclus excluding the extra axiom (b), that doubles of the same thing are equal, because it follows from Common Notion 1 (vol. 1, p. 223). Discussing the corresponding words in I.47, Heath writes: "If they were interpolated, we should have expected to find the same interpolation in I. 42, where the axiom is *tacitly* assumed" (vol. 1, p. 224). Proclus' commentary on I.42 is lost entirely, as Heath notes under I.43 (vol. 1, p. 341).

**Where F and G are.** I.23 constructs the angle at E on A's side of EC. F is then where its second arm meets the parallel through A; G is where the parallel through C meets that same straight line. Each intersection comes from `Prop30.meet`, that a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other: Proclus draws it from I.30 under I.31, as Heath reports. Both F and the point marking the constructed arm lie on A's side of BC. `OnRay.of_sameSideOf` puts F on that ray, preserving the angle. The construction allows A to be F or G.

**What Lean made us say.** Bisecting BC cites I.10, although Euclid gives no bracket. Joining AE requires no further object: the triangles are named by their corners, and `area_cut` says their areas make up ABC. I.38 is used with A as the vertex of both triangles. The conclusion records that E lies between B and C (`Between`), and F lies on A's side of BC (`SameSideOf`); I.44 and I.45 need these placements. The certificate's citations are I.10, I.23, I.30, I.31, I.38 and I.41, with Common Notion 1. Postulate 1 draws the named straight lines and also enters through the six cited propositions; Book Zero supplies the incidence, side, ray and area facts. Postulate 5 enters through I.30, and also through the area theorems I.38 and I.41.

**The modern way.** Put E at the midpoint of BC, let h be A's height above BC, and let u be the unit vector making the given angle θ with C − E on A's side. Then F = E + (h/sin θ)u and G = C + (F − E). The parallelogram has area |EC|h = ½|BC|h, the area of ABC.
