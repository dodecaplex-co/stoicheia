**Doubles of the same thing.** I.38 makes ABE equal to AEC, so ABC is double of AEC, and I.41 makes FECG double of AEC too: both are AEC added to itself, and Common Notion 1 makes them equal (`CommonNotion1`). Some manuscripts give a Common Notion of its own, that things double of the same thing are equal; Proclus excludes it, Heath reports, because it follows from Common Notion 1 (vol. 1, p. 223). Heath, with Tannery, sees no reason to strike out the like words in I.47: "If they were interpolated, we should have expected to find the same interpolation in I. 42, where the axiom is *tacitly* assumed" (vol. 1, p. 224). Proclus' note on I.42 is lost, as Heath remarks under I.43.

**Where F and G are.** I.23 makes the angle at E on A's side of EC; F is where the straight line along its arm meets the parallel through A, and G is where the parallel through C meets that straight line too. Each meeting point comes from `Prop30.meet` (for G, within `Prop31.parallelogram`): a straight line meeting one of two parallels meets the other, which Heath reports Proclus drawing from I.30 under I.31. F lies on A's side of BC, so `OnRay.of_sameSideOf` puts it on the arm itself, and CEF is the angle I.23 made. A may fall at F or at G: I.41 asks only that A lie on the straight line FG.

**What Lean made us say.** Bisecting BC is I.10, though Euclid gives it no bracket. Joining AE asks nothing of Lean: a triangle is named by its corners, and `area_cut` says that ABE and AEC make up ABC. Our I.38 takes A as the vertex of both triangles. The statement also says where the figure lies, as Euclid draws it: E between B and C, and F on A's side of BC. The certificate cites I.10, I.23, I.30, I.31, I.38 and I.41, and Postulate 5 comes in through I.30, I.38 and I.41.

**The modern way.** Put E at the midpoint of BC, let h be A's height above BC, and let u be the unit vector making the given angle θ with C − E on A's side. Then F = E + (h/sin θ)u and G = C + (F − E). The parallelogram has area |EC|·h = ½|BC|·h, the area of ABC.
