**Two roads to alternate angles.** The enunciation offers two hypotheses, and Euclid brings each to the alternate angles AGH, GHD. If the exterior angle EGB is equal to GHD, I.15 puts its vertical angle AGH in its place. If BGH and GHD make two right angles, so do AGH and BGH, by I.13, and when BGH is taken from each, AGH is left equal to GHD. Either way I.27 finishes. The Lean takes the two cases in turn and cites I.15, I.13 and I.27, just as Euclid's brackets do; `CommonNotion1` joins the equalities, and `CommonNotion3` takes the angle BGH away.

**"And they are alternate."** Euclid says so twice and moves on, for his figure shows it. Our I.27 asks for it outright: A and D on opposite sides of GH. The statement puts B and D on one side of EF, and Book Zero's `SameSideOf.inner` says that EF is the straight line GH, naming it with Postulate 1. Since G lies between A and B, A is across GH from B, and so from D (`SameSideOf.oppositeSidesOf_of_between`). The same condition keeps B off GH, so that AB and EH really cross at G, as I.15 wants, and H off AB, so that GH stands on AB, as I.13 wants.

**Six criteria.** Heath reports Proclus' observation that "Euclid could have stated six criteria as well as three" (vol. 1, p. 310), from the other pairs of angles in the figure: two on the same side of the straight line falling on the others, or on different sides, interior or exterior, equal or together equal to two right angles. Each comes back to alternate angles, as Euclid's two do. The three he chose are just the three that I.29 will turn round.

**What it rests on.** Postulate 1, used inside `SameSideOf.inner`, comes in with I.13, I.15 and I.27 as well, and through those three the certificate lists Postulates 2 to 4 and Definition 15. Postulate 5 is not among them: it waits for I.29.

**The modern way.** Put G at (0, 0) and H at (1, 0), with B and D above the axis. Either hypothesis says that GB and HD make the same angle with the direction from H to G, so they point the same way, and AB and CD, which cross the axis at different points, never meet.
