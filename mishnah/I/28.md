**Two roads to alternate angles.** Heath counts the tests so far: "One criterion of parallelism, the equality of alternate angles, is given in I. 27; here we have two more" (vol. 1, p. 310). Euclid brings each to the alternate angles AGH, GHD. If the exterior angle EGB is equal to GHD, I.15 puts its vertical angle AGH in its place. If BGH and GHD make two right angles, so do AGH and BGH, by I.13, and when BGH is taken from each, AGH is left equal to GHD. Either way I.27 finishes.

**"And they are alternate."** Euclid says so twice and moves on, for his figure shows it. Our I.27 asks for it outright, A and D on opposite sides of GH, and we find it as in I.27: the statement puts B and D on one side of EF, which is the straight line GH, and A, with G between it and B, lies across GH from both.

**Not because they are right.** Aristotle knew this proposition and used it to show what a general proof is. Two straight lines at right angles to a third do not meet, but a proof of that should not rest on the angles being right: it follows, in Heath's translation, "by virtue of their being equal (to two right angles) in any way whatever" (vol. 1, p. 309). That is Euclid's second hypothesis; the passage, Heath judges, "certainly refers to I. 28" (vol. 1, p. 309).

**Six criteria.** Heath reports Proclus' observation that "Euclid could have stated six criteria as well as three" (vol. 1, p. 310): pairs of angles on one side of EF or on opposite sides, interior or exterior, equal or together two right angles. The three left out, Heath says, "are of course equivalent but are not quite so easily expressed" (vol. 1, p. 311), and the three Euclid chose are the ones I.29 will turn round. One Aigeias (or Aineias) of Hierapolis, known, it seems, only from Proclus' note here, made I.27 and I.28 one proposition in his abridgment of the *Elements* (Heath, vol. 1, p. 311).

**The modern way.** Put G at (0, 0) and H at (1, 0), with B and D above the axis. Either hypothesis says that GB and HD make the same angle with the direction from H to G, so they point the same way, and AB and CD, which cross the axis at different points, never meet.
