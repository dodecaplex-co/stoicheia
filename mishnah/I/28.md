**Two ways to reach alternate angles.** We have two hypotheses, and Euclid gives each its own short argument. When the exterior angle EGB equals GHD, I.15 replaces EGB by its vertical angle AGH. When BGH and GHD together equal two right angles, I.13 supplies another such sum, AGH and BGH. Subtracting BGH leaves AGH equal to GHD. Each argument now reaches I.27: equal alternate angles make the straight lines parallel. Lean follows these branches separately and cites exactly I.15, I.13 and I.27, the propositions in Euclid's brackets.

**What "alternate" requires.** Equal magnitudes do not tell us where their angles lie. Euclid reads the position from his figure: B and D lie on one side of EF, while A lies across it from B. Book Zero's `SameSideOf.inner` lets us call that straight line GH instead. Then `SameSideOf.oppositeSidesOf_of_between` puts A and D on opposite sides of GH, as I.27 requires. Lean also checks that B is off GH, so the straight lines used in I.15 really cross, and H is off AB when I.13 is applied. No new helper is needed. These are consequences of the given order and side conditions, not additional assumptions.

**Heath's six criteria.** Heath reports Proclus' observation that "Euclid could have stated six criteria as well as three" (vol. 1, p. 310). We can sort the angle pairs by whether they lie on the same side or on different sides of the straight line falling on the other two, and by whether they are interior or exterior. Euclid's chosen conditions suffice because his equalities and subtractions bring them back to alternate angles. Heath's note concerns this choice of criteria; it does not identify a missing premise in the proof.

**What the proof uses.** `CommonNotion1` equates the two sums, and `CommonNotion3` cancels their common angle. Book Zero uses Postulate 1 to name the straight line through the given points. I.13, I.15 and I.27 bring it in too; through them the certificate also records Postulates 2–4 and Definition 15. Postulate 5 is absent.

**The modern way.** Place G at (0, 0), H at (1, 0), and B and D above the horizontal axis. Either hypothesis says that the rays GB and HD make the same angle with that axis, so their direction vectors are positive multiples of one another and AB and CD are parallel.
