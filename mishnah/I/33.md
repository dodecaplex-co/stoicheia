**"The connecting link."** I.33 is, as Heath reports Proclus saying, "the connecting link between the exposition of the theory of parallels and the investigation of parallelograms" (vol. 1, p. 323). The brackets in its enunciation are Heath's: "I have for clearness' sake inserted the words in brackets though they are not in the original Greek" (vol. 1, p. 323). Our statement gives them their force with `SameSideOf`: B and D lie on one side of AC. Then Book Zero's `AreParallel.oppositeSidesOf` puts A and D on opposite sides of BC, for were D on A's side, CD would run within the angle ACB and cross AB, and parallels do not meet. So ABC, BCD are alternate angles, as `Prop29.alternate` asks.

**The order of the letters.** Heath observes that "Euclid is not always careful to write in corresponding order the letters denoting corresponding points in congruent figures" (vol. 1, p. 323), and writes DC, CB and DCB where the Greek has BC, CD and BCD. Lean is stricter still: `Prop4` pairs the corners by their places, the corner of the given angle first, so the triangles go in as BAC and CDB, with BC common, read once each way. Of the remaining angles that I.4 yields, BCA, read the other way round, is the angle ACB that Euclid needs next.

**AC and DB produced.** I.27 wants the falling straight line to meet each of the others between two of its points, so the Lean produces AC beyond C and DB beyond B (Post. 2, `produce`). Neither Heath's figure nor ours shows these ends, and the Lean calls them C₁ and B₁. What I.27 then says of AC₁ and B₁D it says of AC and BD (`AreParallel.of_parallel`), for parallels are whole straight lines, whichever two of their points name them.

**What it rests on.** The certificate cites I.29, I.4 and I.27, Euclid's three brackets. The proof uses Postulate 2 to produce AC and DB, and Postulate 1 to name the straight lines through them; all three citations bring in Postulate 1 as well, and I.27 and I.29 Postulate 2. The rest of the certificate's list, Postulates 3 and 4, Definition 15 and `superposition` among it, comes in with the cited propositions, and Postulate 5 with I.29 alone.

**The modern way.** As vectors, AB and CD, equal, parallel and running the same way, give B − A = D − C. Then C − A = D − B, so AC and BD are equal and parallel too; and A + D = B + C, so the diameters AD and BC of the parallelogram ABDC bisect one another.
