**In the same directions.** Heath explains the enunciation: "I have for clearness' sake inserted the words in brackets though they are not in the original Greek" (vol. 1, p. 323). The condition `SameSideOf`, here placing B and D on one side of AC, gives those words their force. Book Zero's `AreParallel.oppositeSidesOf` then puts A and D on opposite sides of BC. Otherwise CD would enter the angle ACB and cross AB, contradicting the given parallelism. This is what makes ABC and BCD alternate angles, ready for `Prop29.alternate`.

**The order of the letters.** Heath writes: "Euclid is not always careful to write in corresponding order the letters denoting corresponding points in congruent figures" (vol. 1, p. 323). He changes the Greek's BC, CD and BCD to DC, CB and DCB. Lean's `Prop4` pairs corners by position and takes the corner of the given angle first, so we enter the triangles as BAC and CDB. The common straight line is BC read in opposite directions. I.4 gives the equal bases, equal triangles and equal remaining angles; reversing the name BCA gives the angle ACB that Euclid next uses.

**The whole straight lines.** I.27 asks for the crossing straight line to fall between two points on each of the others. So we produce AC beyond C and DB beyond B (Post. 2, `produce`). Heath leaves these ends without letters; Lean gives them subscript names, and the figure leaves them unlettered. Postulate 1 names the straight lines through A, C and B, D. After I.27, `AreParallel.of_parallel` returns the parallelism to AC and BD: changing the points that name a straight line does not change the straight line.

**What the proof rests on.** The certificate cites I.29, I.4 and I.27, exactly Euclid's brackets. It records Postulates 1 and 2 directly, together with rules for incidence, order, sides, and the symmetry of lengths, angles and areas. Postulate 1 also enters through all three cited propositions, and Postulate 2 through I.27 and I.29. Postulates 3 and 4 and Definition 15 enter through I.27 and I.29, and superposition through all three. Postulate 5 enters through I.29.

**The modern way.** Write the points as vectors: equal parallel straight lines running in the same direction give B − A = D − C. Rearranging gives C − A = D − B, so AC and BD are equal and parallel. Also A + D = B + C, so the diameters AD and BC have the same midpoint.
