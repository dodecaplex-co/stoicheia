**A right angle to compare with.** Euclid draws AD at right angles to AC, makes it equal to BA, and joins DC. The right angle is I.11's, though no bracket names it, and Book Zero's `OnRay.exists_len_eq` makes AD equal to BA with the circle of centre A and distance AB (Post. 3). We put D on the other side of AC from B, as Heath draws it, though our I.8 does not ask which side: it compares any two triangles whose three sides are equal.

**The squares Euclid never draws.** Euclid speaks of the squares on DA, AC and DC without describing them; Lean describes all three by I.46, outward from the triangle DAC, as I.47 asks, beside the three our statement gives. `Prop46.square_eq`, which brings in I.4 and I.28, makes the square on DA equal to the given one on AB, and the new square on AC equal to the given one. Common Notion 2 adds them, I.47 gives the square on DC, and the hypothesis makes it equal to the square on BC.

**"So that the side DC is also equal to BC."** Euclid passes from equal squares to equal sides in a clause. Lean needs `Prop46.side_eq`, the converse Heath reports Proclus proving under I.46, and it brings in I.3, I.4, I.28, I.34 and I.46. To use it, `Prop28.square` (I.28) makes both squares parallelograms, and `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` reads each from C. The certificate records Postulates 1, 2 and 3 and Definition 15 directly, through the constructions and Book Zero; Common Notion 5 comes in through `Prop46.side_eq`, and Postulate 5 through I.34, I.46 and I.47.

**The indirect argument within.** Heath reports Proclus' other proof, the one an-Nairīzī gives to Heron: draw the perpendicular on B's side of AC instead, and I.7 will not let it fall anywhere but on AB. De Morgan sees "an appearance of avoiding indirect demonstration by drawing the triangles on different sides of the base and appealing to I. 8, because drawing them on the same side would make the appeal to I. 7 (on which, however, I. 8 is founded)" (vol. 1, p. 369). Our I.8 cites I.7 too, so his parenthesis holds in the certificate.

**The modern way.** Put u = B − A and v = C − A. Since |u − v|² = |u|² + |v|² − 2u·v, the hypothesis says that u·v = 0, so BA and AC are at right angles. Euclid instead builds a right-angled triangle and compares the given triangle with it by I.8.
