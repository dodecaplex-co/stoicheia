**A right angle to compare.** Euclid draws AD perpendicular to AC, makes AD equal to BA, and joins DC. I.11 supplies the perpendicular, though there is no bracket naming it. Book Zero's `OnRay.exists_len_eq` sets the length by a circle with centre A and distance AB, using Postulate 3 and a line-circle intersection. Lean chooses D on the other side of AC from B, as Heath draws it. Our I.8 compares any two triangles, so the side follows Euclid's figure and I.8 does not ask for it.

**The squares Euclid leaves undrawn.** The statement gives the squares with corners of its own, with each pair of far corners on one side of its straight line. Lean describes squares on DA, AC and DC by I.46, outward from the triangle DAC, as I.47 requires. `Prop46.square_eq` says that squares on equal straight lines have equal areas; it brings in I.4 and I.28. It also makes the new square on AC equal to the given one. Common Notion 2 adds the equal areas, and I.47 supplies the square on DC. The hypothesis then makes that square equal to the square on BC.

**From equal squares to equal sides.** `Prop28.square` makes the squares parallelograms by I.28, and `Parallelogram.quadArea_rotate` lets Lean read their areas from C. Then `Prop46.side_eq` gives DC equal to BC, bringing in I.3, I.4, I.28, I.34 and I.46. `Prop46.square_eq` and `Prop46.side_eq` are the two theorems Heath reports Proclus proving under I.46. I.8 now makes the angle BAC equal to the right angle DAC. The certificate records Postulates 1, 2 and 3 and Definition 15 directly through the constructions and Book Zero; Common Notion 5 enters through `Prop46.side_eq`. Postulate 5 comes through I.46 and I.47.

**The indirect argument within.** Heath reports Proclus' alternative: draw the perpendicular on B's side of AC, where I.7 forces it to coincide with AB. De Morgan sees "an appearance of avoiding indirect demonstration by drawing the triangles on different sides of the base and appealing to I. 8, because drawing them on the same side would make the appeal to I. 7 (on which, however, I. 8 is founded)" (vol. 1, p. 369). Our I.8 cites I.7 too, so his parenthesis holds in the certificate.

**The modern way.** Put u = B − A and v = C − A. The identity |u − v|² = |u|² + |v|² − 2u·v turns the hypothesis into u·v = 0, so BA and AC are perpendicular. Euclid instead builds a right-angled triangle and compares its three sides with the given triangle by I.8.
